# Percolation-Based Mapping of Urban-Rural Gradients and Their Spatiotemporal Dynamics in China（2000-2020）

**Author Information**  
Yuxia Wang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> East China Normal University  



## Abstract

Rapid urbanization has blurred conventional urban–rural boundaries, calling for gradient-based approaches that treat space as a continuous spectrum rather than a binary divide. However, existing delineation methods remain largely boundary-oriented and rely on empirical thresholds, limiting consistent national-scale comparison. This study develops a percolation-based framework to identify and track multi-level urban–rural gradients across China from 2000 to 2020. We integrate nighttime lights and gridded population data to construct a composite indicator of human activity intensity, apply land-use masks to exclude spurious signals, and employ percolation theory to detect endogenous connectivity thresholds and critical transitions. The approach reveals nested gradient structures—from dispersed rural settlements and peri-urban zones to urban clusters and core agglomerations—within a unified mechanistic logic. By tracing their temporal evolution, the framework provides a consistent baseline for analyzing socio-ecological processes along the urban–rural continuum and offers a transferable methodology for comparative sustainability research.
