# Climate Modulates Fragmentation-Driven Resilience Loss in Urban–Rural Ecotones

**Author Information**  
Peiqing Jing<sup>1</sup>, Qimin Cheng<sup>2</sup>, Zihan Lu<sup>3</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Wuhan university  

<sup>2</sup> Huazhong University of Science and Technology  

<sup>3</sup> Wuhan University  



## Abstract

Global urbanization is reshaping the spatial configuration of terrestrial ecosystems. Urban–rural ecotones, as the active frontiers of urban expansion, are subject to intense landscape fragmentation. However, how vegetation resilience in these critical transitional zones responds to the coupled stresses of fragmentation and climate variability remains poorly understood. This study examines four major Chinese urban agglomerations spanning a subtropical-to-temperate climate gradient: Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei (BTH), the Yangtze River Delta (YRD), the Pearl River Delta (PRD), and Chengdu–Chongqing (CY). Using multi-source remote sensing data from 2000 to 2025, we integrated multiple land-use products to map urban built-up areas and characterize their spatiotemporal dynamics, and delineated urban–rural ecotones via a moving-window density approach. Vegetation resilience was then quantified using first-order autoregressive coefficients and analyzed alongside landscape pattern indices to assess its spatiotemporal trajectories across urban–rural ecotones. Results reveal that the weakening effect of landscape fragmentation on vegetation resilience is strongly climate-dependent: in the humid southern agglomerations (PRD, YRD, and CY), vegetation resilience remains high and relatively stable, whereas in the semi-arid northern agglomeration (BTH), resilience is markedly lower and more sensitive to climatic fluctuations. Although natural vegetation consistently exhibits greater resilience than the overall landscape across all study regions, this advantage is substantially weaker in BTH than in its southern counterparts, with greater interannual variability, indicating that moisture limitation undermines the buffering capacity of natural vegetation. Further analysis reveals that forest spatial aggregation is significantly and positively associated with resilience, and that this relationship is more pronounced under arid climatic conditions. Our findings suggest that the ecological vulnerability of urban–rural ecotones in arid and semi-arid regions remains underappreciated, and that the interactive effects of landscape fragmentation and climatic stress warrant explicit consideration in urban ecological planning for major agglomerations.
