# Dynamics of human activity intensity and its implication on climate extremes in China from 2000 to 2019

**Author Information**  
Yonghui Yao<sup>1</sup>, Yuchen Liu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> 中国科学院地理科学与资源研究所  

<sup>2</sup> 东北大学  



## Abstract

With the advent of the Anthropocene, human activities have exerted profound impacts on earth systems, while the characteristics and dynamics of human activity intensity (HAI) over the past half-century have remained unexplored in China. This study quantitatively analyzed the HAI patterns, changes and their impacts on climate extremes over the past two decades based on the latest high-precision HAI data from 2000 to 2019 for China. The results showed that: 1) 63.02% of China exhibited low to mild HAI, while 36.98% experienced moderate to extreme HAI. Meanwhile, HAI exhibited a distinct “east-high, west-low” spatial pattern, aligning with the Hu Huanyong Line of population demarcation. 2) 32.61% of China experienced HAI increases, and 14.49% had decreases from 2000 to 2019. No or non-significant HAI changes occupied 38.52% of eastern China but 64.33% of western China. However, the increase-to-decrease ratio of HAI in western China (2.8:1) exceeded that in eastern China (2:1). 3) Settlements in both western and eastern China exhibited left-skewed for HAI and normal distributions for HAI dynamics, while eastern settlements maintained consistently higher HAI levels with greater uniformity than western settlements. HAI pattern and its dynamics in China resulted from the coupling effects of natural, social-economic, and institutional factors. 4) HAI exerted a significant and predominantly positive impact on climate extremes in China, although its explanatory power was weaker relative to that of terrain and vegetation, robust simulation and quantitative analysis of climate extremes should incorporate anthropogenic factors as a critical model component. Human activities exerted non-negligible impacts on climate extremes through their influence on land coverage and interactions with topography. Future research should integrate high-resolution HAI data with non-linear models to unravel the feedback mechanisms between human activities and climate, thereby providing surports for social-ecomomic sustainability and climate adaptation policy-making.
