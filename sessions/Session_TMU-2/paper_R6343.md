# Decomposing Taxi Deadheading Carbon Emissions: Spatiotemporal Patterns, Environmental Drivers in Linear City

## Abstract

This study proposes a decomposition framework dividing taxi deadheading carbon emissions into essential (EDCE, spatial-structure-driven) and unproductive (UDCE, operational-inefficiency-driven) components. Using approximately 180,000 workday/non-workday operational records, real deadheading carbon emissions (RDCE) are computed via a multi-energy emission model; EDCE is derived from ideal routes of Gaode Map API, quantifying UDCE as 44.84% (workday) and 41.75% (non-workday) of RDCE. Urban environmental variables, encompassing socioeconomic, macro-built, and neighbourhood factors, are aggregated into grids. Linear and nonlinear models reveal positive and threshold effects of road density, transport facilities, and natural terrain on EDCE, with marked spatial heterogeneity. EDCE hotspots persistently cluster in the central built-up area on both day types. Distinguishing structurally irreducible from operationally avoidable emissions, this work provides dual-pathway policies: short-term intelligent dispatching and congestion management to eliminate UDCE, and long-term mixed land-use promotion, connectivity enhancement, and targeted electrification to minimise EDCE, thereby advancing sustainable urban mobility.
