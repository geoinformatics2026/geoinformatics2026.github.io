# Uncovering carpooling behavior: motivations, carbon effects, and mechanisms from a dynamic geographical perspective using GeoXAI

**Author Information**  
Zhicheng Zheng<sup>1</sup>, Yaochen Qin<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Henan University  



## Abstract

Scientific evaluation of the CO2 effects of ride-hailing carpooling is crucial for advancing CO2 emission reductions in urban transportation. However, the uncertainty in residents’ travel behaviors and their complex interactions with dynamic geographical contexts represent challenges in understanding the underlying mechanisms of these CO2 effects. By innovatively integrating spatio-temporal big data technology and the GeoXAI model, this study develops a spatio-temporal behavioral analysis framework that links the dynamic built environment, motivation of carpooling behavior, and its CO2 effects. Results from the Chengdu case study reveal significant spatio-temporal heterogeneity in the carbon impacts of carpooling. Over time, areas with high carpooling rates and emission reduction benefits initially shifted from the outer ring to the city center before spreading outwards. This indirectly confirms the profound influence of urban spatial structure on carpooling behavior. Notably, the peak period of carpooling lags approximately two hours behind the conventional commuting peak, and the carpooling rate during peak hours remains below 9%. Essentially, the spatio-temporal dynamics of carpooling motivation and its carbon effects are closely aligned with the rhythms of residents' daily activities. Carpooling is most likely to occur during noon shopping, returning home, and evening activities (e.g., dining, entertainment). Moreover, confirming that the impact of trip distance on carpooling rates follows an inverted U-shaped curve and is highly sensitive to spatio-temporal variations. It also finds that the synergistic effect of high BD, high FAR, and PT convenience can crowd out carpooling space, complementing the potential inhibitory mechanism of the built environment on carpooling demand. Socioeconomic factors act synergistically, shaped by interactions between economic capacity, time cost, and spatial supply-demand relationships. This study aims to provide both theoretical and methodological insights for reducing CO2 emissions in transportation and offer valuable guidance for decision-making and sustainable urban development.
