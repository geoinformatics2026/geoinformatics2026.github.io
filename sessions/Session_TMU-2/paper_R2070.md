# Estimation of Methane in Urban Suburbs Based on Causal Temporal Deep Learning Network

**Author Information**  
Mengyao Li<sup>1</sup>, Hongsheng Zhang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

Methane (CH4) is a critical greenhouse gas whose spatial concentration is closely linked to human socioeconomic activities, with both anthropogenic and natural emission sources. However, the spatial differentiation of methane concentration between urban and rural areas and the causal temporal relationships among influencing factors remain unclear. Accurately estimating methane concentration in urban and suburban areas is therefore of great practical significance for regional emission management. This study selects 12 countries across different provincial regions worldwide as research areas and integrates satellite and ground observation data. A deep learning model that combines causal time-series analysis with a Graph Attention Network (GAT) is developed to accurately estimate the spatiotemporal distribution of methane concentration, and the estimation results are validated. For the influencing factor analysis, both natural and anthropogenic variables are considered. Using the causal time-series model, multiple regression, and geographically weighted regression (GWR), the contributions and interactions of various factors driving regional methane concentration differences are quantitatively assessed. Results show that: (1) the integrated causal time series and GAT model achieves high estimation accuracy across multiple regions, with an R2 of 0.977; (2) significant urban-rural differences in methane concentration exist, particularly in winter, with rural high-concentration areas mainly in paddy fields, wetlands, and livestock breeding zones, while urban patterns are associated with economic development levels and infrastructure; (3) meteorological factors play a dominant role in methane concentration variation and serve as the primary factor influencing urban-rural differences in certain regions and periods, with notable causal temporal relationships identified among various factors.



Keywords: Methane estimation,  Temporal causal deep learning,  Methane urban-rural disparity
