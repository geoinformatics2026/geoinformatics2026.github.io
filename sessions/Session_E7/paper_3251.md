# Urbanization-Induced LULC Transformation and Ecosystem Service Value Dynamics in Dhaka City (1991-2050): An Integrated GIS-Machine Learning-GeoAI Framework

## Abstract

Dhaka City is one of the fastest-growing unplanned megacities in South Asia, experiencing rapid population growth over the past few decades, which has substantially transformed its land use patterns and reduced green spaces. This study tests the hypothesis that continued population growth accelerates built-up urban expansion and consequently reduces the ecosystem service value (ESV). To examine this relationship, this study developed an integrated GIS-Machine Learning-GeoAI framework to quantify historical and projected Land Use/Land Cover (LULC) changes from 1991 to 2050 in Dhaka city and to assess the associated dynamics of ESV loss. The historical LULC classification was conducted using the Random Forest (RF) algorithm within the Google Earth Engine (GEE) platform, while a hybrid Cellular Automata-Artificial Neural Network (CA-ANN) model was employed to simulate future urban expansion and ESV changes up to 2050. The results reveal a dramatic increase of built-up areas, expanding from 3,647.7 ha in 1991 to 12,842.35 ha in 2022. During the same period, vegetation cover declined from 9,495.19 ha to 7,706.95 ha, while water bodies decreased sharply from 5,270.82 ha to 1,789.16 ha. Correspondingly, total ESV declined by approximately $31 million, falling from $54.34 million in 1991 to $22.90 million in 2022. The results of statistical analysis reveal a strong positive correlation between population growth and built-up urban expansion (R = 0.949) and a strong negative correlation between population growth and ESV (R = -0.919). The simulation predicts that built-up areas will further increase by approximately 667.8 ha, accompanied by the ESV loss of nearly $2 million by the end of 2050. The findings confirm that if rapid population growth and uncontrolled urban expansion persist, the city will continue to experience significant ESV declines. The results of this study highlight the urgent need for sustainable urban planning and ecosystem-based urban management strategies.
