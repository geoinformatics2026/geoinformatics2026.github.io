# Emotional Geographies of Youth Migration: Spatiotemporal Sentiment Dynamics from Digital Traces in China

**Author Information**  
Jifei Wang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> THE CHINESE UNIVERSITY OF HONG KONG  



## Abstract

Intercity migration reshapes where people live and how they feel, yet the spatiotemporal evolution of migrant well-being remains poorly understood, lacking high-frequency, granular data. This study develops a computational framework integrating digital trace identification with multi-dimensional sentiment analysis to decode the emotional geographies of youth migration in urban China. Using Sina Weibo location data, we identified 220,820 intercity youth migrants (ages 15-34) across 337 prefecture-level cities. Instead of static administrative records, we identified migration by detecting annual shifts in users' Primary Residential Locations (PRL) via a modal location algorithm. A fine-tuned Large Language Model (LLM) decomposes social media sentiments into employment, housing, commuting, and social domains. This enables intra-group heterogeneity analysis often missed by aggregate flow models. Three findings challenge prevailing assumptions. First, an event-study design reveals a non-linear adaptation trajectory. Initial well-being deteriorates and persists beyond a transitional phase before partially recovering, directly falsifying the “honeymoon effect” narrative. Second, PSM-DiD estimation demonstrates systematic relative deprivation among migrants compared to observationally equivalent non-migrants across life domains. Third, stratifying by migration direction exposes a structural spatial trade-off. Downward migrants to lower-tier cities experience improved employment and housing sentiments but incur measurable losses in social connectivity and commuting satisfaction; upward migrants face the opposite. This highlights a complex negotiation between economic opportunities and lived urban experiences. Furthermore, gaps in digital infrastructure and income explain dimension-specific sentiment shifts, linking macro-level urban attributes to micro-level emotional outcomes. This framework advances GIScience by rendering “emotional spatiality” computationally tractable. It positions LLM-derived sentiment as a spatiotemporally indexed behavioral signal bridging cyberspace and physical urban environments, informing equity-based migration policies.



Keywords: youth migration,  emotional geography,  digital traces,  geotagged social media data,  LLM sentiment analysis,  PSM-DiD
