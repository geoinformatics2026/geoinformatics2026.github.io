# Propagation from Meteorological to Agricultural Drought and Its Driving Factors on the Loess Plateau in China

**Author Information**  
Xiaolei Wang<sup>1</sup>, Huihui Sun<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Zhengzhou University  



## Abstract

Climate warming has intensified the frequency and severity of droughts. Consequently, gaining insights into the propagation process from meteorological drought (MD) to agricultural drought (AD) and its driving factors is critical for enhancing disaster risk assessment and early warning capacities. In this study, the spatiotemporal evolution characteristics of MD and AD across the Loess Plateau (LP) in China during the period 1982–2022 were analyzed, with MD characterized by the multi-scale Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) and AD by the Standardized Soil Moisture Index (SSMI). Subsequently, the propagation time from MD to AD was quantified using the maximum Pearson correlation coefficient method, and a Copula-based probabilistic model was employed to estimate propagation probabilities and identify critical triggering thresholds. Finally, the driving relationships between drought propagation and meteorological factors, as well as human activities, were examined. The results indicate that: (1) Both MD and AD have exhibited significant intensification trends over the LP, with AD worsening at a faster rate than MD; (2) Drought propagation time exhibits distinct seasonal and regional characteristics. It is longer in autumn and winter than in spring and summer, with significant spatial heterogeneity observed across all seasons. (3) The propagation probability from MD to AD increases nonlinearly with the intensification of drought severity. Under a 70% propagation probability scenario, the MD threshold required to trigger mild AD presents a spatial pattern of “lower in the southeast and higher in the northwest” across the LP. (4) Precipitation is the dominant meteorological factor influencing propagation thresholds, while evapotranspiration and soil moisture exert significant positive effects, whereas elevation and slope inhibit drought propagation. These findings provide a scientific basis for regional drought early warning and the formulation of differentiated drought mitigation and adaptation strategies.
