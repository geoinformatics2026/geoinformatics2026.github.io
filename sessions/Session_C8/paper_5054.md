# Airbnb and Tourism Gentrification: Mapping Short-Term Rental Impacts on Residential Neighbourhoods in England

**Author Information**  
Zi Ye<sup>1</sup>, Meixu Chen<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Department of Geography and Planning, University of Liverpool  

<sup>2</sup> School of Social and Public Administration, East China University of Science and Technology  



## Abstract

The rapid expansion of short-term rental (STR) platforms such as Airbnb has changed urban tourism market and also reshaped the neighborhoods where they bring in significant tourist flows. This study examines the spatial evolution of Airbnb activity across England and investigates its relationship with residential neighbourhood changes, with particular attention to tourism-driven gentrification processes.  Using a comprehensive dataset of Airbnb listings and reservation records from 2015 to 2023, we characterise Airbnb activity at the Lower Super Output Area (LSOA) level across England through three dimensions: active listings, guest occupancy, and estimated revenue. Spatial pattern analysis reveals pronounced geographic clustering of Airbnb activity in coastal, heritage, and inner-urban areas, alongside secondary hotspots in post-pandemic rural destinations. Temporal analysis further traces how the geography of Airbnb activity responded to pandemic disruption and subsequent recovery.  We then investigate the association between Airbnb intensity and three dimensions of neighbourhood change: socioeconomic impact (Index of Multiple Deprivation), housing market pressures (residential sales and private rental prices), and residential displacement (population churn in- and out-migration rates). A Multiscale Geographically Weighted Regression (MGWR) framework captures spatially varying relationships between Airbnb concentration — measured through active listings, guest-local ratio, and estimated revenue — and these neighbourhood outcomes. Machine learning methods complement this by identifying the relative importance of Airbnb metrics among a broader set of neighbourhood predictors drawn from two decades of Census data.  Preliminary findings reveal spatially heterogeneous associations between Airbnb intensity and neighbourhood change, with distinct dynamics across urban, coastal, and rural contexts. This research for the first time examines the impact of Airbnb on neighbourhood change at a national scale drawing upon the longitudinal evidence, which contributes to debates on platform economy governance and informs emerging regulatory approaches to short-term rentals in England.
