# Assessing Spatial Justice in Public Coasts: A GIS-Based Analysis of Tourist-Resident Contestation Using LBSN Data

**Author Information**  
Jiayuan Bai<sup>1</sup>, Jun Cai<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Research Section of Environment Design, Dalian University of Technology  

<sup>2</sup> School of Architecture and Fine Art, Dalian University of Technology  



## Abstract

Rapid coastal tourism expansion in China has precipitated overtourism and intense spatial contestation over public coasts. This study leverages GIS and Location-Based Social Network (LBSN) data to assess spatial justice and explore how proactive planning can mitigate tourist-resident conflicts in Dalian. We employed spatial cluster analysis on user-generated data to categorize coastal zones based on divergent usage patterns and sentiments, identifying four distinct types of spatial contestation: squeeze, superimposition, segregation, and alienation. Subsequently, a Structural Equation Model (SEM) integrated with these spatial typologies was utilized to empirically validate the structural relationships between distributive, procedural, and recognition justice. The GIS-based analysis reveals significant disparities in spatial demands, with tourists favoring scenic consumption spaces and residents prioritizing multifunctional areas. SEM results confirm procedural justice as a foundational upstream variable; notably, recognition justice is the strongest direct predictor of satisfaction, exerting an impact nearly four times that of distributive justice. The study demonstrates that conflict mitigation requires a shift from reactive management to justice-oriented spatial planning. We propose strategies including rational spatial zoning guided by LBSN analytics, enhancing procedural transparency, and embedding recognition justice into coastal governance to foster sustainable tourism development.



Keywords: Spatial contestation,  Rights of access,  Public space,  Costal tourism development,  LLMs
