# A Large Language Model-Based Method for Constructing Historical and Cultural Knowledge Graphs and Its Application in Atlas Content Organization

**Author Information**  
Lili Jiang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> 中国科学院地理科学与资源研究所  



## Abstract

Historical and cultural knowledge systems are essential for addressing the lack of systematic organization in atlas content design. Given the fragmented nature and complex interrelations of historical knowledge, this paper proposes an LLM-assisted method for constructing a domain knowledge system to support the "map group—theme—map sheet—indicator" structure of atlases. Using authoritative historical directories, LLMs perform semantic parsing to extract a top-level knowledge framework encompassing fields such as religious culture. Taking religious culture as a case study, a Chain-of-Thought-based stepwise extraction process is designed to transform unstructured texts into structured knowledge graphs, with entity disambiguation combining Jaccard similarity and LLM judgment to enhance consistency. Experiments show Qwen-Plus achieves superior performance (Precision: 79.0%, Recall: 77.37%, F₁-score: 78.16%), resulting in a knowledge graph containing 4,706 entities and 8,425 triplets visualized in Neo4j. This method effectively integrates fragmented historical knowledge into a coherent, spatially expressible system, providing a reliable foundation for historical and cultural atlas compilation.
