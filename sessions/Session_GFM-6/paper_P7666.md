# A Knowledge-Guided Urban Regeneration Decision Framework: Integrating Spatial Knowledge Graphs and Large Language Models

**Author Information**  
Shunli Wang<sup>1</sup>, Minmin Li<sup>2</sup>, Zhipeng Zhong<sup>2</sup>, Jinhao Gu<sup>2</sup>, Yafei Wang<sup>1</sup>, Renzhong Guo<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Shenzhen University  

<sup>2</sup> Guangdong Laboratory of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Economy (SZ)  



## Abstract

Urban regeneration involves heterogeneous knowledge, including policy texts, spatial evidence, project precedents, evaluation criteria, and administrative procedures. Traditional planning support approaches are often limited by data silos, fragmented knowledge, and weak reasoning capacity, while general-purpose large language models (LLMs) remain vulnerable to hallucination and poor source traceability in regulation-intensive contexts. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a knowledge-guided urban regeneration decision framework integrating spatial knowledge graphs (SKGs) and LLMs. The framework consists of three layers: a knowledge base, a domain model, and scenario applications. An Urban Renewal Knowledge Graph was constructed from 450 historical projects and 230 policy documents, containing more than 10, 000 nodes and 11, 000 edges. On this basis, a domain-specific LLM enhanced by Graph Retrieval- Augmented Generation (GraphRAG) was developed to support multi-hop retrieval and reasoning for policy interpretation, precedent retrieval, and scheme review. Experimental results show that the proposed framework achieves a context precision of 0.99 and improves answer correctness by 15% over baseline models, while significantly reducing policy analysis time and improving the standardization of plan evaluation. The study demonstrates that combining structured knowledge graphs with generative AI can provide a more interpretable, reliable, and scalable paradigm for urban regeneration decision support.
