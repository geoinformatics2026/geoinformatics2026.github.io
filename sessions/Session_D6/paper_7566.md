# Asymmetric Effects Between Urban-Induced Cropland Loss and Cropland Displacement in Africa

**Author Information**  
Xintong Jiang<sup>1</sup>, Xun Liang<sup>1</sup>, Qingfeng Guan<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> China University of Geosciences  



## Abstract

Urban expansion threatens food security not only through direct cropland loss but also through indirect cropland displacement, yet the magnitude and structural asymmetry between these two processes remain poorly quantified. This knowledge gap is particularly critical in Africa, where rapid urbanization coincides with one of the world’s fastest population growth rates and where approximately 95% of food production gains rely on cropland expansion rather than yield improvement. Here we integrate historical land-use data with a mc patch-generating land use simulation model (mcPLUS) to develop a coupled human–land framework that explicitly quantifies the asymmetric relationship between urban-induced cropland loss and cropland displacement across Africa from 1985 to 2020. We further construct a Food Sufficiency Oriented Scenario, defining annual cropland demand as the sum of the previous year’s urban-induced loss and additional cropland required to meet rising food demand, to simulate land-system dynamics from 2020 to 2080. Between 1985 and 2020, urban expansion directly converted 72,000 km² of cropland but triggered 2.44 million km² of cropland displacement, representing a mean amplification of 34-fold and peaking at 76-fold under a logarithmic nonlinear relationship. Under future food-security constraints, this asymmetry intensifies substantially, with cropland displacement projected to reach approximately 260 times urban-induced loss by 2080, while their relationship shifts from systematic amplification to scenario-dependent heterogeneous adjustment. These findings reveal a nonlinear and structurally asymmetric land-system response, demonstrating that future food security-oriented land use change in Africa will be increasingly governed by indirect displacement pressures rather than direct urban encroachment alone.



Keywords: cropland displacement,  asymmetric effects,  large-scale land-use simulation,  Africa
