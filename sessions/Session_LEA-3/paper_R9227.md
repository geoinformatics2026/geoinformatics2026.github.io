# Quantifying the Impact of Urban Expansion on Urban Ecological Resilience: A Multi-Scale Gradient Analysis

**Author Information**  
Jinyang Wang<sup>1</sup>, Qimin Cheng<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> State Key Laboratory of Information Engineering in Surveying, Mapping and Remote Sensing, Wuhan University  

<sup>2</sup> School of Electronic Information and Communications, Huazhong University of Science and Technology  



## Abstract

Urban expansion in China is rapidly transforming regional ecosystems, posing escalating challenges to urban ecological resilience. However, the spatially heterogeneous impacts of urban growth on ecosystem stability and recovery capacity remain insufficiently quantified for spatial planning. This study integrates multi-source satellite observations and environmental data from 2000 to 2020 to assess how urban expansion alters ecological resilience across the Yangtze River Economic Belt (YREB). County-level ecological resilience was first quantified using an improved Geographically Weighted Principal Component Analysis (GWPCA) framework, integrating key functional indicators such as Net Primary Productivity (NPP) alongside hydro-climatic and environmental variables. A multi-ring buffer model was then applied around expanding built-up areas to characterize urban-rural impact gradients, separate climate-driven signals from human-induced degradation, and identify distance-dependent and directional effects. The results indicate an overall regional improvement in vegetation functioning and ecological resilience over the two decades, consistent with macro-scale conservation and restoration efforts. Specifically, NPP exhibited a clear long-term increase, with a mean growth rate of 4.28 gC·m⁻²·yr⁻¹; yet, this regional enhancement conceals pronounced local resilience suppression adjacent to newly urbanized land. Expansion-related impacts vary significantly by city type: megacity regions show more human-dominated constraints and broader resilience-deficit, whereas inland and high-elevation cities display greater sensitivity to climatic variability. The eco-urban coupling coordination improved over time and reached 0.663 in 2020, indicating progress but persistent mismatches between rapid urbanization and ecological capacity. Overall, the impacts of urban expansion extended well beyond city cores and exhibited clear spatial anisotropy along development corridors. These findings offer a scientific basis for optimizing low-carbon territorial planning and ecological resilience. By delineating high-resilience vegetation buffers and prioritizing restoration in high-pressure peri-urban zones, policymakers can formulate spatially targeted mitigation pathways to advance regional sustainable development.
