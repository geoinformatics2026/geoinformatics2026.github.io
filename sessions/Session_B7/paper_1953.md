# Comparing Weighting Techniques for a Composite Spatial Indicator of Urban Heat Vulnerability

**Author Information**  
Ryan Turner<sup>1</sup>, Chayn Sun<sup>1</sup>, Matt Duckham<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> RMIT University  



## Abstract

Abstract - Weighting techniques are a crucial methodological step in generating composite spatial indicators of urban heat vulnerability, as they distil interpretable information from various complex spatiotemporal data. However, a lack of consensus and standardisation in the choice of weighting technique leads to potential bias, reduced reproducibility and ultimately results in a disconnected excess of outcomes from many different approaches. To demonstrate this, this study systematically evaluates three common weighting techniques of equal weighting (EQW), entropy weighting (ENW), and principal component analysis (PCA), exploring their theoretical basis, and quantifying their impact on resultant urban heat vulnerability indices through spatial and statistical analyses. A 50.29% disagreement rate in vulnerability classifications across the three techniques highlights the importance of intentional methodological design to ensure reliable and interpretable outcomes of spatial urban heat vulnerability assessments. The observed discrepancies further demonstrate that when employed as decision-support tools, urban heat vulnerability indices require a rigorously justified and coherent methodological framework to bolster confidence in outcomes.



Keywords: urban heat vulnerability,  urban heat risk,  urban informatics,  composite spatial indicator,  weighting,  equal weighting,  entropy weighting,  principal component analysis
