# Understanding human mobility patterns disrupted by the 2025 California wildfires using mobile phone location data

**Author Information**  
Kai Sun<sup>1</sup>, Nina Lam<sup>1</sup>, Yingjie Hu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Chinese University of Hong Kong  

<sup>2</sup> University at Buffalo  



## Abstract

The rising frequency and intensity of wildfires pose significant threats to people and communities. Human mobility is a key lens for examining disaster disruptions and community resilience in the face of such events. There is limited understanding in how human mobility patterns are disrupted by wildfire disasters and how those patterns vary spatially across communities. This study examines human mobility shifts due to the devastating Eaton and Palisades wildfires in California in January 2025 using anonymized mobile phone location data. We examine the shifts across three stages: the wildfire stage, the immediate post-wildfire stage, and the mid-term recovery stage. Three indices, the Visitor Count Change (VCC) index, the Trip Distance Change (TDC) index, and the Mobility Intensity Change (MIC) index, are used to quantify shifts in human mobility patterns due to these wildfires at the census tract level. We employ both spatial and nonspatial statistical algorithms to identify physical and socioeconomic variables that potentially influence human mobility shifts. Preliminary results indicate spatial and temporal disparities in wildfire-induced disruptions. They also suggest that factors such as the share of older adults, unemployment rate, housing overcrowding, and lack of vehicle access may be associated with wildfire-related mobility responses across the two wildfires. These findings enhance our understanding of wildfire disruptions and could inform future response and mitigation efforts aimed at identifying and supporting vulnerable communities.
