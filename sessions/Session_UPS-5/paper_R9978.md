# Combining Street View Imagery and Vision-Language Models to Map Informal Settlements

**Author Information**  
Sixuan Li<sup>1</sup>, Alice Duhaut<sup>2</sup>, Geetika Nagpal<sup>2</sup>, Michele Andreottola<sup>2</sup>, Cong Peng<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Peking University  

<sup>2</sup> The World Bank  



## Abstract

Mapping and monitoring informal settlements at scale demands interpretable, spatially explicit measures of street-level conditions, yet household surveys and engineering audits remain costly and infrequent. Street-level imagery, now available globally through platforms such as Google Street View, captures rich signals about built environment quality, infrastructure, and economic activity that would otherwise be difficult to observe systematically. We develop a method that translates this imagery into auditable measures of informality: views are sampled along road networks within settlements, and a vision-language model extracts structured attributes spanning commercial activity, basic services, road environment, and building quality, which are then aggregated into interpretable composite scores. The method retains uncertainty signals and raw evidence for targeted human review, making it suitable for both settlement characterization and program evaluation. We apply this pipeline to Kenya, linking the resulting Street-informed Informality Index (SII) to settlements treated by the Kenya Informal Settlements Improvement Project (KISIP). Within-county fixed-effects models, complemented by remote sensing covariates and propensity-score matching, show that KISIP1 exposure is associated with lower composite informality. Improvements concentrate in public-goods dimensions, with road environment and basic services showing the largest gains. These findings illustrate how the pipeline can reveal which visible dimensions of informality shift following an intervention, and they suggest a practical path for monitoring and evaluating urban upgrading programs at scale.
