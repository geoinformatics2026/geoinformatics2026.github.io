# Beyond the Shortest Path: Perception-Aware GIS Routing Using Multimodal GeoAI

**Author Information**  
Menglei Guo<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Manchester  



## Abstract

Cycling is a critical pillar for sustainable urban mobility, yet cyclists often deviate from the physically shortest routes—a phenomenon known as the “shortest-path paradox” (Lima et al., 2016). Rooted in behavioral geography, this research addresses the paradox by identifying "cognitive friction", such as perceived danger or visual stress, in spatial decision-making (Meister et al., 2023). Conventional GIS models often fail to quantify these subjective costs, as they focus primarily on physical distance or travel time. To bridge this gap, this study presents a Perception-Enhanced Path Planning (PAHR) framework that integrates Multimodal GeoAI with Street-View Imagery (SVI). We utilise Visual Question Answering (VQA) as a reasoning tool to interpret SVI, effectively translating raw visual semantics into a quantifiable Perception-Aware Impedance (PAI)—essentially a digital "weight" that reflects how a human perceives the environment (Xu et al., 2025). By treating the Large Language Model as a "geographic cognitive agent," the framework assigns these perceptual weights to road segments. These are then incorporated into a spatially explicit routing graph, where the model optimises paths that are not just physically short, but psychologically comfortable. The framework was validated using a high-fidelity dataset comprising GPS trajectories collected via mobile sensing from volunteers in Tianjin, China. Preliminary results indicate a substantial improvement in prediction accuracy, with the perception-aware model achieving over 76% route similarity with observed trajectories in visually complex urban areas, significantly outperforming traditional algorithms. This research contributes a novel GeoAI-driven methodology for integrating perceptual factors into spatial decision-making and offers new tools for identifying perceived bottlenecks in sustainable urban mobility and human-centred transportation planning.
