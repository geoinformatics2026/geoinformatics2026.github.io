# Assessing the relationship between built environment change and socioeconomic attributes evolution using time series street view imagery

**Author Information**  
Kun Zhou<sup>1</sup>, Filip Biljecki<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National University of Singapore  



## Abstract

Exploring how cities can be understood through their appearance has long been a central concern in urban studies. Existing research has examined how visual features of the built environment can be used to infer socioeconomic attributes, but most analyses typically use only the newest data available or cross-sectional observations, making it difficult to capture how socioeconomic attributes change influence environmental changes over time. Recent advances have enabled change detection through remote sensing data. However, systematically identifying changes in the built environment and evaluating how they relate to changes in socioeconomic attributes remains a significant challenge. This study bridges the gap by applying time series street view imagery (SVI) and highlighting street furniture as a micro-environmental indicator. Our approach first aligns panoramic images from different years using pixel similarity, correcting for horizontal misalignment caused by variations in capture orientation and panorama starting points. Second, we apply large vision-language models (VLMs) to detect street furniture changes in time series SVI, analyzing 39,578 images from four cities in the United States. Third, we assessed the relationship between changes in street furniture and in different socioeconomic attributes to examine whether the micro-scale environmental element is related to dynamic fluctuations of socioeconomic factors. Results show that VLMs reliably identify fine-grained street furniture changes in time series SVI and can distinguish change types. Further experiments reveal that the relationship between street furniture change and socioeconomic attributes change varies substantially across cities and neighbourhood contexts, indicating that the relationships between street furniture change and socioeconomic factors evolution are complex and context-dependent. This study demonstrates the effectiveness of time series SVI in capturing neighbourhood changes and highlights its potential for advancing the understanding of dynamic urban processes.
