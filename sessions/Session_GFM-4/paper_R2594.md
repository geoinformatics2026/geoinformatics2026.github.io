# An individual sample-based approach: a new thinking in geographic analysis

**Author Information**  
A-Xing Zhu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Wisconsin-Madison  



## Abstract

Geographic analysis increasingly depends on the rapidly available geospatial data that is generated by citizens.  While the provision of this new form of data mitigates the challenges in geographic data collection, particularly sample collection, it does bring stiff challenges to geographic analysis based on the average model concept which requires data (samples) to be representative in a geographic sense, that is often not met with this new form of data. This paper presents a new thinking on geographic analysis based on the Third Law of Geography. Under the Third Law of Geography, geographic analysis can be conducted based on geographic similarity between a sample and a point of interest. This allows the unique representation of a single sample to be used in geographic assessment. Research examples from spatial prediction have shown that geographic analysis based on this individual sample perspective does not require samples to be of specific size nor a particular spatial distribution to achieve a high-quality prediction. The uncertainty associated with each prediction based on this new thinking is more indicative to the quality of the prediction, thus can be used to show the spatial distribution of prediction accuracy. These properties make geographic analysis based on this new thinking more adapted in effective use of citizen-generated spatial data in the spatial big data era.
