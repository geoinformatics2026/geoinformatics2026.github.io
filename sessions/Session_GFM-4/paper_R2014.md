# Place reviews as an emerging geospatial instrument for sensing the built environment

**Author Information**  
Filip Biljecki<sup>1</sup>, Haixiao Liu<sup>1</sup>, Junbo Xia<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National University of Singapore  



## Abstract

Advancing geo-innovations for sustainable and resilient cities necessitates widely available spatial data on urban spaces and should include humans. While geospatial data sources such as points of interest (POIs), street view imagery (SVI), and mobile phone trajectories have become well established in GIScience, one growing data stream remains largely untapped: place reviews on platforms such as Google Maps, Dianping, and TripAdvisor. Much as SVI emerged two decades ago as a new lens on cities, place reviews now present a similar opportunity in GIScience, yet their potential remains underexplored. This presentation comprises two parts. The first establishes a position and a conceptual foundation: we characterise place reviews as geocoded, timestamped, longitudinal, crowdsourced, and multimodal (text and images) records that characterise human experiences at specific locations, and discuss considerations such as bias, quality, data access, challenges, and differences among services. The second part affirms their potential through two case studies using those sourced from Google Maps, both centred on Singapore, offering relevance to the host city of the conference. The first case study presents an innovation in urban functional classification: it utilises reviews to infer urban functions at multiple scales, demonstrating that analysing and processing reviews results in an activity-aware geospatial data layer extending standard POI data and in robustness across multiple spatial scales. The second example leverages over five million reviews in conjunction with time-series SVI to analyse urban emotions, presenting another novelty: revealing how urban transformations such as changes in greenery shape emotional responses, which are sensed from place reviews. These contributions advocate for place reviews as a complementary and underexploited geospatial data source compatible with GeoAI developments and use cases, and offers advantages such as cross-city comparison. We conclude by outlining directions towards integrating place reviews into GIScience, in particular VGI research.
