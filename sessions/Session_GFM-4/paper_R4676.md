# Foundational issues of GeoAI in the era of LLMs

**Author Information**  
Song Gao<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Wisconsin-Madison  



## Abstract

Geospatial Artificial Intelligence (GeoAI) is the integration of geospatial technologies and AI, especially for developing data-driven and knowledge-guided spatially-explicit machine learning and deep learning methods for geographical sciences. GeoAI can also be considered as a subject to develop intelligent computer programs (e.g., agents) with capabilities to mimic the processes of human perception, spatial reasoning, and discovery about geographical phenomena and dynamics. Despite its high importance, the development of sophisticated geospatial capabilities has been a secondary focus in the mainstream advancement of large language models (LLMs) and multimodal AI agents. This has created a critical gap: while foundation models and AI agents excel at generalist tasks, they may struggle to comprehend and reason with specialized geospatial data (e.g., vector layers for wildfire spread boundary, platial layers for vague cognitive regions, raster imagery for flood mapping), and complex geospatial tasks (e.g., multi-phase spatial reasoning, agentic spatial analysis workflows). This paper aims to discuss some of the key scientific questions and challenging issues of GeoAI in the era of LLMs.  The science of GeoAI may cover four fundamental pillars: predictability, interpretability, reproducibility, and social responsibility (e.g., transparency, fairness, trustworthiness, and ethics), which have been critical for spatially explicit modeling and geographical knowledge discovery. These scientific issues remain important in the era of foundation models and (geo-)agentic AI. In addition, some of the foundational issues in GeoAI that are worth exploring in the era of LLMs may include (but not limit to) (1) the representation gap, (2) uncertainty, (3) geospatial hallucination, (4) spatial or geographical biases, and (5) geo-alignment. Addressing these emerging issues and risks in GeoAI development requires not only technical solutions but also societal and ethical discussions.
