# Global vegetation carbon loss driven by drought

**Author Information**  
Wenli Liu<sup>1</sup>, Longgang Xiang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Wuhan University  



## Abstract

In the context of rising global temperatures, advancing carbon neutrality has emerged as a key pathway to achieving sustainable development. However, the increasing frequency of drought events poses a serious threat to the carbon budget dynamics of global ecosystems. To better understand the impact of drought on vegetation carbon sinks, this study developed a comprehensive drought index and applied run theory to identify and extract drought events from time-series data. Based on this framework, we quantitatively assessed vegetation carbon losses caused by drought, analysed the evolutionary patterns of carbon source/sink functions, and explored the probability of carbon loss under various drought scenarios. The results show a general intensification of drought conditions worldwide, particularly pronounced in regions such as Central Asia and Africa. The duration of drought exhibits a spatial pattern that gradually decreases from arid to humid zones. Across most parts of the globe, a decline in carbon sink capacity was observed, with the most substantial changes in net ecosystem exchange (NEE) occurring in Central Asia due to drought stress. Nevertheless, the effects of drought on vegetation carbon sinks are not uniformly adverse; in certain areas, such as the eastern coast of North America, carbon sink capacity has improved. Vegetation types vary considerably in their response to drought, with evergreen broadleaf forests (EBF) and deciduous broadleaf forests (DBF) showing more pronounced reductions in carbon sequestration relative to other vegetation classes. As drought intensity increases, the probability of vegetation carbon loss rises markedly, particularly under extreme drought conditions, where ecosystems in both sub-humid and humid regions become more susceptible to impairment of carbon sink functions.
