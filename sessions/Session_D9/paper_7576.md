# Towards Complexity-Aware Scenario Modelling in Urban Digital Twins: A Socio-Technical Framework

**Author Information**  
Candela Sol Pelliza<sup>1</sup>, André Barriguinha<sup>1</sup>, Miguel Castro Neto<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> NOVA Information Management School  



## Abstract

Urban Digital Twins (UDTs) have emerged as promising tools for data-driven urban planning, generating high expectations across the academic, technical and policy domains, particularly towards the predictive capabilities of simulation models as decision-support tools. Yet, current implementations fall short on this promise: most UDTs focus predominantly on the physical dimensions of cities, neglecting social and behavioural dynamics, and lack robust what-if scenario capabilities that acknowledge urban complexity and uncertainty. Furthermore, existing simulation approaches struggle to translate predictions into actionable decision-support tools that urban authorities can practically adopt. Together, these gaps reveal a need to fundamentally rethink how what-if scenarios and simulation models for UDTs are conceived, developed and integrated, so they can effectively account for social dynamics and complex systems’ uncertainty from their conception, better serving data-driven urban planning. This work seeks to develop a conceptual framework for socio-technical, complexity-aware what-if scenario modelling in UDTs, bridging the gap between technical simulation capabilities and urban governance needs. The study follows a two-step approach: first, a structured literature synthesis across three interconnected strands, namely limitations of urban simulations in UDTs and contributions from complexity science; socio-technical approaches to UDT modelling, design, and integration; and decision-support strategies for scenario tools in urban planning, maps the theoretical and methodological foundations to support this shift. Building on this, a conceptual scenario-modelling workflow is designed for its integration within the NexTCity project UDT, focused on scenario planning for decarbonisation in Lisbon. The framework seeks to specify which modelling approaches best address key socio-technical dimensions, such as social dynamics, uncertainty, and participation, and define the pathway for their technical integration into UDTs. The outcomes will contribute to the growing discussion on making UDTs socially responsive and practically useful, offering a replicable framework and laying the groundwork for future empirical model development and validation UDT applications.



Keywords: Urban Digital Twins,  Socio-technical approach,  Urban complexity
