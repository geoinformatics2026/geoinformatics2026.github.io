# Understanding movement and behaviors of minnows in experimentally derived complex turbulent flows using novel geospatial datasets

**Author Information**  
Adam Grodek<sup>1</sup>, Sean Bennett<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University at Buffalo/Singapore Institute of Management  

<sup>2</sup> University at Buffalo  



## Abstract

The general objective of this research is to investigate the movement and schooling behaviors of minnows within complex, turbulent river flows using novel geospatial datasets. Using a laboratory setting, the study utilizes a recirculating hydraulic flume and an overhead camera system to collect two primary datasets. These datasets include instantaneous velocity data obtained through large-scale particle image velocimetry and fish swimming pathways derived from videos of fish swimming in experimental trials. By integrating these novel geospatial datasets, this study examines how fish respond to temporal and spatial variations in turbulent environments in various experimental settings. The project aims to quantify the specific time and length scales at which fish transition from individual to collective behaviors. It is expected that these scales will vary based on the complexity and energy of the flow conditions. Additionally, the research seeks to identify the preferred pathways that fish schools use when navigating through turbulent river sections. This work introduces methods for primary data collection to explore individual and group decision-making processes that are currently not well-understood in scientific literature. Ultimately, the findings will offer insights into fish adaptation and emergent behaviors, potentially challenging current practices in fish passage design and the restoration of stream corridors. Ultimately, this work seeks to better inform stream restoration, fish passage design and engineering, and assist with the management of waterways that are conducive to the survival of small-bodied fish.
