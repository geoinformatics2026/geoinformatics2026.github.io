# Spatial Inequality in the Landscape of Fencing: A Geographical Analysis of USA Fencing Clubs

**Author Information**  
Kevin Lu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Overlake School  



## Abstract

Fencing in the United States often framed as a high- cost, elite sport-shows a highly uneven geography that mirrors broader spatial privilege and unequal access. Using the national distribution of USA Fencing member clubs, this paper applies point-pattern analysis and density-based clustering to identify concentrated metropolitan hubs and large underserved regions. I interpret these patterns through intertwined mechanisms: participation costs, the concentration of cultural and educational capital, historical pathways through private clubs and universities, and travel constraints that structure competitive participation. Brief case studies of Fencer's Club illustrate how legacy, coaching networks, and urban agglomeration consolidate fencing capacity in a small number of advantaged locales. I argue that this "elite agglomeration" dynamic reproduces inequities in opportunity and talent development, and I outline implications for expanding access through regional capacity-building and targeted outreach.



Keywords: Fencing,  Spatial Inequity,  Clustering Analysis
