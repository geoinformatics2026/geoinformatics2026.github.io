# A Soybean Mapping Framework via Multi-Index-Derived Pseudo-Label Enhancement and Contrastive Embedding Neural Network

**Author Information**  
Tianyu Liu<sup>1</sup>, Jin Chen<sup>1</sup>, Qiang Li<sup>1</sup>, Kai Tang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Beijing Normal University  



## Abstract

Accurate and timely monitoring of soybeans spatial distribution is critical for ensuring food security and promoting the formulation of mutually beneficial international trade policies. However, large-scale mapping continues to face two major practical challenges: index-based methods exhibit limited transferability across regions and years due to their sensitivity to threshold setting, and classifier-based methods are constrained by the scarcity, high acquisition cost, and potential inaccuracy of training samples. We propose a fully label-free soybean mapping framework that combines multi-index pseudo-label generation and the Pseudo-label Enhanced Contrastive Embedding Network (PEACE-Net). Pseudo-labels are generated by applying a majority voting mechanism to three well-validated soybean indices (GWCCI, GWCCI2, and SMCI), which collectively capture a broad spectrum of discriminative features of soybean, with cross-validation implemented across the indices to enhance reliability. PEACE-Net is a dual-head Transformer architecture incorporating a soft probability update mechanism: a contrastive head that constructs a discriminative embedding space, and a classification head whose soft outputs iteratively refine pseudo-labels, thereby progressively reducing the risk of overfitting under weak supervision. The framework was evaluated across five representative sites in the United States, Brazil, Argentina, and China from 2021 to 2024, and through three large-scale regional mapping experiments. Multi-index pseudo-labels demonstrated more accurate and stable performance than single-index labels. PEACE-Net outperformed baseline methods across sites and years, achieving an average OA of 90.72% and F1-score of 0.91, with balanced Precision and Recall. Large-scale regional mapping in three areas shows a strong linear correlation (R² > 0.90) between mapped soybean area and county-level data, closely matching official records. These findings underscore the significant potential of the proposed framework for large-scale soybean mapping at both regional and global scales. By integrating the complementary strengths of index-based and classifier-based methods, the framework achieves fully automated, end-to-end, and label-free mapping with high efficiency and precision.



Keywords: Crop mapping,  Pseudo-label,  Crop index,  Soft probability update mechanism,  Contrastive learning
