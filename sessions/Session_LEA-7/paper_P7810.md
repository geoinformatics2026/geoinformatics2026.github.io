# Field Validation of Chlorophyll Fluorescence Imaging for Early Detection of Wheat Powdery Mildew: Identifying QYmax as a Key Physiological Indicator

**Author Information**  
Zhichao Chen<sup>1</sup>, Yijia Chen<sup>1</sup>, Chengyuan Hao<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Henan Polytechnic University  



## Abstract

Wheat powdery mildew poses a severe threat to global food security, and its early detection is crucial for achieving precise prevention and control. Although chlorophyll fluorescence imaging technology has demonstrated potential for early diagnosis in laboratory studies, its practical application in complex field environments remains limited. To address this gap, this study aimed to validate whether this technology can detect photosynthetic physiological stress induced by pathogen infection before visible symptoms appear under natural field conditions, thereby assessing its feasibility for early disease warning. In experimental fields in Henan Province, China, we systematically collected chlorophyll fluorescence images and key parameters (QYmax, Rfd, Y(NO)) from 94 wheat leaves at three critical growth stages. Simultaneously, disease severity (DS) was quantified using automated software. The results validated that QYmax, reflecting the maximum photochemical efficiency of photosystem II, showed the strongest negative correlation with DS $(r=-0.77)$. It exhibited a consistent and significant decline throughout disease progression, often preceding visible symptoms. In contrast, Rfd and Y(NO) parameters demonstrated greater variability. This study confirms the feasibility of using chlorophyll fluorescence imaging technology, with QYmax as a sensitive indicator, for the early detection of physiological stress caused by wheat powdery mildew in the field. The established technical workflow, integrating high- resolution fluorescence imaging with automated disease assessment, provides essential data and a methodological foundation for developing monitoring strategies based on photosynthetic physiology.



Keywords: Chlorophyll Fluorescence Imaging, Powdery mildew of wheat, Disease Detection
