## Abstract

Spatiotemporal heterogeneity is a fundamental attribute of geographic phenomena and serves as a core conceptual link between GIScience and artificial intelligence. It causes deep learning operators with global stationarity assumptions to exhibit limited adaptability when applied to geographically heterogeneous processes. Although various heterogeneity-aware strategies have been developed for model refinement, the fragmentation of existing studies hinders the distillation of a generalized methodology for heterogeneity modeling. This paper proposes a geographic perspective of “reconciling deep learning with spatiotemporal heterogeneity”, examining how spatiotemporal heterogeneity serves as a geographic prior to guide the reconfiguration of deep learning models. Based on a dual-axis framework of stratified and local heterogeneity, we developed a taxonomy of deep learning methods for heterogeneity modeling. The taxonomy comprises six core strategies: stratified divide-and-conquer, stratified feature embedding, stratified parameter learning, local feature embedding, local parameter learning, and local auxiliary supervision. Using this taxonomy, we reviewed the implementation pathways and empirical performance of existing methods across representative geographic scenarios and further discussed key frontier challenges related to model transferability, interpretability, computational complexity, uncertainty quantification, and spatiotemporal foundation models. The established taxonomy provides a unified cognitive framework for heterogeneity-aware deep learning research, advancing geospatial artificial intelligence toward a knowledge-driven paradigm.
