# 3D geovisualisation of a complex urban environment using UAV remote sensing in Sheffield, UK

**Author Information**  
Jingxia Wang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The University of Sheffield  



## Abstract

Effective communication and interpretation of microscale urban heat require visualisations that link local thermal patterns to 3D urban form, terrain, and shading. This study develops a 3D geovisualisation methodology that integrates UAV-derived thermal products and mobile sensing temperature observations into a campus-scale 3D model in Sheffield, UK. The aim is to enable interactive exploration of land surface temperature (LST) in relation to buildings, surface characteristics, and topography. A DJI Matrice 4T was used to acquire RGB and thermal imagery during multiple surveys in late June 2025, with flight planning targeting approximately 80% forward and side overlap, supported by the Royal Society. RGB images were processed to generate an orthomosaic and photogrammetric outputs. Thermal imagery was processed using documented, on-site acquisition parameters and then co-registered to high-resolution basemaps, with a 5 m digital surface model (DSM) used as an additional elevation reference and for building geovisualisation. This study constructs a campus-scale 3D scene by combining the DSM surface with building footprints, with building footprints extruded using DSM-derived heights in ArcGIS Pro. The georeferenced LST orthomosaic is then overlaid onto the 3D model to visualise surface temperature patterns on and around buildings and across the terrain. High-resolution RGB orthomosaics provide contextual layers and land cover and even material proxies (e.g., vegetation, roofs and pavements) to support interpretation of thermal contrasts in relation to 3D urban structure. The resulting 3D geovisualisation in an ArcGIS Pro local scene enables intuitive inspection of thermal variation around buildings and along streets, supports clear communication to non-specialists, and provides a transferable approach for interactive visualisation and exploration of UAV thermal survey outputs and for future digital twin development in complex urban environments.
