# Multi-scale urban emergency simulation based on digital twins

**Author Information**  
Haojia Lin<sup>1</sup>, Renzhong Guo<sup>1</sup>, Biao He<sup>1</sup>, Xi Kuai<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Shenzhen University  



## Abstract

Enhancing emergency management capabilities is critical to the construction of sustainable cities. Urban emergency management, however, is a complex system involving interactions between urban virtual space and real space, which requires simulation in the virtual space and feedback to the real space. Digital twins hold great potential in addressing this challenge, but the fragmentation issue among modeling, simulation, and visualization processes remains unresolved. To solve this problem, this study integrates these three aspects into a unified framework and proposes a multi-scale simulation method based on digital twins, with epidemic risk assessment as a case study. Firstly, an entity-oriented unified urban database is constructed, integrating multi-source urban data to establish a digital twin of the urban human settlement environment for emergency modeling. Secondly, a multi-scale urban emergency simulation method in a three-dimensional geographical environment is proposed to support emergency simulation for multi-level urban management units. Furthermore, an adaptive fusion rendering method for emergency information and three-dimensional scenes is developed to enhance the refinement of emergency visualization. Experimental validation is conducted through thousands of epidemic outbreak scenarios for epidemic risk assessment. The results demonstrate that the proposed method can effectively construct a digital twin of the urban human settlement environment, enabling integrated multi-scale emergency modeling, simulation, and visualization. This approach contributes to improving the effectiveness and efficiency of urban emergency management.
