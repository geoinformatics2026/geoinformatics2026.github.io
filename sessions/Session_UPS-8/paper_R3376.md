# An Indicator-driven Architecture for Decarbonisation-Oriented Urban Digital Twins

**Author Information**  
Nuno F. Soares<sup>1</sup>, Candela Sol Pelliza<sup>2</sup>, André Barriguinha<sup>2</sup>, Miguel Neto<sup>2</sup>, Bruno Jardim<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> CCG/ZGDV Institute & ALGORITMI Research Centre, University of Minho  

<sup>2</sup> NOVA Information Management School  



## Abstract

Urban Digital Twins for climate action are frequently presented either as technology integration initiatives or as 3D visualisation platforms, while the architectural rationale that connects data, indicators, governance, and operational use remains underexplained. This paper addresses that gap by introducing an architecture-first approach for a decarbonisation-oriented Urban Digital Twin, defined from system responsibilities, decision-support requirements, and expected operational use rather than from implementation contingencies or a fixed technology stack. The proposal is applied in a multi-city initiative spanning the three Portuguese Mission Cities, Porto, Guimarães, and Lisbon, initially centred on use-cases in the domains of buildings and mobility emissions. The architecture is structured around a set of interrelated functions covering external system integration, management of contextual and historical information, indicator calculation, analytics and simulation support, thematic and 3D visualisation, open-data exposure, and traceability. A key feature of the approach is the combination of indicator-oriented modelling with a semantic interoperability layer that allows climate actions, datasets, and derived results to be connected across heterogeneous municipal settings under consistent governance conditions. Within this perspective, the Urban Digital Twin is treated not as a purely visual representation of the city, but as an operational environment for monitoring, simulating and forecasting decarbonisation interventions and their effects on emissions. This architectural stance supports the progressive inclusion of new datasets, indicators, and urban domains without requiring the replacement of existing municipal platforms. The work illustrates the approach through representative slices from the buildings and mobility pillars, and discusses how the proposed structure supports comparability across cities while remaining extensible for future scenario-based evolution. The contribution lies in making explicit how the main architectural capabilities of a climate-oriented Urban Digital Twin can be organised to support coherent integration, indicator management, analysis, visual interpretation, and governance across different urban contexts.
