# Learning from Failure: Self-Evolving Spatial Skills for Textual Spatial Reasoning in Large Language Models

**Author Information**  
Linyue Wang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National University of Defense Technology  



## Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) often suffer from error proporgations when an intermediate spatial cognition becomes inaccurate. Most existing methods repair this issue case by case, failing to consistently improve the performance of spatial reasoning in future tasks. To address this, we propose Self-Evolving Spatial Skills (SESS), a framework that integrates structured reasoning over a shared spatial representation with failure-driven continuous learning, allowing the system to record failures, abstract recurrent error patterns, and synthesize validated corrections into long-term memories or executable skills. Across three tested benchmarks, our approach achieves its most significant performance gains on the highly challenging multi-step relational composition task, outperforming a static skill library baseline by 9.5%. On other tasks, our approach notably enhances robustness of path selection and consistency of solution process. The experiment results suggest that consolidating repeated failures into long-term assets could significantly benefit multi-step complex reasoning tasks such as spatial reasoning. Mechanistic analysis further reveals that the accuracy gains are primarily driven by validated executable skills, whereas long-term memory mainly stabilizes reasoning pathways.



Keywords: GIScience, textual spatial reasoning, geographic question answering, self-evolving spatial skills, learning from failure
