# Simulating land-use change and carbon storage in the Zhengzhou metropolitan area using an intPLUS-InVEST-Stacking-SHAP framework

**Author Information**  
Jichao Ma<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Henan University  



## Abstract

Rapid urban expansion in the Zhengzhou metropolitan area has reshaped land-use patterns and intensified ecological pressure, yet its implications for regional carbon storage remain insufficiently understood. Here we integrate land-use, natural and socioeconomic data within an intPLUS-InVEST-Stacking-SHAP framework to quantify land-use change and carbon-storage dynamics from 2000 to 2020, project future trajectories to 2050 under multiple development scenarios, and identify the dominant drivers of carbon storage. From 2000 to 2020, the region experienced substantial cropland loss (4,260.25 km2), marked expansion of built-up land (3646.46 km2), and a modest increase in forest area. Over the same period, total carbon storage decreased by 75.13 × 106 t and showed a clear spatial gradient, with higher values in peripheral mountainous areas and lower values in the central plains. Carbon storage declined sharply in cropland but increased in forest land, partly offsetting regional losses. By 2050, carbon storage is projected to decrease under all scenarios, although the magnitude of decline varies: the cropland-protection scenario yields the smallest overall loss, whereas the ecological-protection scenario most effectively enhances forest carbon sequestration. Natural geographic factors consistently dominate carbon-storage variation, and slope emerges as the leading driver after 2010, a pattern that is projected to persist to 2050. These results clarify how alternative land-use pathways may reshape metropolitan carbon-storage capacity and provide a transferable framework for assessing carbon consequences of land-use change in rapidly urbanizing regions.
