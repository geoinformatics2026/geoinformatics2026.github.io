# COVID Impacts on Community Access to Formal Long-term Care and Associated Disparities

**Author Information**  
Yiming Zhang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee  



## Abstract

Studies have shown higher infection and death rates during the COVID-19 pandemic in long-term care (LTC) facilities, especially in residential facilities. However, limited research has examined the COVID-19 impacts on the spatial accessibility of LTC facilities and how the impacts differ across various types of LTC services. To address this gap, this study leveraged seven types of LTC facilities, including residential and non-residential types in Wisconsin, and analyzed the census-tract-level spatial accessibility from 2006 to 2023. Utilizing an interrupted time series design, we analyzed the pandemic's immediate and sustained effects on accessibility. The result revealed a significant immediate negative impact on accessibility in rural areas, while urban residential facility accessibility showed resilience during the initial outbreak in 2020. However, the long-term trajectory indicates a sustained decline across nearly all facility types. The regression result showed that the pandemic exacerbated the preexisting disparities in accessibility, particularly in rural areas with higher percentages of racial minority groups and residents without vehicles. These findings underscore the critical need for targeted policies to ensure equitable access to LTC during future public health emergencies.



Keywords: Long-term care facilities, Residential and non-residential, Spatial accessibility, COVID-19, Urban-rural disparity
