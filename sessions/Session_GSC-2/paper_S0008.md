# Scale matters: spatial resolutions reshape mangrove landscape structure and its drivers in China

**Author Information**  
Hanwen Zhang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

High-resolution mangrove mapping captures rich spatial details of highly fragmented mangrove landscapes, yet the effects of spatial scaling on landscape structure remain insufficiently evaluated. This limitation introduces uncertainty in large-scale mangrove monitoring and may bias ecological inference. To address this gap, we developed a framework to quantify scale-dependent MLS distortion using China’s sub-meter mangrove dataset, and evaluated changes in area (ΔTA), patch number (ΔNP), fractal dimension (ΔFD), and nearest-neighbor distance (ΔENN) across resolutions from sub-meter to 30 m. Mangrove area remained relatively stable at intermediate scales (3–8 m), with ΔTA fluctuating around zero, but variability increased markedly with pixel size (standard deviation >20% near 30 m). In contrast, structural metrics showed strong and systematic distortion: ΔNP declined to around−50% and ΔENN increased by over 1100% at 30 m, while ΔFD decreased by 8–14%. MLS distortion was highly spatially heterogeneous, with extreme area overestimation in Guangdong and 40% loss of patch count in Zhejiang at 30 m. Explainable machine learning models showed that scale effects arise from coupled landscape structure and environmental context. Fragmentation and isolation (ΔNP and ΔENN) dominated model predictions, while environmental factors, including hydrology, urbanization, and population density, further modulated MLS distortion. Notably, stable mangrove area did not imply structural consistency. Overall, this study provides new insights into how mangrove spatial patterns are reshaped across resolutions. The proposed framework enables more consistent cross-scale interpretation of mangrove data and supports more reliable monitoring, assessment, and management of coastal ecosystems.
