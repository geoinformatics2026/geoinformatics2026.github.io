# Advancing Urban Sprawl Quantification Through an Attention-Weighted Deep Neural Framework and Multidimensional Spatial Indicator Integration

**Author Information**  
Farasath Hasan<sup>1</sup>, Xintao Liu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Hong Kong Polytechnic University  



## Abstract

Urban sprawl has become one of the most pressing issues in recent years, along with high-density developments (Balandi, 2023). Rapidly growing urban areas have raised several issues, including environmental degradation, fragmented land development, etc. However, although quantifying urban sprawl has become a focus in recent research, several persistent challenges remain. Many of the conventional indices used in urban sprawl analysis primarily rely on predefined indicators or hard-coded values, which cannot be used in diverse geographic landscapes. Also, most used urban sprawl indices focus on the large study areas and derive a single value for the whole study area, and do not focus on the micro pixel-level deviations. Also, the majority of indices only employ a single year for the formation of sprawl indices, which does not represent a sprawl occurring over a time period. Therefore, we propose a novel method for quantifying the urban sprawl using an attention-enhanced deep neural framework. This framework was integrated with multiple spatial indicators such as land use efficiency, growth magnitude, growth speed, morphological complexity, spatial dispersion, and land use mix. These indicators are used to capture the structural, functional, and temporal changes in urban change. We propose a novel deep neural architecture that can learn the hierarchical feature representations directly from the data and derive more advanced sprawl values. The attention-weighting method has been used for the dynamic estimation of the feature importance of each indicator. It also helps in identifying the most accurate data-driven weights rather than fixed, subjective weights. The proposed deep neural network changes the weighted indicators into latent representations in the analysis, and this process learns the complex interaction patterns in diverse study areas. Thus, the proposed methodology can be used for comparative studies on how urban sprawl is changing worldwide.



Keywords: Urban sprawl,  Data-driven weights,  Attention-enhanced deep neural framework,  Pixel-level deviations,  Hierarchical feature representation
