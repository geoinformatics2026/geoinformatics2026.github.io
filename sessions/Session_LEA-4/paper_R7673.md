# Remote-Sensing Indicators of Dryland Stress in Punjab, Pakistan: Trends in Vegetation Greenness, Productivity, Moisture Constraint and Degradation Severity

**Author Information**  
Junjun Wu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Aerospace Information Research lnstitute,Chinese Academy of Sciences  



## Abstract

Land sustainability is a pressing global issue, as desertification and soil degradation have reduced land productivity by up to 50% in some regions. This study uses remote sensing data to investigate the causes of land degradation in Punjab, Pakistan, from 2001 to 2024, applying two methods. It first analyzes UNCCD‑recommended indicators: NDVI, Soil Organic Carbon (SOC), Land Use, and Net Primary Productivity (NPP). The Land Change Modeler (LCM) assesses land cover changes, while Moisture‑normalized NPP (MNPP) is derived from MODIS data to account for water availability. Results show severe fertility decline and low SOC in southern Punjab. From 2001 to 2024, built‑up areas increased by 3.04%, while grassland, tree cover, and sparse vegetation decreased by 2.9%. MNPP declined significantly over the period, with sharp drops in southern and central Punjab. Non‑degraded land decreased by 1,235 km², and very high degradation increased by 160%. Northern districts remained productive due to stable irrigation. Both Weighted Overlay and MNPP methods link degradation to low SOC and climatic stress. Long‑term monitoring is essential to support anti‑desertification policies and ensure food security.
