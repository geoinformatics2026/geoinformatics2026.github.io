# The Impact of Ground-level Ozone Pollution on Subjective Well-being at different levels-An Empirical Study in China.

**Author Information**  
Yingying Mei<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Hubei University  



## Abstract

Exploring the impact of ground-level ozone pollution on individuals' subjective well-being (SWB) in China not only supplement the theories of environmental pollution and public health, but also provides a basis for precise environmental health risk management. A partial proportional odds model is innovatively introduced to explore the differential impacts of ground-level ozone pollution and relevant sociodemographic factors on varying levels of SWB. The model relaxes the parallel lines assumption, enabling effective handling of variable combinations that simultaneously satisfy and violate this assumption, thereby improving precision in identifying differential effects of various variables across SWB levels. Main results: 1) Ground-level ozone pollution exhibits significant heterogeneity in its impact on SWB across different levels, with increased pollution significantly reducing the probability of individuals reporting higher SWB levels. Notably, individuals with greater baseline SWB levels demonstrate heightened sensitivity to ozone pollution. 2) Females, individuals with higher self-rated annual household income, those reporting better health status, and party members display a greater likelihood of reporting positive SWB levels (e.g., “happy” and “very happy”). 3) Advanced age significantly increases the probability of individuals reporting a “very happy” SWB level. Having an education level above high school and having a spouse significantly reduce the probabilities of individuals reporting “very unhappy,” “unhappy,” and “neutral” SWB levels while enhancing their probability of reporting a “happy” level. The results provide important evidence for promoting coordinated improvements in environmental governance and well-being. Future research will explore the moderating mechanisms associated pollution exposure levels and individual protective behavious, guiding targeted environmental health interventions.
