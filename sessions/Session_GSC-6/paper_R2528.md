# Using mHealth to understand momentary mechanism of physical activity behaviors of US Pacific Islanders

**Author Information**  
Neng Wan<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Utah  



## Abstract

Pacific Islanders represent one of the fastest growing racial/ethnic groups in the United States. Pacific Islanders suffer from disparities in a variety of health problems such as obesity, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and cancer at several sites. Although physical activity is an effective way to reduce the risks of these health problems, there is very limited knowledge on the PA behaviors of Pacific Islanders. This abstract describes a study that utilizes mobile-health (mHealth) technologies to promote PA behaviors of Tongan Americans (TAs). Specifically, this is a longitudinal study to examine the influence of neighborhood factors on PA adoption and maintenance among 150 sedentary TA adults. The study aims to investigate how neighborhood factors (e.g., access to PA resources, safety, greenspace) and psychosocial factors (e.g., self-efficacy, positive/negative affect) interactively influence PA behaviors. Participants are assessed using real-time, state of the art methodologies consisting of PA sensors, ecological momentary assessment, and GPS. This system tracks behavioral and physiologic data in real-time and can objectively detect PA behaviors and negative affect/stress of participants. GPS permits real-time mapping of an individual’s space-time trajectories and relevant neighborhood exposures/characteristics (e.g., proximity to PA facilities; neighborhood safety) using geographic data. It is expected that the findings of this study will help improve PA initiation and maintenance among US Pacific Islanders in future intervention works.



Keywords: mobile health (mHealth),  physical activity,  neighborhood effects,  Pacific Islanders
