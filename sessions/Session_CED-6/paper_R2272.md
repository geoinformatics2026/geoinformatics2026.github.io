# FloodLang and FloodLoc-Four: A Bilingual Dataset and Fine-Grained Multilevel Framework for Flood-Related Geospatial Information Understanding

**Author Information**  
Wenying Du<sup>1</sup>, Changyan Xie<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> China University of Geosciences (Wuhan)  



## Abstract

Social media offers vital near real-time data for flood response, yet extracting accurate geospatial information from unstructured texts remains a significant challenge. The emergence of large language models (LLMs) presents new opportunities to address this task. This study systematically evaluates the spatial reasoning capabilities of LLMs within disaster contexts. We introduce FloodLoc-Four, a hierarchical framework comprising four tasks: disaster-related information validity classification, location span extraction, spatial semantic classification, and standardized address generation. Additionally, a bilingual benchmark dataset, FloodLang, was developed covering Chinese and English flood social media texts. Experiments indicate that Gemini 2.5-Pro and GPT-5 achieve the best overall performance, showing clear advantages in English across all tasks, particularly in location span extraction and standardized address generation. In contrast, the DeepSeek series exhibits more balanced performance between Chinese and English, with relatively small cross-lingual gaps across task levels. While Qwen and Llama exhibit limited cross-lingual stability. These results delineate the practical capability boundaries of current LLMs and offer guidance for selecting models in multilingual flood emergency applications.
