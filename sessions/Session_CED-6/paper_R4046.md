# Multi-Agent Collaboration-Driven Dynamic Assessment of Flood Risk

**Author Information**  
Xin Xiao<sup>1</sup>, Hui Lin<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Jiangxi Normal University  



## Abstract

The evolution of flood risk exhibits significant spatiotemporal dynamics and systemic complexity, rendering traditional static assessment methods inadequate for the demands of fine-grained flood management decision-making. To address this limitation, this paper proposes a dynamic flood risk assessment method driven by multi-agent collaboration. This approach constructs agent models encompassing flood propagation and the responses of elements at risk, explicitly defining their attributes, behavioral rules, and collaborative interaction mechanisms. By integrating multi-source data—including hydrological, topographical, and exposure data—alongside Large Language Model (LLM) based multi-agent collaboration technology, this study dynamically simulates the entire flood lifecycle from occurrence and propagation to recession. Furthermore, it quantifies the threat levels posed by floods to exposed elements such as populations, buildings, and infrastructure in real time across multiple spatiotemporal scales. The results demonstrate that this method enables the dynamic identification, classification, and assessment of flood risk, thereby providing a robust scientific basis for flood mitigation decision-making and the dynamic allocation of emergency resources.
