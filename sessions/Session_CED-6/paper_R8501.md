# Equity-informed resilience of emergency medical services to urban flooding: Identifying access gaps for vulnerable older populations

**Author Information**  
Yuting Lu<sup>1</sup>, Lei Fang<sup>2</sup>, Ruishan Chen<sup>3</sup>, Shenjun Yao<sup>1</sup>

## Abstract

Population ageing is reshaping the demand for urban health services. Older adults are more vulnerable to acute health events due to physiological decline, limited mobility, and social isolation. Against this backdrop, the accessibility and equity of Emergency Medical Services (EMS) have become essential for resilient urban service provision, given their pivotal role in reducing morbidity and mortality in time-sensitive incidents. However, urban flooding presents a formidable challenge to EMS resilience by increasing emergency demand and compromising supply through network disruptions. This interplay poses a growing challenge for maintaining equitable and resilient EMS provision in cities. A growing body of research has assessed EMS accessibility and equity under flooding, often using spatial accessibility methods such as the two-step floating catchment area approach. However, most studies estimate EMS demand primarily by population size, overlooking the socio-health heterogeneity for older adults. In practice, EMS needs depend on living arrangements, health burden, and social support. These disparities can be amplified by flood-induced mobility restrictions and the absence of immediate assistance. Critically, how alternative demand representations influence the identification of EMS access gaps and equity patterns under flood-induced disruption scenarios remains under-investigated. To address the research gap, this study develops an equity-informed resilience assessment of EMS under urban flooding, using Shanghai as a case study. We quantify and map flood-induced EMS access gaps for vulnerable older populations by integrating flood scenarios with network-based travel impedance and 2SFCA-based spatial accessibility modelling. We examine flood-induced EMS access gaps for vulnerable older populations under baseline and representative flood disruption scenarios, and assess how gap identification varies with alternative vulnerability-informed demand representations. We then discuss implications for equity-informed resilience planning. The proposed framework provides an actionable and transferable basis for identifying equity-relevant EMS access gaps under flood disruption and informing resilience-oriented planning in ageing cities.
