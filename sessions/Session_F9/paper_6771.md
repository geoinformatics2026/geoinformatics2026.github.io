# Sampling the City: Revisiting the Neighborhood Effect Averaging Problem (NEAP)  Through the Interplay Between Mobility and Socio-spatial Contexts

**Author Information**  
Yuhan Cui<sup>1</sup>, Mei-Po Kwan<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> CHINESE UNIVERSITY OF HONG KONG  



## Abstract

The neighborhood effect averaging problem (NEAP) describes mobility-based measurements (MBM) of environmental exposure converging towards a regional average versus residence-based measurements (RBM). While widely documented, the underlying statistical mechanisms remain unclear. We propose a novel framework conceptualizing MBM as repeated micro-environmental samples through markov process whose means converge per the Mean Ergodic Theorem (MET) and whose distributions approach normality per the Lindeberg-Feller Central Limit Theorem (LCLT), with real-world spatiotemporal autocorrelation and heterogeneity modulating this convergence. Analyzing trajectories of 732 Hong Kong residents across nine urban services using multilevel mixed-effects models, we examined MBM central tendency (coefficient of variation) and normality (Shapiro-Wilk W) as functions of mobility patterns, service distribution, and initial RBM distributions. The results confirm that greater exposure diversity, driven by higher mobility entropy and dispersed time-available services, intensifies NEAP, supporting our MET-LCLT mechanism. Crucially, a three-way interaction emerged: clustered services constrain mobility’s averaging effect, perpetuating residential disparities, whereas well-distributed time-available services enable mobility to overcome segregation barriers. These findings demonstrate that the NEAP arises from the interplay of individual movement, social (segregation) context, and spatial (environmental factor distribution) contexts, highlighting exposure diversity as the core mechanism and providing crucial insights for both mobility-based research and equity-oriented urban planning.
