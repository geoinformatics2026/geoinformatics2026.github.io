# Assessing Personal Exposure to Waste Burning Using Passive Sampling and Geospatial Mixed Methods

**Author Information**  
Yan Lin<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Penn State Department of Geography  



## Abstract

Unregulated solid waste disposal (USWD) and waste burning remain ongoing environmental health concerns in many rural and Native American communities in the United States, largely due to long-standing gaps in waste management infrastructure. However, the specific chemicals people are exposed to and how exposures vary within communities remain poorly understood. This study uses a community-engaged mixed method approach to examine individual exposure to chemicals potentially associated with waste burning in Indigenous communities in the United States. Silicone wristband passive samplers were used to measure personal exposure. The wristband data were combined with geospatial modeling, activity logs, surveys, and interviews to examine whether chemicals consistent with waste burning exposure to explain variation in individual exposure patterns. The study found multiple potentially hazardous chemicals on wristbands, several of which are commonly associated with waste burning at both individual and community level. Results from this study suggest that combining passive sampling with geospatial mixed methods can help characterize personal exposure patterns at both individual and community scales, demonstrating great needs for multi-level intervention on waste management in these communities.



Keywords: Unregulated solid waste disposal,  Passive sampling,  Individual exposure assessment,  Mixed method
