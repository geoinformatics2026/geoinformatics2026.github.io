# How does the livelihood vulnerability of rural households facing multiple risks affect their livelihood resilience?

**Author Information**  
Yue Sun<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Capital Normal University  



## Abstract

Although rural households in China may have officially exited poverty, many still confront weakened sustainable livelihood capacities due to compounded risk shocks, thus becoming vulnerable poverty-eradicated households. However, measuring the spatiotemporal heterogeneity in the effects of rural household livelihood vulnerability on resilience from a multi-risk perspective remains a challenge. In response, this study integrates the theoretical foundations of livelihood vulnerability and resilience to construct a comprehensive sustainable livelihood–vulnerability–resilience analytical framework for rural households under multi-risk disturbances. Based on this framework, a forward-looking Vulnerability as Expected Poverty (VEP) model embedding diverse risk factors as feature vectors is utilized to evaluate livelihood vulnerability, while a cloud model-based fuzzy comprehensive assessment approach is adopted to quantify livelihood resilience. After clarifying the correlation between vulnerability and resilience, quantile regression and a factor contribution model are further applied to reveal the spatiotemporal impact mechanisms through which multi-level and multi-risk dominated vulnerability on resilience. The case analysis of Fugong County in China reveals evident spatiotemporal heterogeneity in both livelihood vulnerability and resilience, with their negative relationship intensifying over time. As livelihood vulnerability levels increase, the key internal factors of livelihood resilience and their respective contribution adjust accordingly. Furthermore, variations in the dominant risk categories shaping vulnerability generate differentiated impacts on overall livelihood resilience and its internal dimensions. Notably, resilience fluctuates most significantly when vulnerability is primarily dominated by social risks, whereas the smallest changes occur when labor and income risks predominate. This study provides a viable methodological reference and technical basis for policy decisions aimed at consolidating poverty alleviation achievements and fostering sustainable livelihoods. It effectively enhances both anticipatory capacities and post-shock response abilities of vulnerable rural households confronting multiple risk disturbances.



Keywords: livelihood vulnerability,  livelihood resilience,  risk,  spatiotemporal impact,  sustainable development
