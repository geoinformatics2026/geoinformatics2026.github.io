# Three-Dimensional Shading Patterns and UV Exposure Risk in Plateau Cities Using Multi-Source Data

**Author Information**  
Shanshan Chen<sup>1</sup>, Yinjie Li<sup>1</sup>, Chun Li<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Yunnan University  



## Abstract

The formation of shading in urban three-dimensional landscapes is significant for mitigating ultraviolet (UV) exposure. Research on the UV radiation exposure risks and the spatial characteristics of shading resources in plateau cities remains relatively sparse. This study utilizes AI-based global forest mapping, building height data (3D-GloBFP), and mobile signaling data to analyze the UV exposure risk of populations and the spatial characteristics of urban shading in the typical plateau city of Kunming. The results indicate that: (1) There are significant spatial differences in shading coverage and UV exposure risk in the main urban area of Kunming. Wuhua District and Panlong District have better shading conditions and lower exposure risks, whereas Guandu District and Chenggong District experience insufficient shading and higher risks; (2) Both trees and buildings serve as important sources of shading, with trees being the dominant contributor; (3) High-priority regulatory areas are mainly concentrated at traffic intersections. The findings reveal the spatial relationship between urban shading resources and population UV exposure risk from a supply-demand perspective, providing a scientific basis for regulating UV risks in urban settings.
