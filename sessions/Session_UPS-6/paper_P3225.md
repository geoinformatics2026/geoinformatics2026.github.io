# Research on the Spatial Patterns of Global Major Cities

**Author Information**  
Anjie Sheng<sup>1</sup>, Shengyuan Jing<sup>1</sup>, Zhiwu Zhou<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National Geomatics Center of China  



## Abstract

Centered on the urban concentric zone theory, this study integrates global mapping data, multidimensional statistical data from the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), GHSL human settlement layer data, and economic and social statistical information. It constructs a comprehensive evaluation index system encompassing the spatial form index, polycentric index, network index, and spatial intensive utilization index to systematically analyze the core characteristics, typological differentiation, and evolutionary mechanisms of the territorial spatial patterns of global major cities. The study clarifies the spatial division criteria and identification features for suburbanized cities, satellite cities, strong central cities, and medium-sized single-center cities. It reveals the intrinsic coupling relationships between polycentric layout patterns, transportation network connectivity, and spatial intensive utilization efficiency. The results indicate that global urban spatial patterns present significant regional differences; geographical conditions profoundly influence spatial form, while transportation accessibility and polycentric development promote each other, with European cities performing prominently in spatial intensive utilization. This study aims to provide theoretical support and a practical paradigm for optimizing China's territorial spatial development and protection patterns and promoting the high-quality construction of world- class cities.
