# How can the urban landscape affect block-scale urban renewal potential? Insights from large language models and explainable machine learning

**Author Information**  
Yukun Jiang<sup>1</sup>, Qingsong He<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Huazhong University of Science and Technology  



## Abstract

Understanding how urban landscape influences urban renewal potential (URP) is critical for accurately identifying renewal demands and enhancing planning decision-making. However, precisely quantifying and systematically decoding their association remains challenging due to the multifactorial nature of URP, coupled with the physical complexity and latent perceptual attributes of landscape elements. To address this gap, this study constructs a six-dimensional URP evaluation framework, employing Large Language Models (LLMs) to parse real-world renewal samples and objectively anchor indicator weights, thereby achieving a precise assessment of block-scale URP. Building on this, we integrate machine learning models with SHAP-based interpretability techniques to uncover the nonlinear driving mechanisms and threshold effects underlying the landscape–URP relationship. Results indicate that block-scale URP in Wuhan exhibits a typical core-periphery pattern, with high-potential blocks predominantly clustered in central waterfront areas. These high-potential neighborhoods cluster into three types: comprehensive improvement, high economic return, and perceptual enhancement. The comprehensive improvement type accounts for the largest share (37.03%), while the high economic return type exhibits the highest mean potential score (0.55). Interpretable machine learning further shows that building scale exerts the strongest influence on URP, followed by three-dimensional building volume and public transit accessibility. Notably, the dominant landscape drivers, threshold effects, and bivariate synergies vary markedly across neighborhood types. These findings offer valuable insights for formulating type-specific renewal strategies at the neighborhood scale.
