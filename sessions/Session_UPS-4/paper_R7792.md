# Assessment of Urban Vibrancy Resilience Using Mobility Big Data and Spatially Varying Modeling

**Author Information**  
Meixu Chen<sup>1</sup>, Yunzhe Liu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> East China University of Science and Technology  

<sup>2</sup> East China Normal University  



## Abstract

Urban resilience research increasingly requires spatially explicit and computationally robust approaches capable of capturing dynamic adaptation under prolonged disruptions. This study develops a reproducible geospatial analytical framework to quantify Urban Vibrancy Resilience (UVR) and evaluate its spatially heterogeneous association with multidimensional socioeconomic deprivation in New York City. We integrate 52 months (2019–2023) of large-scale For-Hire Vehicle mobility data to construct longitudinal vibrancy trajectories for 234 taxi zones. After seasonal-trend decomposition, four resilience dimensions-impact range, impact pace, recovery pace, and rebound ratio-are derived to characterise disturbance intensity and adaptive recovery. These indicators are synthesised into a composite Urban Vibrancy Resilience Index (UVRI) using Principal Component Analysis, which enables dimensionality reduction while preserving the dominant variance structure. Socioeconomic deprivation is operationalised through a spatially harmonised Carstairs Index based on census-derived indicators of unemployment, poverty, overcrowding, and car ownership. To detect spatial dependence, global Moran’s I and Local Indicators of Spatial Association are employed, revealing significant clustering of resilience hot spots and cold spots. To address spatial non-stationarity, Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR) is used to estimate location-specific relationships between deprivation and UVR while controlling for demographic composition, land-use structure, and accessibility metrics. Results demonstrate strong spatial heterogeneity in vibrancy resilience. High-resilience clusters concentrate in central economic cores, whereas persistent low-resilience zones align with historically deprived neighbourhoods. A statistically significant and spatially varying negative association between socioeconomic deprivation and UVR is identified, with stronger localised effects in outer-borough districts. By integrating mobility big data analytics, spatial decomposition, and local regression modelling within a unified framework, this study advances geo-innovative methodologies for diagnosing urban resilience disparities. The fully open-data workflow is transferable across metropolitan contexts, providing a scalable geospatial decision-support tool for sustainable and resilient urban governance.



Keywords: Urban vibrancy resilience,  Mobility big data,  Geospatial analytics,  Spatial heterogeneity,  Socioeconomic deprivation
