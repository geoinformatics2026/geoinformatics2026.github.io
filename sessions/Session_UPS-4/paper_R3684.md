# Resilience Dynamics of the Service Industry under Prolonged COVID-19 Disruptions in Shanghai, China: A POI-based Analysis

**Author Information**  
Yiming Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Changshan Wu<sup>1</sup>, Zi Yin<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee  

<sup>2</sup> Southwest Jiaotong University  



## Abstract

To control the spread of COVID-19, the Chinese government maintained extensive control and prevention measures until early 2023. Due to its close-contact nature, the service industry was severely impacted by these measures. However, most studies focus on a single sector in the first two years of the pandemic since 2020; limited research has comprehensively examined the prolonged COVID-19 disruptions and cross-sector effects on the resilience of the service industry. To address these gaps, this study utilized a city-level resilience framework to assess how service industry resilience evolved during prolonged COVID-19 disruptions by examining business stability and vitality, using category-based Point of Interest (POI) data from Dianping. Business stability measures structural resilience, while vitality represents consumer activities. The result reveals a significant spatial disparity in business resilience across the service industry, with downtown areas showing greater stability while the periphery exhibits greater vitality. Catering and Shopping sectors play dominant roles in affecting business stability, whether individually or cross-sectorally. Business vitality experienced a greater recovery in Catering and Entertainment & Recreation after the complete reopening in 2023, while Shopping and Accommodation recovered more slowly, likely due to shifting tourism patterns and sustained online shopping habits.



Keywords: Business resilience,  Service industry system,  Spatial heterogeneity,  Urban post-COVID recovery
