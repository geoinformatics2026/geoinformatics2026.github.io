# Identification, influencing factors, and governance strategies of rural shrinkage in highly urbanized areas— A case study of Suzhou City

**Author Information**  
Yice Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Yasi Tian<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Soochow University  



## Abstract

In highly urbanized regions, overall urban growth coexists with localized rural shrinkage, leading to one-way resource flows to cities and undermining coordinated and sustainable regional development. Existing research remains constrained by limited identification accuracy, reliance on survey-based data with restricted spatial coverage, and insufficient understanding of shrinkage mechanisms in economically advanced contexts. Taking Suzhou as a case study, this study integrates population census data with a stacking ensemble learning approach to estimate village-level population changes and examine the spatial patterns of rural shrinkage from 2010 to 2020. From a multidimensional perspective, change-based indicators are systematically incorporated to construct a dynamic influencing-factor system. A Random Forest–SHAP model is applied to reveal the dominant mechanisms and synergistic effects of rural shrinkage in a highly urbanized context. Based on key influencing-factor characteristics, K-means clustering is employed to classify types of shrinking villages and propose differentiated governance strategies. The findings reveal that: (1) Suzhou exhibits a multi-core, contiguous pattern of rural shrinkage; (2) rural population change is primarily driven by external structural forces, with industry, economy, and infrastructure jointly enhancing population attraction, though diminishing marginal effects warrant attention; county-level towns exert far stronger spillover effects on rural development than the prefecture-level urban core; and improvements in transportation infrastructure exhibit threshold effects and pronounced spatial heterogeneity in their impacts on population stability; (3) Suzhou’s rural areas can be classified into four types—marginal decline, low-momentum stability, integrated upgrading, and location-advantaged—and differentiated governance strategies are proposed for each type of shrinking village within the framework of smart shrinkage. This study enriches the conceptual scope and methodological framework of rural shrinkage research and provides practical insights for the governance of shrinking villages in highly urbanized regions.
