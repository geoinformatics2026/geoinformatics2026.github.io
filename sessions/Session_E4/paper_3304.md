# Examining Urban-Rural Disparities of Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias and Potential Risk Factors in Texas

**Author Information**  
Yongmei Lu<sup>1</sup>, Victor Quarshie<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Texas State University  



## Abstract

There are an estimated 400,000 residents aged 65+ in Texas who are living with Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Dementia (ADRD). Accordingly, caring for these ADRD patients costs Texas over $33 billion annually. Texas State Plan for Alzheimer’s Disease 2024-2028 listed Risk Reduction as the 2nd priority area, calling for more research into understanding and managing the risk factors for ADRD. Existing studies on urban-rural disparities of ADRD are inconclusive and vary based on geographic regions and populations. The few empirical analyses on ADRD risk factors for Texans focused on lifestyle and behavior factors, and there is a lack of comparison between urban and rural areas. This paper will report on our exploratory study analyzing urban-rural disparities of ADRD in Texas. Preliminary findings on the association between ARDR prevalence with certain environmental factors and healthcare accessibility will be examined and the difference for urban and rural populations discussed.



Keywords: Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementia,  Health Disparity,  Risk Factors,  Texas
