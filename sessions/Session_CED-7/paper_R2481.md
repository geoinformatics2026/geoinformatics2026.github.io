# A Combined Method of Driving Factor Analysis and Machine Learning for Early Warning of Moroccan Locust Outbreaks Using Remotely Sensed Data

**Author Information**  
Bo Sun<sup>1</sup>, Shujie Wei<sup>1</sup>, Jing Qian<sup>1</sup>, Qiming Zhou<sup>2</sup>, Shuhong Peng<sup>3</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences  

<sup>2</sup> College of Surveying and Geo-informatics, Tongji University  

<sup>3</sup> School of Computer Science and Engineering, Huizhou University  



## Abstract

Climate change has intensified locust outbreaks and cross-border invasions in Central Asia. Traditional locust plague monitoring system, largely reliant on manual field reports from observers or farmers, is time-consuming and labor-intensive. Remote sensing has become a powerful tool for large-scale locust surveillance, such as fine-scale habitat mapping and migration forecasting and locust outbreak risk analysis. However, due to significant interspecific and regional differences in locust ecology, we still lack a knowledge of the dominant drivers of outbreaks in Tajikistan. Focusing on Moroccan locust (Dociostaurus Maroccanus) in southern Tajikistan, this study proposes a monitoring and early-warning framework that combines remote sensing-derived environmental variables with machine learning techniques. The framework comprises two components: (1) identification of key driving factors of locust habitats, and (2) spatial prediction of high-risk outbreak areas. Leveraging historical locust plague records and ground observations in the recent decade, we first analyze the multidimensional environmental factors of locust habitat suitability, including topography, soil texture, moisture, vegetation condition, land surface temperature, precipitation. Subsequently, machine learning algorithms, including Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Isolated Forest (IF), are applied to delineate potential habitat areas where adult locust laid eggs in the preceding year. Building on these results, we develop a predictive outbreak model by integrating a biological accumulated temperature model. Specifically, cumulative growing degree days above 0°C combined with a predefined thermal threshold and remotely sensed surface temperature data are used to dynamically forecast the spatio-temporal distribution of locust outbreaks. Results demonstrate that the combination of driving factor analysis and machine learning achieves approximately 80% accuracy in predicting high-risk locust habitat. By integrating machine learning model with biological model, this study offers an operative solution for detecting locust habitats and dynamic forecasting of locust outbreaks, providing critical decision for locust plague early-warning in the arid and vulnerable regions of Central Asia.
