# Leveraging Seismic Signals for Sensing Urban Human Activity and Transportation

**Author Information**  
Heng Cai<sup>1</sup>, Hao Tian<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Texas A&M University  



## Abstract

Human activities in urban environments generate ground vibrations that are continuously recorded by seismic sensors. High-frequency ambient seismic signals provide a passive and continuous record of collective human activity that is independent of traditional communication infrastructure. With near-real-time availability and long-term historical coverage, seismic observations represent a resilient and complementary data source for monitoring urban dynamics during disruptive events. Transportation dynamics are another direct proxy of collective human activity. Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) technology expands this capability by converting existing underground fiber-optic cables into dense arrays of virtual seismic sensors, enabling cost-effective, continuous, and privacy-preserving traffic monitoring.  This presentation demonstrates the feasibility of seismic sensing as an emerging urban sensing modality through two case studies. The first study examines Winter Storm Uri in the Dallas–Fort Worth metropolitan area, Texas. By analyzing high-frequency seismic signals from 13 stations, we quantify human-induced seismic noise using spectral and displacement metrics and validate the results using multi-source datasets. The seismic signals capture key spatiotemporal patterns of human mobility during the disaster, including diurnal activity cycles, abrupt activity disruption, and gradual post-event recovery.  The second study develops a deep-learning framework to extract traffic dynamics from DAS data collected along urban fiber-optic infrastructure in College Station, Texas. Raw strain-rate waveforms are converted into spatiotemporal DAS images for vehicle detection and speed estimation. A three-stage training strategy combining synthetic and real data is used to address limited labeled datasets. Detection models including YOLOv8, YOLOv11, and RT-DETR are evaluated, and the outputs are aggregated to derive traffic flow, density, and congestion indicators. Results show significant improvements in detection accuracy and computational efficiency, achieving faster processing compared with classical slant-stacking methods. The studies illustrate seismic sensing combined with AI methods, can transform seismic infrastructure into a scalable, privacy-preserving urban sensing system for monitoring human activity and transportation dynamics.



Keywords: human dynamics,  urban sensing,  deep learning
