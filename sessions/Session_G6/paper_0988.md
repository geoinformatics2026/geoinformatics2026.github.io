# Hierarchical Decision Mechanisms and Behavioral Heterogeneity in Urban Cycling Route Choice

**Author Information**  
Xinyue Ma<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Department of Urban Planning, School of Architecture, Harbin Institute of Technology Shenzhen, Shenzhen, China  



## Abstract

Despite growing recognition of cycling as a sustainable urban transport mode, the multilevel cognitive processes underlying cyclists' route decisions and the heterogeneity of individual decision styles remain insufficiently understood. Existing approaches typically examine isolated factors or rely on sociodemographic segmentation, failing to capture the hierarchical nature of route choice or uncover behaviorally grounded cyclist typologies. This study addresses these gaps by proposing a global–local–action hierarchical decision framework and an integrated AIRL–SHAP analytical pipeline. Using shared-bike GPS trajectories from Bantian, Shenzhen, combined with road network attributes and street-view-derived visual perception indices, the framework inversely recovers latent reward functions from observed cycling behavior and decomposes feature contributions across decision layers. Results reveal a dual-pillar structure: global path planning factors (left-turn avoidance and travel distance) establish feasibility constraints, while local environmental attributes (road hierarchy, vegetation coverage, and building density) refine experiential quality. Action-layer features (intersection turning feasibility) contribute less than 0.1%, confirming their role as physical constraints rather than preference signals. Building on trajectory-level SHAP attributions, a dynamic attribution–clustering paradigm is introduced to derive "decision fingerprints" for each trip and identify four cyclist profiles through unsupervised clustering: Global Planners (29.9%), Local Perceivers (8.9%), Balanced Opportunists (51.9%), and Dynamic Adapters (9.3%). These findings offer a theoretically grounded and empirically validated foundation for targeted cycling infrastructure investment and personalized navigation design in high-density urban contexts.
