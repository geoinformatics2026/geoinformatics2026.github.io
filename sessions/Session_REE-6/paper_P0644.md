# Fine-Scale Estimation of Chlorophyll and Carotenoid Content in Norway Spruce Using UAV Hyperspectral Remote Sensing Data and a PROSAIL–Random Forest Hybrid Approach

**Author Information**  
Adenan Yandra Nofrizal<sup>1</sup>, Lucie Cervena<sup>1</sup>, Jakub Lysák<sup>1</sup>, Zuzana Lhotáková<sup>2</sup>, Eva Neuwirthová<sup>2</sup>, Albrechtová Jana<sup>2</sup>, Kupková Lucie<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Department of Applied Geoinformatics and Cartography, Faculty of Science, Charles University  

<sup>2</sup> Department of Experimental Plant Biology, Faculty of Science, Charles University  



## Abstract

Monitoring leaf biochemical traits such as chlorophyll (chl) and carotenoid (car) content is essential for assessing plant health and detecting early physiological stress. Traditional pigment determination methods are destructive and limited in spatial coverage, restricting their application at stand and landscape scales. This study evaluates a hybrid retrieval framework that combines the PROSAIL radiative transfer model (RTM) with a Random Forest (RF) machine learning algorithm to estimate chlorophyll and carotenoid content of Norway spruce (Picea abies (L.) Karst) needles using high-resolution UAV-based hyperspectral imagery. Hyperspectral data (400-1000 nm, 3 cm spatial resolution) were acquired using a Headwall Nano- Hyperspec sensor mounted on a DJI Matrice 600 Pro platform in a homogeneous Norway spruce stand in Krkonoae National Park, Czechia. The hybrid approach demonstrated strong predictive performance, achieving R^2 of 0.82 for chlorophyll and 0.79 for carotenoids in Tet. High-resolution spatial maps revealed distinct within-canopy variability, with mean chlorophyll and carotenoid concentrations of $33.92~\mu g/cm^{2}$ and $5.92~\mu g/cm^{2}$ for Tet Boudy and $29.46~\mu g/cm^{2}$ and 4.43 $\mu g/cm^{2}$ for Rejdi, respectively. These results demonstrate that the integration of RTM simulations with machine learning and UAV hyperspectral data provides a robust, non-destructive method for individual-tree level biochemical trait retrieval.
