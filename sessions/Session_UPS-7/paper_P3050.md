# Research on the Spatial Pattern of Industrial Land and Its Influencing Factors - A Case Study of the East African Community and Zambia

**Author Information**  
Shengyuan Jing<sup>1</sup>, Hui Zhao<sup>1</sup>, Yihang Xiao<sup>2</sup>, Anjie Sheng<sup>1</sup>, Zhuolin Wu<sup>2</sup>, Zijin Li<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National Geomatics Center of China  

<sup>2</sup> School of Economics and Trade, Hunan University  



## Abstract

Industrial land is the core carrier for industrialization and urbanization in Global South countries. Taking East African Community (EAC) member states and Zambia as research objects, this study integrates multisource geospatial data, and adopts GIS spatial technologies and public opinion quantitative analysis to explore the spatial pattern of industrial land and its influencing factors. The results show: (1) Industrial land presents a "core-agglomeration, periphery-scattering" pattern, centered on Lake Victoria and extending along railway trunk lines, with Tanzania, Uganda and Kenya forming a "core triangle"; (2) It is highly correlated with urbanization and high-density built-up areas, and Chinese industrial parks have a significant radiation effect on the expansion of surrounding built-up areas, especially in Kenya and Zambia during 2010-2020; (3) Transportation accessibility, mineral resource endowment and cooperative public opinion environment are core influencing factors. This study provides a scientific basis for optimizing regional land use and promoting intensive land use.
