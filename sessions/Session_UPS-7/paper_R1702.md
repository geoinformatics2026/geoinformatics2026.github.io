# Geospatial Insights into Urban Resilience: A Systematic Review of Urban Resilience Against Extreme Weather Events

**Author Information**  
Jingting Huang<sup>1</sup>, Qingyang Yu<sup>1</sup>, Linyan Li<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> City University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

The escalating frequency of extreme weather events, combined with rapid urbanization, poses profound challenges for cities worldwide. Although urban resilience has become a pivotal framework for climate adaptation, the spatial distribution of research focus and empirical evidence remains inadequately explored. This study undertakes a systematic review of 265 peer-reviewed articles from 63 countries to assess the current state of urban resilience evaluation and enhancement methodologies, with a focus on geospatial patterns and context-specific adaptation strategies. Adhering to PRISMA guidelines, the analysis uncovers notable spatial disparities: Asia (35.32%), Europe (28.25%), and North America (18.21%) predominate in literature, whereas Africa (10.04%) and South America (2.23%) are markedly underrepresented despite their elevated climate vulnerabilities. Thematic examination delineates six resilience dimensions: infrastructure, institutional, community-based, spatial planning, economic, and general, with community-based (31.67%) and infrastructure resilience (26.18%) garnering the greatest emphasis, in contrast to the relative neglect of spatial planning (13.71%) and economic dimensions (2.99%). Methodologically, 53.03% of studies prioritize assessment frameworks, while only 32.20% advance actionable enhancement strategies. Drawing on this synthesis, we propose a geospatially informed framework for urban resilience assessment and augmentation, alongside differentiated adaptation strategies tailored to varied urban contexts, including coastal cities, high-density megacities, rapidly urbanizing areas, historical heritage sites, and resource-limited regions. This review underscores critical gaps in geographic research and illustrates how geoinformatics can facilitate the creation of equitable, contextually attuned, and spatially explicit resilience frameworks vital to sustainable urban development amid intensifying climate risks.
