# Toward quantum geospatial science: What if geospatial attributes and coordinates are quantum entangled?

**Author Information**  
Xiao Huang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Emory University  



## Abstract

Spatial modeling traditionally treats geographic coordinates and attributes as separable inputs, introducing spatial dependence through external smoothing, weighting, or graph-based mechanisms. This paper proposes an alternative representational perspective grounded in quantum theory: what if spatial coordinates and attributes are quantum entangled? Building on the mathematical formalism of Hilbert spaces, superposition, and entanglement, this work conceptualizes coordinate-attribute coupling as structural inseparability rather than as post hoc spatial adjustment. This idea was evaluated using fidelity-based quantum kernels with systematically varied entanglement topologies. In a controlled synthetic experiment with analytically defined spatially varying coefficients, coordinate-feature entanglement consistently improves recovery of linear gradients and localized Gaussian hotspots relative to separable embeddings, reducing coefficient mean squared error across multiple regression frameworks. Hybrid and full entanglement configurations further reshape similarity geometry, though gains depend on alignment between topology and underlying spatial structure. A real-world case study on ride-hailing demand in Chicago demonstrates modest but stable improvements in predictive accuracy and spatially varying feature interpretation under coordinate-feature entanglement. These findings suggest that entanglement functions as a geometric prior, embedding spatial heterogeneity intrinsically within representation and opening a new direction for quantum-inspired geospatial modeling.



Keywords: quantum geospatial science,  coordinate–attribute entanglement,  Hilbert space embedding,  quantum GeoAI
