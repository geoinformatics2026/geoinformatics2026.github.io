# Bridging Civil Advocacy and Urban Planning: A Spatial-Semantic Framework for Automating Intersection Improvement Reports

**Author Information**  
Chun-Chieh Yang<sup>1</sup>, An-Syu Li<sup>1</sup>, Xu-Lin Chen<sup>1</sup>, Yang-Chou Juan<sup>1</sup>, Yi-Chung Chen<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Department of Computer Science and Engineering, National Chung Hsing University  



## Abstract

Traffic intersection redesign requires extensive man-ual comparison and rigorous bureaucratic documentation, posing a significant barrier for urban planners and civil advocacy groups. To address this challenge, this study introduces an automated retrieval-augmented generation pipeline powered by a multimodal large language model (LLM) to synthesize regulation- compliant administrative reports. The proposed framework op- erates by first extracting structured textual descriptions from paired original and improved intersection designs. A retrieval module then dynamically integrates relevant legal frameworks from an external traffic regulation knowledge base. By combining these visual descriptions with grounded legal context, the system generates comprehensive, multi-perspective administrative docu- ments. This approach effectively translates visual layout modifi- cations into formal policy justifications, accelerating procedural timelines and lowering the expertise barriers for infrastructural improvements.



Keywords: Urban Planning,  Spatial-Semantic Analysis,  Intersection Redesign
