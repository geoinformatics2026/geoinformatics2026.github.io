# By Looks or by Form: Disentangling Streetscape Appearance and Structure to Read Urban Wealth Gaps

## Abstract

Street view imagery (SVI) presents a strong capability of representing the urban environment, which makes it widely applied in urban-relevant deep learning tasks. However, geometry and appearance dimensions are often conflated in SVI, causing low explanatory power. For example, the sky regions in SVI can encode both chromatic style and perceived street scale. The study proposes an explicit disentanglement learning framework, that separates streetscape embeddings into geometry-oriented and appearance-oriented dimensions, capturing how places are shaped versus how they look. Using the method, we examine how metropolitan areas of 67 global cities can be decoded by the geometry and appearance dimensions differently, which enhances the morphological understanding of the cities from a street perspective. It is found that wealthy cities are associated with diversity of both appearance and geometry in street environments, while economically underdeveloped cities are dominated by structural diversity. Furthermore, we reveal that geometry dimensions serve as a prevalent strong indicator of street activities at the global level, while appearance dimensions can be the leading factors to recognize poverty in African and Asian cities. The findings provide new insights into how urban geometry and appearance interact at the street level, informing high-quality urban design and urban renewal.
