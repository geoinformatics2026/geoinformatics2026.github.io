# Bridging geospatial and micro-climate variables using deep learning for better urban heat resilience assessment

**Author Information**  
Trung Luong<sup>1</sup>, Chayn Sun<sup>1</sup>, Trinh Tran<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> RMIT University  



## Abstract

Land surface temperature (LST) derived from thermal remote sensing, alongside near-surface air temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, and radiation, is widely utilized to assess the absorptive and recovery capacities of urban areas in heat resilience evaluations. However, these metrics exhibit critical limitations that undermine their efficacy: satellite-derived LST products, despite their broad spatial coverage, inadequately capture human-relevant thermal conditions, whereas neighborhood-scale micro-climate variables are more appropriate in reflecting individual thermal comfort and risk exposure but remain prohibitively expensive and labor-intensive to acquire at sufficient resolution and coverage. This study aims to bridge geospatial and microclimate variables by developing a practical, data-driven method to translate satellite indicators into neighborhood-level conditions, thereby enhancing urban heat resilience assessments. Selecting Hanoi City (Vietnam) as the case study, the research incorporates diverse geospatial thermal datasets with meteorological data from mobile monitoring stations and contextual layers delineating urban morphology and land cover to employ machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models to predict near-surface air temperature and heat stress indices at the neighborhood scale. Results demonstrate that integrating micro-climate observation from mobile stations with appropriate ML/DL architectures can reliably estimate near-surface air temperature from LST. These findings establish a feasible, scalable framework for cities to combine satellite-derived LST with sparse micro-climate measurements to yield improvements and consistent accuracy of climate factor estimation to enable more reliable urban heat resilience evaluations at the neighborhood level.
