# Quantifying indoor–outdoor disparities in individual-level heat exposure based on spatiotemporal activity-travel trajectories

**Author Information**  
Liangkan Chen<sup>1</sup>, Mei-Po Kwan<sup>1</sup>, Jianwei Huang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Chinese University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

As global urbanization and climate change intensify, urban heat exposure has emerged as a prominent environmental challenge. Traditional assessment methods, largely reliant on static population distribution and Land Surface Temperature (LST), often overlook individual mobility, leading to the Uncertain Geographic Context Problem (UGCoP). To accurately characterize real-world heat exposure, this study conducted a longitudinal empirical investigation in Hong Kong from 2021 to 2025. By integrating GPS trajectories, mobile micro-environmental sensor data, and subjective activity-travel diaries from over 700 participants, we constructed a multi-source heterogeneous spatiotemporal activity dataset. Utilizing Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMM), we systematically quantified the coupling mechanisms between indoor and outdoor heat exposure while controlling for socio-demographic characteristics, multi-dimensional geographic environments, and activity categories. The results reveal significant heterogeneity and socioeconomic polarization in individual heat exposure: young and middle-aged males bear higher heat loads due to commuting and outdoor activities, whereas high-income groups exhibit superior heat-avoidance adaptive capacity. Further analysis demonstrates that traditional static measurements entail systematic biases across social groups, underscoring the necessity of incorporating indoor micro-environments and activity-travel patterns to eliminate errors and explain heat exposure inequalities. By elucidating the mechanisms of thermal inequality driven by activity disparities, this study suggests that policy interventions should adhere to a "people-oriented" principle—shifting from universal cooling strategies to mobility-based targeted interventions rooted in climate justice to enhance equity in urban climate adaptation.
