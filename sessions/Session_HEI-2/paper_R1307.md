# Greenspace Morphology and Momentary Mental Well-Being: A Mobility-Based Analysis of Urban Exposure

**Author Information**  
Lingwei Zheng<sup>1</sup>, Mei-Po Kwan<sup>1</sup>, Yang Liu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Chinese University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

As urban populations increasingly experience psychological stress, understanding how urban greenspace supports momentary mental well-being has become a critical research priority. While prior studies often rely on static residential measures, less attention has been paid to how the morphological configuration of greenspace shapes real-time psychological responses during daily mobility. This study integrates high-resolution vegetation data with geo-located momentary assessments to examine how multidimensional greenspace morphology relates to momentary stress and emotional states. Both landscape-level coverage and configuration indicators—including patch size, shape regularity, and spatial arrangement—are analyzed using mobility-based exposure measures. The findings indicate that greenspace morphology is significantly associated with momentary psychological outcomes, with different dimensions of spatial configuration exhibiting distinct patterns of association. In particular, structural characteristics beyond overall coverage appear to play an important role in shaping real-time stress responses. The results further suggest that the relationship between greenspace and mental well-being may vary across spatial contexts and activity settings. These findings highlight the importance of incorporating mobility-based exposure and configuration-sensitive perspectives into urban greenspace planning, moving beyond simple coverage metrics toward more nuanced spatial design strategies to support mental health.



Keywords: Greenspace morphology,  Ecological momentary assessment (EMA),  Mobility-based exposure,  Momentary mental well-being,  Landscape configuration
