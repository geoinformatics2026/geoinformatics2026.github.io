# Multi-scale trade-offs/synergies of ecosystem service supply-demand risk in Wuhan Metropolitan Area based on service flow

**Author Information**  
Yuling Peng<sup>1</sup>, Haona Peng<sup>1</sup>, Jingyu Tian<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> School of Civil Engineering and Architecture, Wuhan Institute of Technology,Wuhan 430074, China;  



## Abstract

With rapid urbanization, the imbalance between ecosystem service (ES) supply and demand (ESSD) threatens human survival and development, particularly in metropolitan areas. To promote the construction of green metropolitan areas and regional sustainable development, six typical ESs were selected. A spatial flow model of ESs was established by integrating the supply-demand competition-based two-step floating catchment area method with service path attribute network theory, which objectively simulates the generation, flow, and consumption processes of ES flows (ESF) at the grid scale. Based on the simulation results of ESF, a dynamic assessment model for ESSD risk (ESSDR) was constructed using current and trend indicators, and the ESSDR in the Wuhan Metropolitan Area from 2002 to 2022 was quantitatively analyzed. Pearson correlation analysis and a geographically weighted regression model were used to detect trade-offs and synergies among different types of ESSDR at the 1 km grid, township, and district levels. The results show that the ESSDR of carbon sequestration, water yield, and water purification was severe, with average ESSDR indices of −0.41, −4.03, and −4.00, respectively. The ESSDR of soil conservation, food production, and landscape culture was relatively low, with average ESSDR indices of 9.72, 9.40, and 6.85, respectively. Strong synergies were observed between food production and landscape culture, as well as between water purification and water yield. In contrast, food production and water purification showed a strong trade-off. These three pairs of relationships formed the core of ES interactions. The trade-offs and synergies of ESSDR exhibited obvious scale effects, and their intensity increased with the expansion of the research scale. Carbon sequestration showed strong compatibility and played a significantly positive regulatory role in the overall ESSDR. The findings can provide a scientific basis and strong support for the efficient management and decision-making regarding ESSDR in metropolitan areas.
