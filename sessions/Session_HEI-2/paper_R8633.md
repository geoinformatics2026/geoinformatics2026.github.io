# How Do Landscape Patterns and Morphological Spatial Patterns Effect the Habitat Quality of Urban Park Green Spaces? — An Interactive Effects Analysis

**Author Information**  
Li Zeng<sup>1</sup>, Han Yang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Southwest Petroleum University  



## Abstract

Against the backdrop of urban park construction, while the area of urban park green spaces (UPGS) has increased remarkably, the fragmentation of green spaces landscape patterns—caused by impervious surface—has simultaneously intensified. This fragmentation, in turn, degrades the urban habitat quality (HQ) and impairs the healthy and sustainable development of cities. Taking Chengdu, a national demonstration zone for park cities, as the study area, we constructed a HQ impact assessment framework by integrating landscape patterns and morphological spatial patterns. Subsequently, we identified the key driving metrics affecting HQ, and further employed interpretable machine learning and the Geodetector to quantify the contribution of these metrics and analyze the interactive effects among the driving metrics. The results indicate that: (1) There is a significant spatial heterogeneity in the distribution of UPGS and HQ in Chengdu, which generally presents the characteristic that UPGS is densely distributed and well-connected in the core area, while being sparsely scattered in an embedded pattern in the peripheral areas; (2) The largest patch index (LPI) contributes the most to the impact on HQ, the higher the value of the LPI, the more stable the UPGS system becomes; (3) The splitting index (SPLIT) is a key enhancing metric, which amplifies the impact on HQ when it acts synergistically with other metrics. Finally, to facilitate the green and sustainable development of cities, we proposed that optimizing the habitat of parks around the government square, designing a cross-patch corridor for Longjiaoshan Park, and integrating the spatial system of existing fragmented parks at the boundary junction of Shuangliu and Wuhou Districts.
