# Urban rooftop mitigation potential assessment and socio-economic exposure response to future heat extremes

**Author Information**  
Jingqi Chen<sup>1</sup>, Na Dong<sup>1</sup>, Jing Shang<sup>2</sup>, Zhen Liu<sup>3</sup>, Huabing Huang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Sun Yat-sen University

## Abstract

Against the backdrop of intensifying global climate change and frequent extreme high temperature events, deeply understanding the future pathways of high temperature and assessing the potential cooling impacts of mitigation measures are of great significance. Based on the mesoscale Weather Research and Forecasting model in combination with two Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs), namely SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5, this study evaluates the spatial-temporal extreme temperature changes in the typical Guangzhou and Foshan metropolis from 2020 to 2050 and the mitigation effects of three rooftop strategies (cool roof, grass roof, and sedum roof). Results show that both the daily average 2-m air temperature (T2) and the daily maximum 2-m temperature (T2M) will increase significantly in the future without any mitigation strategies. Under the SSP5-8.5 scenario, warming (about 1.4℃) is much stronger than that of SSP1-2.6 (about 0.6℃), and the number of days with high temperature (HD) and the heat wave strength (HWS) would be remarkably intensified in the high building density area. Meanwhile, the risks of socio-economic exposure are projected to surge with the population exposure of 2050 turns to 1.83 to 1.85 times that of 2020, and the economic exposure will soar to 5.36 to 6.93 times. Implementing roof mitigation measures can effectively reduce both heat exposure (HD and HWS) and socio-economic exposure, among which cool roof provides the most substantial cooling effect, with an average cooling and exposure reduction benefit approximately twice that of green roofs under both SSP1-2.6 and SSP5-8.5 scenarios. This research offers localized and practical insights for formulating evidence-based climate resilience plans, protecting public health, and reducing economic losses.
