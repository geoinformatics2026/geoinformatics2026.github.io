# An integrated LiDAR–optical framework for explaining urban thermal variability across multiple sites

**Author Information**  
Yuncheng Deng<sup>1</sup>, Jinliang Wang<sup>2</sup>, Suling He<sup>2</sup>, Jiya Pan<sup>2</sup>, Jianpeng Zhang<sup>2</sup>, Qianwei Liu<sup>3</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Yunnan Normal University Faculty of Geography  

<sup>2</sup> Faculty of Geography, Yunnan Normal University, Kunming, China  

<sup>3</sup> Yunnan Data and Application Center of China, High-resolution Earth Observation System  



## Abstract

Urban heat studies have primarily focused on vegetation quantity and land-cover composition, while the independent contribution of three-dimensional (3D) green structure and its variability across urban green types remain insufficiently quantified. This study investigates eight representative residential sites in Chenggong District, Kunming, and develops a 30-m structural indicator system derived from airborne LiDAR non-ground point clouds, including height percentiles (P10–P99, IQR), vertical complexity (entropy and normalized entropy), surface roughness (plane-fitting RMSE), and vertical stratification proportions. These metrics are spatially aligned with Landsat growing-season land surface temperature (LST) and optical indices (NDVI and NDBI). To disentangle the relative effects of surface composition and 3D structure, three model groups (LiDAR-only, Optical-only, and integrated models) are constructed and evaluated using Spearman correlation, Ridge cross-validation, and SHAP-based interpretability analysis, and ΔLST is calculated to represent relative thermal anomalies. Results show that optical variables generally explain more variance than structural metrics, indicating that site-scale thermal patterns are primarily governed by impervious–vegetation gradients. However, integrated models consistently outperform single-source models, demonstrating a stable incremental contribution from 3D structural information, particularly in park-dominated and highly vegetated residential sites. NDBI ranks as the dominant predictor in seven of eight sites, confirming impervious surfaces as the principal warming driver, whereas NDVI dominates in the mountainous high-coverage site. Among structural metrics, vertical complexity consistently shows the strongest influence, suggesting that multilayer canopy structure enhances cooling through shading and turbulent heat exchange. These findings reveal clear type-dependent thermal mechanisms and provide quantitative evidence for optimizing urban green structure to improve cooling performance.
