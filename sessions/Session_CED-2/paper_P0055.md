# Deep Learning–Based Spatial Downscaling of Land Surface Temperature: A Comparative Study of U-Net, ResNet, and Hybrid Models

**Author Information**  
Nutcha Nontarak<sup>1</sup>, Kittiphop Simachokchai<sup>1</sup>, Nuntikorn Kitratporn<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> GISTDA  



## Abstract

Land Surface Temperature (LST) is a critical variable for urban climate analysis, environmental monitoring, and heat risk assessment. However, satellite-derived LST products are often limited by the trade-off between spatial and temporal resolution, restricting their applicability for fine-scale studies, particularly in heterogeneous landscapes. To address this limitation, this study investigates the effectiveness of deep learning-based convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for nonlinear spatial downscaling of LST through the integration of multi-source geospatial data, including multispectral indices and topographic information. Three CNN architectures, namely-U-Net, ResNet-50, and a hybrid U-Net-ResNet model were implemented and systematically compared for LST downscaling in Nakhon Ratchasima Province, Thailand. Model performance was evaluated using root mean square error (RMSE) and coefficient of determination (R2). The results indicate that the hybrid U-Net-ResNet architecture achieves the best performance, with an RMSE of $1.80^{\circ}C$ and an $R^{2}$ of 0.818, demonstrating improved accuracy compared with the U-Net and ResNet-50 models. These findings suggest that hybrid deep learning frameworks provide an effective nonlinear approach for generating high-resolution LST products, supporting regional-scale environmental monitoring, urban heat island (UHI) assessment, and climate-resilient urban planning



Keywords: land surface temperature, downscaling, convolutional neural networks
