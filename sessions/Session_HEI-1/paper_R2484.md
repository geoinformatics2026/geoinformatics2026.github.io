# A Cost-Weighted Method for Measuring the Accessibility of Emergency Medical Services

**Author Information**  
You Wan<sup>1</sup>, Fu Ren<sup>1</sup>, A-Xing Zhu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Wuhan University  

<sup>2</sup> University of Wisconsin-Madison  



## Abstract

Emergency medical service (EMS) accessibility refers to how easily and effectively individuals can obtain timely emergency care when faced with acute health threats. Accurately measuring EMS accessibility is essential for optimizing resource allocation and ensuring equitable service delivery. Existing accessibility approaches have advanced the evaluation of general medical service accessibility, but they largely overlook the direct role of time-related costs in shaping access outcomes. The omission of time-costs is especially consequential in the context of EMS, where timely intervention is often the determining factor in patient survival and recovery. Unlike routine healthcare, where some delay may be tolerable, emergency care requires minimizing transfer-related time and distance to ensure rapid response. Service providers must simultaneously manage the allocation of vehicles and personnel, and reduce travel time to achieve effective intervention. However, existing accessibility methods typically treat time or distance as secondary modifiers, rather than integrating them as critical cost variables that fundamentally define the effectiveness of EMS allocation. To address this methodological gap, this study develops an advanced EMS-specific accessibility measurement method by introducing the cost-weighted two-step floating catchment area (C2SFCA) approach, which directly incorporates travel-time costs into the accessibility evaluation. C2SFCA advances existing methods in two key ways: (1) it constructs the supply–demand service network using a contingency-based time threshold rather than fixed geographic distances, and (2) it applies time-weighted demand to more realistically capture the cost of service provision over varying distances. In controlled simulations, C2SFCA captures accessibility changes driven by location alteration and new site selection more precisely than traditional fixed-radius approaches. A real-world case study in Wuhan, China, further validates the method’s ability to reflect spatial and temporal variations associated with emergency station deployment. Furthermore, sensitivity analyses indicate robust performance regarding reasonable parameter changes, enhancing the method's practical applicability.
