# Spatial Accessibility and Health Equity of Older Adults Care Facilities: An Integrated Framework of MG2SFCA and Agent-Based Modeling in Fuzhou, China

**Author Information**  
Qiuyi Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Siying Wu<sup>1</sup>, Xin Wu<sup>1</sup>, Fengxiao Cao<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> FuJian University of Technology  



## Abstract

This study develops an integrated framework combining static accessibility assessment and dynamic agent-based modeling to evaluate spatial equity of three types of older adults care facilities (medical, meal, residential) across 2,275 communities in Fuzhou. Using the modified Gaussian two-step floating catchment area method and agent-based simulation(ABM), this study finds meal assistance facilities exhibit the highest inequality (Gini = 0.6797). Medical facility distribution is driven by building density and housing prices, residential care by river proximity and street density, and meal services by older adults population density. Comprehensive policy interventions (facility optimization, age-friendly transportation, and health education) demonstrate synergistic effects in improving health equity. This research provides methodological innovations and empirical evidence for aging-society facility planning, emphasizing cross-sectoral coordination as key to achieving spatial health equity.
