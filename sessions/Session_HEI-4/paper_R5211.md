# Exploration of the temporal and spatial characteristics of the spread of dengue fever

**Author Information**  
Moran Chen<sup>1</sup>, Hongyan Ren<sup>1</sup>, An Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Yilan Liao<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Institute of Geographic Sciences and Natural Resources Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences  



## Abstract

Dengue fever is an acute infectious disease caused by the dengue virus through the bite of Aedes mosquitoes. It is highly prevalent in some densely populated and highly urbanized areas in southern China. Its spread and prevalence are closely related to the suitable mosquito breeding environment in urban areas, frequent population movement, and human-mosquito contact in typical urban facilities. Therefore, clarifying the relationship between population movement and disease transmission and identifying key risk nodes are of great significance for implementing precise prevention and control. Using the dengue fever epidemic in Guangzhou as a case study, this research combined population positioning data to construct four population dynamic landscape indicators including DTMI, TMI, SMI and STPI for characterizing the spatiotemporal differentiation of the population in Guangzhou. In addition, based on the co-location pattern mining method and Monte Carlo simulation, the spatial nodes for the spread of dengue fever were identified through adaptive thresholds and significance tests at the 0.05 level. And the association between spatial nodes and the prevalence of dengue fever was analyzed. The results show that the high-value clusters of population dynamic indicators are mainly located in the central urban areas with high population mobility and carrying capacity, high population density and strong population attraction, which is consistent with the distribution of dengue fever high-incidence areas. Furthermore, the dengue epidemic intensity of areas near identified spatial nodes such as newsstands and convalescent facilities is significantly higher than that of the regional average and non-node areas. High-intensity population movement, high-density population concentration during the peak period of mosquito bites, diffusion hub function of dense traffic stations and road network, and close human-mosquito contact at spatial nodes collectively promote the spread of the disease. This study provides important support for the precise prevention and control of dengue fever in complex urban environments.



Keywords: Dengue fever,  Population dynamics,  Spatial node,  Precise prevention and control
