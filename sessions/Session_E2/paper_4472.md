# Process Decomposition and Environmental Driving Mechanisms of Water Use Efficiency in the Poyang Lake Basin

**Author Information**  
Xiankun Shi<sup>1</sup>, Hui Lin<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Jiangxi Normal University  



## Abstract

Water use efficiency (WUE) is a core indicator for assessing the drought resistance and productivity of watershed ecosystems. In response to the complex river-lake relationships and highly fluctuating wetland habitats in the Poyang Lake Basin, this study employs a "process decomposition" approach to analyze the drivers of WUE. The WUE is physically decomposed into four interconnected intermediate processes: vegetation physiological efficiency (GPP/T), transpiration allocation (T/ET), soil water extraction (ET/SW), and water replenishment retention (SW/TWI). First, variance decomposition is used to quantitatively isolate and evaluate the independent contributions of these four processes to the spatiotemporal heterogeneity and interannual variability of WUE, identifying the key processes driving fluctuations in carbon-water coupling within the watershed. Second, a structural equation model (SEM) is constructed to systematically analyze the direct effects and indirect cascading impacts of meteorological factors (temperature, precipitation, radiation, wind speed) and leaf area index (LAI) on each intermediate process and WUE. From an intermediate perspective, this study elucidates the mechanisms underlying WUE changes in the Poyang Lake Basin, providing a logically rigorous theoretical foundation for watershed ecosystem management and optimal water resource allocation.



Keywords: Poyang Lake Basin,  Water Use Efficiency,  WUE Chain,  Variance Decomposition,  Structural Equation Model
