# Examination of urban resilience during the COVID-19 pandemic with the analysis of MRT data in Taipei metropolitan area

**Author Information**  
Bing Sheng Wu<sup>1</sup>, Pin-Yan Liu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National Taiwan Normal University  



## Abstract

In recent years, urban resilience has been broadly discussed while cities around the world face a variety of natural and human-induced hazards. The core concept of urban resilience emphasizes on the general capacity and ability of a community to withstand stress, survive, adapt and bounce back from a crisis or disaster and rapidly move on. While the COVID-19 pandemic limits human mobility and affects human lives significantly, it also provides an opportunity for researchers to explore how citizens and societies recover from the devastating outbreak. This study aims to analyze Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) ridership data in Taipei metropolitan area from 2019 to 2023 and distinguish dynamics of human mobility during the pandemic period for the interpretation of urban resilience. The first stage is to categorize the functional features of MRT stations with the dynamic time warping (DTW) algorithm. Two indices, vulnerability and recovery are introduced to evaluate urban resilience for the categories of MRT stations. The analytical results highlight four types of MRT stations, which are commercial, residential, mix, and tour-oriented stations, in Taipei metropolitan area. In addition, a higher level of resilience takes place in residential-oriented MRT stations, and most of commercial and tour-oriented stations experience a lower level of recovery. In summary, the adoption of MRT data successfully reveals human reactions during the COVID-19 period. Moreover, this study delineates the spatial variation of urban resilience in Taipei metropolitan area and explore how urban structures influence the degree of urban resilience.



Keywords: GIS,  Urban resilience,  Dynamic time warping,  Mass Rapid Transit (MRT),  COVID-19
