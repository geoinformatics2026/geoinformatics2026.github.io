# Landslide susceptibility for linear cultural heritage corridors: Multi-scale ensemble learning and temporal analysis of the Ancient Shu Roads

**Author Information**  
Haixia Feng<sup>1</sup>, Qingwu Hu<sup>1</sup>, Daoyuan Zheng<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Wuhan University  



## Abstract

Landslides pose persistent threats to linear cultural heritage systems, which are strongly influenced by complex topography and geomorphic processes. However, linear cultural heritage corridors have received limited attention in existing landslide susceptibility studies. This study uses the Ancient Shu Roads in China as a case study and develops a multi-scale landslide susceptibility assessment framework for linear cultural heritage systems. Fifteen influencing factors related to topography, geology, vegetation, hydrology, and human activities were incorporated using both slope units and pixel units. An automated ensemble learning approach based on the AutoGluon framework was applied to capture nonlinear relationships between landslides and environmental factors. Its performance was compared with transformer-based, gradient-boosting-based, and tree-based models. Model interpretability was analyzed using Shapley additive explanations. The results show that the Baoxie Route exhibits the highest proportion of high-susceptibility landslides, while the Tangluo Route shows the lowest. Regarding controlling mechanisms, landslide susceptibility along the corridor is mainly governed by the combined effects of slope height, topographic wetness index, and distance to rivers. In terms of spatial representation, slope units provide a more physically meaningful description of geomorphic processes, and their scale selection significantly affects the effectiveness of different learning models. AutoGluon-based ensemble learning achieves the best performance under a medium-scale slope-unit configuration (AUC = 0.959, Acc = 88.65%). Model performance varies across different sample sizes and spatial evaluation units. Random forest maintains high and stable predictive accuracy across all three spatial types, demonstrating strong adaptability and robustness to variations in sample size. Regarding temporal processes, time-series experiments indicate clear temporal variability in landslide susceptibility patterns. Long-term land use transitions, particularly cropland reduction and forest expansion, are significantly associated with decreased susceptibility, while monthly precipitation variability leads to pronounced intra-annual risk fluctuations.
