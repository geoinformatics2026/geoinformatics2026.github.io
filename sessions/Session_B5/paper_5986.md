# Identifying and Analyzing the Evolution of Urban Functional Areas in Guangzhou by Integrating Nighttime Light and POI Data

**Author Information**  
Yihan Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Yifang Zhuang<sup>1</sup>, Shanshan Li<sup>1</sup>, Yili Lin<sup>1</sup>, Tiansen Guan<sup>1</sup>, Yining Qiu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Guangdong University of Finance & Economics  



## Abstract

This study establishes a coupling analytical framework integrating nighttime light and points of interest (POI) data to achieve high-precision identification of urban functional zones in Guangzhou. Using multi-temporal data for 2012, 2016, and 2020, we systematically analyzed the spatial structure evolution and driving mechanisms. The results demonstrate strong consistency between the two datasets, with consistently classified grid cells exceeding 75% across all study years. Guangzhou exhibits a polycentric spatial structure characterized by "central agglomeration and peripheral dispersion, " evolving from single- core diffusion to multi-axis extension. Eight coupling types correspond to distinct functional zones, validated through quantitative land use analysis. Driving mechanism analysis reveals significant shifts in dominant factors: from population density and infrastructure in early stages to subway stations in the intermediate stage, ultimately evolving to permanent population size and urbanization rate. The coupled analysis provides an effective methodological pathway for identifying urban spatial structure, delineating functional zones, and investigating driving mechanisms, offering empirical support for multi-source data integration in urban studies.
