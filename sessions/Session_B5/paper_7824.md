# Study on Land Subsidence Characteristics in Guangzhou Metro Protection Zones Based on Time-Series InSAR and GIS Spatial Analysis

**Author Information**  
Qilin Hu<sup>1</sup>, Junxia Wushan<sup>2</sup>, Sihuang Chen<sup>1</sup>, Simin Hao<sup>2</sup>, Sen He<sup>2</sup>, Fei Wang<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Guangzhou Metro Design Research Institute Co., Ltd.  

<sup>2</sup> Institute of Safety Science and Technology, Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School  



## Abstract

This study performs a spatiotemporal analysis and multi-dimensional attribution of land subsidence within Guangzhou's urban rail transit zones. Utilizing TS-InSAR (SBAS and PS-InSAR) on multi-source SAR imagery, we identified a "Stable North, Subsiding South" paradigm. While the urban core remains stable, extreme subsidence exceeding 280 mm/year persists in the rapidly developing Panyu and Nansha districts. Integrating GIS and machine learning, we quantified the driving mechanisms: a superposition of thick (15-40m) marine- terrestrial soft soils and anthropogenic pressures, including heavy building loads, groundwater extraction, and metro-induced socio- agglomeration. Tectonic faults further catalyze localized differ- ential settlement. We propose a lightweight, intuitive analytical framework that bypasses the complexity of traditional geotech- nical modeling. This approach provides essential spatial decision support for operational safety, early warning, and sustainable infrastructural planning in high-density deltaic metropolises.



Keywords: Time-Series InSAR,  land subsidence,  urban rail transit,  Geographic Information Systems,  spatial attribution,  Pearl River Delta,  soft soil consolidation
