# Spatio-Temporal Evolution and Drivers of Energy Value Chain Networks: A Cross-Sectoral Comparative Perspective

**Author Information**  
Siyuan Kang<sup>1</sup>, Wei Luo<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National University of Singapore  



## Abstract

In global energy trade, domestic value-added (DVA) moves across borders through infrastructures and institutions that differ between fossil fuels and electricity or gas. Yet, systematic evidence comparing the structural organization of these sectors remains limited. We use the 2011–2023 Asian Development Bank multiregional input–output tables for further energy value chain network analysis, supplemented by UN Comtrade bilateral energy trade data for validation. Based on a Leontief input–output framework and value-added decomposition, we construct directed and weighted DVA trade networks and examine their structure using centrality and community metrics. Exponential random graph models assess both endogenous network effects and exogenous drivers, including population growth, GDP growth, energy use, FDI, trade openness, regional trade agreements, and geopolitical alignment. Results show that fossil fuel DVA networks involve larger and more volatile flows, concentrated around the United States, Russia, and major Asian hubs. Electricity and gas DVA networks have grown over the past decade, marked by increasing European participation and persistent sensitivity to infrastructure constraints, pipeline shifts, and grid bottlenecks. These networks also differ in community structure: fossil fuels form stable and cross-regional clusters, while electricity and gas networks are more localized and fragmented. In addition, these DVA trade network patterns reflect reciprocity, triadic closure, and bilateral asymmetries driven by differences in energy security, energy consumption, and socio-economic characteristics. These findings provide a comparative view of how value flows are organized across energy sectors and offer insights for diversification and risk management.
