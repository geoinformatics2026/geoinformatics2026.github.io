# Inter-provincial Flow of Virtual Agricultural Elements and Their Telecoupling Impacts on the Agricultural Environmental Stress in China

**Author Information**  
Hongrun Ju<sup>1</sup>, Shengrui Zhang<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> School of Environment and Geography, Qingdao University  

<sup>2</sup> Management College, Ocean University of China  



## Abstract

Land use, water scarcity, and carbon emissions from agriculture collectively influence both food security and environmental health. While studies on the inter-regional transfer of virtual agricultural elements have been conducted at international and national levels, limited research has examined the impact of these flows on agricultural environmental stress, particularly lacking in quantitative analysis of carbon emission reduction pressures within the agricultural sector. The aim of this study is to obtain of virtual elements flow pattern and quantify the telecoupling impacts on agricultural environmental stress, illuminating the links between regional virtual resources arising from grain trade. The Environmental Extension Multi-regional Input-Output (EE-MRIO) model was employed to analyze the spatial pattern of virtual agricultural land, water, and carbon flows across Chinese provinces. Meanwhile, the agricultural environmental stress was quantified using the environmental footprint model, with three key indicators: Cropland Stress Index (LSI), Agricultural Water Stress Index (WSI), and Agricultural Carbon Emission Reduction Stress Index (CSI). The results showed that virtual “land-water-carbon” flows in the agricultural sector accounted for 43.30%, 42.69%, and 39.39% of their respective total footprints in China. The interprovincial flows of virtual “land-water-carbon” move largely from the north to the south and from inland to the coast. The agricultural environmental stress varies in provinces in China, with the western and northern regions generally being higher. The average LSI, WSI, and CSI all got alleviated from the virtual resources flow in China. Economically developed areas such as Beijing, Shanghai, and Tianjin have gained great deduction of the stress, while western areas like Xinjiang, Gansu, Shaanxi and other outflow areas have their agricultural environment stress increased. Based on these results, it is recommended to identify key regions for managing different types of environmental stress; establish ecological compensation standards; and adjust cropping patterns in areas experiencing high environmental stress.
