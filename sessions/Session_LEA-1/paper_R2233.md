# Persistent Inequality in Coal-Related Emission Responsibilities During the Energy Transition

**Author Information**  
Jiajie Shang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Curtin University  



## Abstract

This study develops an environmentally extended multi-regional input–output (EE-MRIO) framework to examine the transboundary redistribution of coal-related environmental burdens under the global energy transition, with particular attention to the structural inequality embedded in international production–consumption linkages during 2012–2023. The study attributes upstream coal-related emissions to final demand and derives both production-based accounts (PBA) and consumption-based accounts (CBA) to compare where emissions are generated and where they are ultimately driven by consumption. Preliminary results indicate that higher-income economies have generally reduced their territorial reliance on coal, yet a notable share of coal-related emissions remains embodied in internationally traded goods consumed in these economies. In contrast, lower-income countries continue to bear a disproportionately large share of production-side coal-related burdens within global supply chains. The comparison between PBA and CBA further suggests a persistent asymmetry in carbon responsibility, with the divergence between production and consumption accounts being more pronounced in wealthier economies. Distributional analysis also reveals that coal-related environmental burdens remain unevenly allocated across countries and income groups, indicating that the benefits of decarbonisation have not been shared equitably. By integrating carbon footprint accounting with inequality analysis, this study provides an empirical basis for assessing whether contemporary energy transitions alleviate coal dependence in a globally just manner or instead reproduce existing structural disparities.
