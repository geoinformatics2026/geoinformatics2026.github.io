## Abstract

This study introduces the Local Pathway Association (LPA) model to address the limitations of traditional path analysis, which often overlooks local effects in large-scale spatial studies. By integrating Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) with local coefficient estimation, the LPA model follows a three-step process: first, it determines the optimal local range for variables using distance decay functions; second, it extracts local data to estimate causal path coefficients; and finally, it extends this analysis point-by-point across the entire study area. Applied to climate, soil, and vegetation data on the Tibetan Plateau, the LPA model successfully captured spatial heterogeneity and avoided the overestimation or underestimation common in global models. With its strong data compatibility, the LPA model serves as a robust technical tool for understanding localized interactions and complex variations within large-scale geospatial and Earth systems.
