# X-minute cyclable neighborhood：Beyond the one-size-fits-all approach to unpacking the social inequalities

**Author Information**  
Haonan Cai<sup>1</sup>, Shiliang Su<sup>1</sup>, Qianqian Li<sup>1</sup>, Xuanting Chen<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> School of Resource and Environmental Sciences, Wuhan University  



## Abstract

Amid rapid urban expansion and increasing functional zoning, the X-minute neighborhood has attracted growing attention as a planning concept for enhancing daily accessibility and urban equity. Existing methods, however, often rely on fixed spatial thresholds or static buffers, which inadequately reflect residents’ actual daily mobility practices and the social heterogeneity embedded therein. To address this limitation, this study introduces the concept of the X-minute cyclable neighborhood. Then, a neighborhood delineation method is proposed to identify such neighborhoods within a citywide cycling network based on shared bicycle origin–destination data. Based on the delineated neighborhoods, a dual-dimensional indicator framework is developed, capturing cycling activity patterns and spatial morphological features to characterize internal mobility structure and spatial organization, as well as their association with residents’ socioeconomic status. Results indicate that: (i) neighborhood morphology and internal mobility patterns are closely associated with education, occupational status, and marital composition; (ii) neighborhoods can be classified into distinct types exhibiting systematic differences in socioeconomic composition and cycling behavior. These findings demonstrate that X-minute cyclable neighborhoods are behavioral spatial units shaped jointly by social structures and daily cycling practices, providing a novel framework for analyzing urban spatial disparities from a mobility-based perspective.



Keywords: X-minute cyclable neighborhood,  Neighborhood delineation,  Neighborhood morphology,  Mobility pattern,  Socioeconomic status.
