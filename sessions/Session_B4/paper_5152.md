# Automated and Context-Adaptive Assessment of Community Livability via Generative AI and Multimodal Geospatial Data

**Author Information**  
Hanzhi Zu<sup>1</sup>, Guosheng Yang<sup>1</sup>, Minwei Zhao<sup>1</sup>, Cai Wu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> THE HONG KONG UNIVERSITY OF SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY (GUANGZHOU)  



## Abstract

Community livability is a critical issue in the context of sustainable development goals and resilient urban transformation, necessitating systematic assessment. Traditional evaluation methods largely rely on universal indicator systems and expert judgment, often facing limitations such as narrow evaluation metrics, ambiguous core concepts, and high implementation costs, which hinder their capacity to capture local and contextual characteristics in complex urban environments. This study proposes a generative AI–based Automated and Context-Adaptive Assessment Framework. Drawing on Lefebvre’s triad of spatial theory and grounded theory, we construct a “Skeleton–Flesh–Skin” three-layer representation of urban communities. Heterogeneous geospatial data are transformed into inputs interpretable by large language models. The “Skeleton” layer derives topological centrality indicators from OpenStreetMap road networks and translates structural features into textual spatial descriptions to enhance semantic understanding of spatial form. The “Flesh” layer organizes street-level POI sequences into functional semantic summaries, capturing activity composition and interaction patterns. The “Skin” layer leverages a vision–language multimodal model (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct) to extract perceptual features directly from street imagery. These layers are integrated via structured prompting to generate context-specific textual representations of community livability. Grounded coding and term-frequency analysis are then applied to inductively derive livability dimensions and construct a quantitative indicator system with weighted structure. Applied to Beijing Road Street, Guangzhou, three livability dimensions were identified: traffic environment perception (walking 0.342; other transport 0.316), environmental quality perception (0.237), and community atmosphere perception (0.105), reflecting the high-density, mixed-use context with overlapping tourist and resident activities. The framework enables automated induction of livability dimensions and interpretable quantitative modeling, offering a reproducible and scalable approach for generating local knowledge and supporting quantitative studies of diverse urban issues. Potential biases in generative models, uncertainties in multi-source data integration, and the need for field validation are also discussed.



Keywords: Community Livability,  Generative AI,  Urban Perception
