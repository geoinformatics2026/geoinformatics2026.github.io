# Retail Beverage Price Inequality: Spatiotemporal Modeling of U.S. Soft Drink Prices (2008–2020)

**Author Information**  
Lan Mu<sup>1</sup>, Chen Zhen<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Georgia  



## Abstract

Retail food prices vary systematically across U.S. geographic space and time, yet most official price measures remain geographically coarse or highly aggregated. This study develops a county–month soft drink price index for the contiguous United States (2008–2020) to document and interpret fine-grained spatial price inequality. The index is constructed from USDA/Circana retail scanner data for counties satisfying a confidentiality “rule of three” (≥3 reporting stores). Working at the barcode level, we estimate rolling-window multilateral price indexes using the GEKS method with hedonic controls for retailer effects, reducing unit-value bias and store heterogeneity.  Each county–month is treated as a spatiotemporal observation within a space–time cube (1,580 counties × 156 months). We characterize long-term trends and seasonal structure using time-series decomposition and Mann–Kendall tests, and cluster counties by price level, correlation structure, and Fourier-based periodicity. Change-point detection identifies shifts in mean and slope, while emerging hot spot analysis and local indicators of spatial association map evolving regional concentrations.  Results reveal a sustained upward trend in soft drink prices alongside two distinct seasonal regimes. Spatially, monthly price maxima shift from west to east and concentrate in northern regions, whereas minima follow a north–south pattern and cluster in the East. These gradients are interpreted through spatial equilibrium and New Economic Geography frameworks, which link regional price levels to wages, rents, amenities, market access, agglomeration, and transport costs.  The findings demonstrate that subnational price differences exhibit persistent geospatial structure rather than random fluctuation. By integrating multilateral price index methods with GIS-based spatiotemporal analysis, this study provides a reproducible framework and public panel dataset to support research on regional inequality, food environments, and health disparities.



Keywords: County–month beverage price index,  Spatiotemporal analysis,  Retail scanner data,  Spatial equilibrium and New Economic Geography,  Regional price inequality
