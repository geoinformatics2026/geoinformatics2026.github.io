# Mapping Visual Attention to Navigational Cues in Real-World Wayfinding: Extraversion-Introversion and Gender Differences from Mobile Eye Tracking

**Author Information**  
Shiyuan Cheng<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Henan University  



## Abstract

This study examined pedestrian wayfinding in an unfamiliar real-world environment in 116 participants using a 2×2 between-subjects design crossing extraversion group (introverted vs extraverted) and gender (female vs male). Behavioral trajectories and wearable eye-tracking data were recorded simultaneously to test whether extraversion and gender influence wayfinding performance, the temporal dynamics of visual processing, and the allocation of attention across environmental cues. Introverted participants showed a lower path deviation rate and shorter saccade durations, suggesting a more locally organized mode of information sampling and verification. Gender differences were observed in completion time and stopping behavior: male participants completed the task more quickly but stopped more often, whereas female participants devoted a greater proportion of attention to signage. Most measures showed no interaction between extraversion group and gender. These findings provide process-level evidence on individual differences in real-world wayfinding, particularly those associated with extraversion and gender, and clarify how such differences are expressed through cue-based information use at decision points.
