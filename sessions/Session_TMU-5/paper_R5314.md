# Spatial Assessment of Distributed Photovoltaic Layout Suitability on Urban Roads in Singapore: An 'Air-Ground Coordination' Energy Sharing Perspective

**Author Information**  
Tieming Chen<sup>1</sup>, Yifan Lin<sup>2</sup>, Yaxing Li<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Zhejiang university of technology  

<sup>2</sup> Zhejiang University of Technology  



## Abstract

Faced with the challenges of extreme land scarcity and high-density urbanization in Singapore, this study aims to explore a novel "air-ground coordination" energy utilization model and seek an integrated energy solution that can simultaneously meet the demand for ground electric vehicle charging and low-altitude drone replenishment. A multi-dimensional comprehensive evaluation framework was constructed in this study: ① Refined supply and demand quantification: Using a high-precision urban model (including building and tree data), the actual effective solar radiation after considering dynamic shadow occlusion was calculated. ② Multi-criteria evaluation system: An indicator model was established from three dimensions, namely energy supply potential (radiation quantity and spatial feasibility), ground traffic demand (traffic flow density and pavement bearing capacity), and low-altitude flight suitability (airspace safety and route accessibility). ③ Spatial coupling analysis: The analytic hierarchy process (AHP) and other mathematical methods were adopted to determine the weights, and multi-layer data were overlaid in a geographic information system (GIS) to identify "golden nodes" that can serve both vehicles and drones for parking and replenishment. The study generated the "Road/Sidewalk Photovoltaic Effective Radiation Heat Map" and the "Air-Ground Energy Sharing Node Suitability Distribution Map" across Singapore. Based on the evaluation results, urban areas were divided into three types of construction zones: Road Photovoltaic Zones, suitable for open and low-speed road environments; Integrated Charging Tower Zones, located at core nodes with dense traffic and under drone routes, where facilities integrating photovoltaic canopies and drone landing pads are recommended; and Avoidance Zones, including deep and narrow streets with severe light occlusion or ecologically sensitive areas. This study breaks through traditional photovoltaic layouts, integrating drone infrastructure into ground charging planning to efficiently utilize urban stock space. It supports new energy siting in high-density cities and advances the energy transition of smart cities.
