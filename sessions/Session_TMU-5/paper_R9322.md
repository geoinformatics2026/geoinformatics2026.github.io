# A Unified Geospatial Framework for Electric-Vehicle Charging Behavior

**Author Information**  
Xiaobai Angela Yao<sup>1</sup>, Farnoosh Roozkhosh<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of Georgia  

<sup>2</sup> University of Gerogia  



## Abstract

Electric-vehicle (EV) charging behavior is examined across multiple disciplines but remains conceptually fragmented, hindering cumulative insight and policy translation. This talk presents a unified geospatial framework that synthesizes four analytical lenses, session-based, spatiotemporal, user-based, and vehicle-based, and maps them to nine recurring data families (e.g., session logs, charger locations, trajectories, socioeconomic indicators, state of charge (SoC), temporal/pricing signals). We review how Geospatial Artificial Intelligence (GeoAI) methods, clustering, probabilistic modeling, agent-based simulation, graph neural networks, and equity/accessibility diagnostics, are deployed within each lens, and where definitions of “charging behavior” diverge or align. Applying this typology reveals persistent blind spots: limited integration of built-environment context (land use, amenity density, urban form), weak linkage between user/session data and neighborhood characteristics, and sparse treatment of seasonality and temperature on charging time, location choice, and delivered energy. We demonstrate how multi-scale coupling of activity spaces with charger networks, equity-aware siting that fuses demographic vulnerability with utilization dynamics, and context-adaptive demand models that join SoC, travel trajectories, and time-of-use (TOU) pricing can close these gaps. By clarifying constructs, datasets, and methodological pathways, the framework enables urban geographers, transportation planners, and energy modelers to co-design user-centric and equitable charging ecosystems.
