# Travel-Demand-Driven Mapping of EV Charging Demand and Supply Adequacy: A London Case Study

**Author Information**  
Jizhou Tang<sup>1</sup>, Xiaowei Gao<sup>2</sup>, Tao Cheng<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> SpaceTimeLab, University College London  

<sup>2</sup> SpaceTimeLab, Imperial College London  



## Abstract

Rapid electric-vehicle (EV) adoption is intensifying the need for evidence-based planning of public charging infrastructure, yet charging demand is rarely observed directly at fine spatial and temporal scales. This study develops a travel-demand-driven framework to quantify EV charging (EVC) demand and evaluate supply adequacy across London. Using anonymised vehicle GPS traces, we identify each driver’s likely home area and restore daily travel trajectories on the road network to estimate usage intensity. For each LSOA, we derive monthly average car use as total distance travelled by resident users. We then combine these mobility-derived distances with area-level EV ownership shares (from census and related official statistics) to infer EV vehicle-kilometres travelled and translate them into electricity demand for charging. On the supply side, we integrate the geographic distribution and rated power of public charge points and construct a queueing-based throughput model to approximate the maximum daily energy that local chargers can realistically deliver under congestion and availability constraints. By comparing inferred demand with modelled supply, we generate city-wide maps indicating where charging provision appears adequate and where shortfalls are likely, providing an interpretable diagnostic layer for planners. The proposed network provides reproducible, spatially explicit demand inputs for subsequent EVC siting and capacity optimisation, supporting sustainable urban mobility planning.



Keywords: EV Charging,  GPS Trajectories,  Home Detection,  Charging Demand Mapping,  Queueing Model
