# Extreme Hydrothermal Fluctuations Regulate the Carbon Storage Over Two Decades in Ecological Engineering Zones of China

**Author Information**  
Yang Li<sup>1</sup>, Yaochen Qin<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Henan University  



## Abstract

As a key stabilizer of the Earth’s climate system, terrestrial ecosystem carbon storage is essential for global ecological security and sustainable human development. In recent decades, the increasing frequency and intensity of extreme weather events have substantially disrupted the stability of terrestrial carbon storage, yet the nonlinear and synergistic effects of multiple extreme climate indices remain poorly understood. This study simulates terrestrial carbon storage by constructing spatially dynamic carbon density datasets coupling with secondary land-use classifications to identify trends and abrupt. Using 13 extreme climate indices defined by the ETCCDI and attribution models that account for spatial heterogeneity and nonlinearity to quantify their contributions to carbon storage dynamics The results show that carbon storage in China's terrestrial ecosystems exhibited an overall downward trend over two decades (24.2 Pg C 10-3 y-1, P<0.01), with pronounced losses in northern China. Abrupt changes in carbon storage within ecological engineering zones were temporally clustered, and their decline rates were generally lower than the national average. The influence of extreme climate indices can be categorized into two dominant control modes: water-driven enhancement and heat-induced stress. Humidification-related extreme climate indicators, including total precipitation, promote carbon accumulation, whereas extreme thermal indicators, such as summer days, accelerate carbon loss. These processes generate a spatially differentiated pattern characterized by water-driven increases in the north and heat-induced losses in the south. This study highlights regional disparities in carbon storage response to extreme climate events and provides critical insights for enhancing terrestrial carbon sequestration under climate change scenarios.



Keywords: Terrestrial carbon storage,  Extreme climate indices,  Nonlinearity,  Spatial heterogeneity
