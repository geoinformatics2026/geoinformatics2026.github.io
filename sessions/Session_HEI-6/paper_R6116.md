# Assessing the Impact of Emergency Food Service Disruptions on Accessibility: A Geospatial Analysis of Food Banks During a Crisis

**Author Information**  
Hari Sannamuri<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> University of central Oklahoma  



## Abstract

Food insecurity remains a critical issue, particularly during times of crisis when emergency food services face disruptions. This study analyzes changes in food bank accessibility using geospatial methods, focusing on the effects of service closures and transportation barriers. By applying an accessibility model to pre- and post-pandemic data, we reveal that even a small percentage of food bank closures resulted in a significant decline in overall access, disproportionately affecting suburban and transit-dependent populations. Children were identified as the most impacted group due to the reduction of school-based food programs. Our findings highlight the need for resilient food distribution systems, improved transportation planning, and policy interventions to mitigate future accessibility challenges.
