# Geospatial Heterogeneity in Policy Outcomes: Assessing the Health Impacts of Energy and Environmental Interventions in China

**Author Information**  
Zhaoyin Liu<sup>1</sup>, Linyan Li<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Department of Data Science, City University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

Background Building sustainable and resilient societies requires rigorous evaluation of the public health outcomes of environmental policies. However, large-scale interventions may exhibit profound geospatial heterogeneity across different regions and local scales. This report synthesizes three national-scale spatial Health Impact Assessment (HIA) studies in China to quantify policy impacts and examine their spatial distribution. The analyses cover two major national initiatives: a clean heating campaign across 88 northern cities and national land greening targets across 98 major cities. Method We developed a spatially explicit analytical framework integrating multi-source datasets on air quality, greenness, rooftop solar potential, population distribution, and energy demand. Policy impacts were quantified using a combination of quasi-experimental and modelling approaches, including Difference-in-Differences for clean heating, Generalized Additive Models for greening targets, and multi-objective optimization coupled with atmospheric simulation for RPV deployment. Health benefits were estimated by linking environmental changes to established exposure–response relationships. Result Across the three policy domains, we identify a consistent pattern of pronounced geospatial heterogeneity in both environmental improvements and associated health benefits. Rather than following administrative or policy boundaries, the realized benefits are strongly shaped by the spatial alignment, or misalignment, between intervention intensity, baseline environmental conditions, and population exposure. In particular, densely populated urban regions frequently emerge as zones where marginal environmental improvements translate into disproportionately large health gains, while areas with strong resource endowments do not always yield the highest social returns. Conclusion Our results reveal systematic geographic disparities in the impact of policy implementation. By employing spatial analysis to account for local geographical contexts, this research provides stakeholders with more tailored, evidence-based recommendations. Acknowledging these spatial heterogeneities is vital for developing context-specific strategies that promote spatial equity and foster truly resilient societies.
