# Reliability-Oriented Urban Road Collapse Risk Assessment via InSAR–LiDAR Fusion, Dynamic Cloud-Model Grading, and LLM-Assisted Diagnosis

**Author Information**  
Zhen Liu<sup>1</sup>, Mei-Po Kwan<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Chinese University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

Urban road collapse presents growing threats to traffic safety and sustainable city operations, highlighting the need for proactive risk identification and early warning. This study proposes an integrated, mechanism-informed framework for urban road collapse risk assessment and forecasting that combines physical simulation, multi-source deformation monitoring, uncertainty-aware prediction, and explainable decision support. First, finite-element simulations of cavity evolution and pavement response are conducted to establish a deformation-indicator system that captures key stages of hazard initiation and progression, complemented by embedded sensing of surface and base-layer deformation. Second, time-series InSAR and multi-epoch LiDAR deformation products are aligned within a road-mileage reference and fused to estimate deformation states and associated uncertainties, enabling trend forecasting along road sections. Third, a novel risk grading approach is developed by integrating dynamic entropy weighting, a coupled two-dimensional cloud model, and reliability-constrained grading criteria to support both current risk classification and future risk level prediction. Finally, a rule-constrained large-language-model module incorporates contextual information such as construction history, climate conditions, traffic volume, and loading together with observed and predicted deformation to generate auditable, interpretable risk reports and actionable recommendations. The proposed framework provides a coherent pathway from mechanism-based indicators to uncertainty-aware risk grading and decision support, facilitating data-driven management of urban road collapse risks.
