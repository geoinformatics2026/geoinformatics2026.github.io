# Study on Land Subsidence Characteristics in Guangzhou Metro Protection Zones Based on Time-Series InSAR and GIS Spatial Analysis

**Author Information**  
Qilin Hu<sup>1</sup>, Junxia Wushan<sup>2</sup>, Sihuang Chen<sup>1</sup>, Simin Hao<sup>2</sup>, Sen He<sup>2</sup>, Fei Wang<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Guangzhou Metro Design Research Institute Co., Ltd.  

<sup>2</sup> Institute of Safety Science and Technology, Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School  



## Abstract

This study performs a spatiotemporal analysisand multi-dimensional attribution of land subsidence withinGuangzhou's urban rail transit zones. Utilizing TS-InSAR (SBASand PS-InSAR) on multi-source SAR imagery, we identified a "Stable North, Subsiding South" paradigm. While the urbancore remains stable, extreme subsidence exceeding 280 mm/yearpersists in the rapidly developing Panyu and Nansha districts.Integrating GIS and machine learning, we quantified thedriving mechanisms: a superposition of thick (15-40m) marine-terrestrial soft soils and anthropogenic pressures, including heavybuilding loads, groundwater extraction, and metro-induced socio-agglomeration. Tectonic faults further catalyze localized differ-ential settlement. We propose a lightweight, intuitive analyticalframework that bypasses the complexity of traditional geotech-nical modeling. This approach provides essential spatial decisionsupport for operational safety, early warning, and sustainableinfrastructural planning in high-density deltaic metropolises.Index Terms-Time-Series InSAR; land subsidence; urban railtransit; Geographic Information Systems; spatial attribution;Pearl River Delta; soft soil consolidation.
