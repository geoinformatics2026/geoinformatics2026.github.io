# A Multi-dimensional Evaluation Framework for Urban Park Service Performance and Spatial Equity: A Case Study of Shanghai

**Author Information**  
Dayun Sun<sup>1</sup>, Shenjun Yao<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> ECNU  



## Abstract

Urban parks are vital public green spaces for residents' well-being and fostering sustainable urban environments. However, disparities in the quality and spatial distribution of park resources can lead to environmental injustice. This study aims to establish a geo-analytical framework that integrates theoretical service pressure, actual usage vitality, and intrinsic park quality. Using Shanghai as a case study, we systematically evaluate park service performance and diagnose the equity of spatial configuration. At first, the intrinsic quality of each park was quantified using the Shannon Diversity Index. Then, the Inverted Two-Step Floating Catchment Area (i2SFCA) model was employed, incorporating fine-grained community population data and transportation networks, to estimate the theoretical service pressure for each park. Subsequently, theoretical pressure was juxtaposed against actual vitality density derived from annual visitor data to identify parks exhibiting significant deviations in service performance. Finally, multiple regression analysis was conducted to examine the association between the comprehensive performance of parks and the socio-economic attributes of surrounding communities. The results indicate a significant spatial mismatch between the distribution of park quality and areas of potential high demand. Primary environmental inequities manifest more notably in the quality of parks rather than merely in their quantity. In central districts characterized by an aging population, communities inhabited by vulnerable groups, may possess a sufficient quantity of small-scale parks. However, the comprehensive performance scores of these facilities are significantly lower. This highlights the systemic barriers these groups face in accessing high-quality recreational services. Based on these findings, targeted improvement strategies are proposed, including the precise regeneration of underperforming parks and optimizing new resource allocation to bridge service gaps.We provide a scientific basis for the renewal of existing stocks and the equitable allocation of urban parks, thereby promoting the precise regeneration and fair distribution of urban green space resources.



Keywords: Urban park,  i2SFCA,  Environmental equity,  Spatial analysis,  Service performance
