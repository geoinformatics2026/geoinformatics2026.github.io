# Spatiotemporal Evolution and Topographic Anchoring of Antarctic Deep-water Polynyas

**Author Information**  
Xiaowen Luo<sup>1</sup>, Xiaole Fan<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> State Key Laboratory of Submarine Geoscience, Second Institute of Oceanography, Ministry of Natural Resources  

<sup>2</sup> Ocean College, Zhejiang University  



## Abstract

Antarctic deep-water polynyas are critical drivers of deep ocean ventilation, exerting profound impacts on the global thermohaline circulation and biogeochemical cycles. However, traditional binary identification methods often overlook transitional states in their life cycle, and a systematic analysis of their circum-Antarctic distribution patterns remains limited. Utilizing high-resolution multi-source spatial data from 2003 to 2022—including the Daily Edge of Each Polynya in Antarctic (DEEP-AA) remote sensing dataset, GLORYS12V1 3D ocean reanalysis products, and BedMachine Antarctica v3 bathymetry—this study systematically investigates the spatiotemporal evolution of these open-ocean polynyas. Our spatial analysis successfully redefined the geographic extent of eight recurring deep-water polynyas, revealing that their distribution is strictly anchored to prominent seafloor topographic features such as seamounts, ridges, and fracture zones. To explain this spatial inevitability, we propose a "Heat Reservoir-Transport-Mixing" conceptual dynamic framework. Topographic steering and mesoscale eddies mechanically lift the Circumpolar Deep Water (CDW) warm core to the base of the mixed layer, serving as vertical heat pumps that sustain these polynyas at specific geographic coordinates. Crucially, we introduce the concept of the "Silent Period," which reshapes the current understanding of the polynya life cycle. During phases typically classified as "closed" due to high sea ice concentration (>60%), the region is not dormant. Instead, highly efficient air-sea heat exchange persists through thin ice (<10 cm), driving continuous brine rejection. This cryptic "Silent Period" acts as a critical saline preconditioning stage, actively preparing the water column for dramatic overturning events and the modification of Antarctic Bottom Water (AABW). This study provides vital observational constraints and spatial analytical frameworks for correcting parameterization biases of polar processes in global climate models.



Keywords: Antarctic Deep-water polynyas,  Topographic anchoring,  Silent Period,  Multi-source remote sensing
