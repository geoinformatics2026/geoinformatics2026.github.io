# Improving remote estimation of phytoplankton primary production with the aid of Geostationary satellite observations

**Author Information**  
Wei Yang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Chiba university  



## Abstract

Remote estimation of phytoplankton primary production (PP) has advanced significantly over recent decades by leveraging satellite observations and sophisticated PP models. However, monthly mean upstream products from polar-orbiting satellites (e.g., MODIS) are commonly used to generate PP products, raising a concern about whether neglecting diurnal or daily PP variabilities may compromise the accuracy of monthly- and annual-scale quantifications. In this study, we aim to investigate this concern by comparing PP quantified using high-frequency data at multiple timescales. A theoretical time-resolved model was utilized for PP modeling, driven by either diurnal photosynthetically available radiation (PAR) from the Advanced Himawari Imager (AHI) onboard Himawari-8 (H8) or daily PAR from MODIS. Preliminary evaluation against in situ measurements corroborated the superiority of AHI-based daily PP estimation over MODIS, attributed to the robustness of AHI PAR data across varying sky conditions. Satellite IPP products were generated in the full-disk area of H8 between 2016 and 2019 under “daily-to-monthly-to-annual” (DtA) and “monthly-to-annual” (MtA) scenarios for comparison, using other requisite daily and gap-free biogeochemical products. Our analysis unveiled moderate spatiotemporal discrepancies between DtA-based IPP products from AHI and MODIS, confirming an overestimation in MODIS-derived monthly (< 8%) and annual total PP (~5%). In contrast, under the MtA scenario, MODIS substantially overestimated monthly (~14–30%) and annual total PP (~20%) and gave biased temporal trends (~1.3–1.6 times higher) compared to DtA-based PP estimates of AHI. The discrepancies between IPP products were largely subject to the cloud induced variabilities in daily PAR products and ocean color data coverage. This study emphasizes the necessity of modeling PP at finer timescales using high-frequency observations and provides insights for improving PP quantification with the aid of geostationary satellites.
