# Enhanced visualization service for spatiotemporal process of disaster chain based on virtual geographic environment

**Author Information**  
Lin Fu<sup>1</sup>, Junjie Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Gaoyun Shen<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> SouthWest Petroleum University  



## Abstract

Providing realistic simulation and visualization services for disaster chain events is of great significance for scientific disaster prevention and mitigation. However, existing disaster chain visualizations focus on single disaster types without adequately considering chain reactions. They also lack the integration of numerical simulations with geographic scenario elements, making them insufficient for emergency management decision-making needs. Therefore, proposes a spatiotemporal process enhancement visualization service for disaster chains based on virtual geographic environments (VGE). Firstly, explores the numerical simulation technology for the movement process of the Landslides - Barrier lakes - Outburst flood (LBO) disaster chain under the constraint of disaster information. By integrating modeling numerical simulation of the disaster chain with the VGE of disasters, research the spatiotemporal process evolution laws of the disaster chain. Proposes an enhanced visualization method for the spatiotemporal process of the disaster chain considering the evolution laws. Enhance the representation of the spatiotemporal evolution process of disaster chain through multi-level semantic constraints and multidimensional visual variables. Based on this, questionnaire surveys and eye-tracking experiments were conducted to design an evaluation methodology for assessing the visualization effectiveness of disaster scenarios. Evaluates the visualization effect of the spatiotemporal processes of the disaster chain. A question-answering module was developed using Large Language Models, which integrates disaster chain scenario information with network data to enable efficient comprehension and retrieval of disaster information. Finally, a prototype system was developed and case studies were conducted for experimental analysis. Results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively achieves dynamic simulation and enhanced visualization of the spatiotemporal processes from incubation, occurrence, and development to disaster impact across the disaster chain. By providing shared services for simulating and visualizing these spatiotemporal processes, users could rapidly, clearly, and efficiently access relevant disaster information, thereby delivering technical support for emergency response and management decision-making along the disaster chain.
