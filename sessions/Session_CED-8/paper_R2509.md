# Research on the Coordination Mechanism of the Human-Land System under Disaster Risk: A Case Study of Jilong Town in Southeastern Tibet

**Author Information**  
Xugong Jia<sup>1</sup>, Jue Wang<sup>1</sup>, Ming Chang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Chengdu University of Technology  



## Abstract

This section employs an Agent-Based Modeling (ABM) simulation on the NetLogo platform to empirically analyze the coordination mechanism of the human-land system in Jilong Town under geological disaster risk. Grounded in the Social-Ecological Systems (SES) framework, the model simulates the interactions between key agents—local residents, migrant merchants, and government entities—within the context of dynamic resource systems and governance structures. The simulation specifically explores system responses to stochastic geological hazard events, quantifying their disruptive impacts on critical socioeconomic indicators and spatial resource allocation. Results generate post-disaster recovery trajectories, visualized through impact and recovery curves, which reveal the differential resilience and adaptive capacities among agent groups. The analysis identifies pivotal factors within the SES—such as risk perception heterogeneity, resource accessibility, and governance intervention timing—that significantly influence the system's recovery pace and long-term coordination stability. These findings provide a dynamic, mechanistic understanding of human-land system interactions in high-risk mountainous areas, offering evidence-based insights for enhancing systemic resilience and informing targeted policy interventions for disaster risk reduction and sustainable development.



Keywords: Geological Disaster Risk,  Human-Land System Coordination Mechanism,  SES,  ABM
