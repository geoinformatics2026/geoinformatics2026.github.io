# A Flood Disaster Chain Observation Planning Method Based on Space–Air–Ground Multi-Agent Negotiation

**Author Information**  
Zongran Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Yunbo Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Bingshu Huang<sup>1</sup>, Ke Wang<sup>1</sup>, Chuli Hu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> China University of Geosciences (Wuhan)  



## Abstract

In flood multi-hazard and disaster chain scenarios, complex spatiotemporal interactions and cascading effects require large-scale, continuous, and dynamic observation. Traditional disaster monitoring planning methods primarily rely on sequential planning and fully distributed planning based on time-window task allocation, assuming fixed occurrence times and spatial scopes. These approaches often lead to inefficient resource utilization or delayed responses when confronting multiple types of sudden disasters with uncertain occurrence times. To address this challenge, this study defines a Multi-Risk Area Continuous Coverage Planning Problem (MRACCP) based on spatiotemporal reasoning risk heatmaps within the disaster chain, enabling high-quality acquisition of disaster process information with greater impact and higher likelihood of occurrence. Furthermore, this study constructs a multi-agent negotiation framework for coordinating aerial, terrestrial, and space observation resources to address the MRACCP problem. This framework comprises a task negotiation layer and a platform planning layer, featuring a virtual negotiator agent for inter-platform task coordination and platform planning center agents responsible for specific task execution and scheduling. For the subproblems of agent task negotiation, allocation, and execution scheduling, this study proposes a hierarchical negotiation solution method for disaster chain observation schemes. Specifically, for the task coordination and selection processes at the overall task negotiation layer and platform planning layer, a multi-agent iterative negotiation planning process is designed. Building upon this, a platform negotiation method guided by marginal contribution assessment (SA-MCGNA) is introduced. At the platform planning layer, distinct planning algorithms are developed for three platform categories using greedy and heuristic approaches. Real-world experiments validate the effectiveness of the multi-agent task negotiation process and SA-MCGNA method. Results demonstrate that the proposed multi-agent negotiation framework and hierarchical negotiation solution significantly enhance the efficiency of coordinated task planning throughout the entire disaster chain process.
