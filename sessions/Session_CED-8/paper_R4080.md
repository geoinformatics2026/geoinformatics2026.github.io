# Spatial Relationship-Aware Observation Event Logic Graph for Flood Disaster Chain: Construction and Observation Planning Application

**Author Information**  
Bingshu Huang<sup>1</sup>, Yunbo Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Chuli Hu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> China university of geosciences (Wuhan)  



## Abstract

Comprehensive and timely monitoring is essential for effective disaster mitigation, particularly for complex flood disaster chains involving interconnected events like heavy rainfall, breaches, and landslides. Determining “where,” “what,” and “how” to observe remains a significant challenge for full-chain monitoring. To address this, it is essential to construct a knowledge base that models the causes and logic knowledge governing the occurrence and spatiotemporal development of both disasters and observation events. Adopting the Event Logic Graph (ELG) as the knowledge representation scheme, this research proposes the Flood Disaster-Chain Observation Event Logic Graph (OELG) that considers spatial relationship features governing inter-disaster interactions.A “Extraction-Generalization-Reasoning” three-stage framework is developed for progressive graph construction: (1) extracting “causal-spatiotemporal” relationships from heterogeneous texts by coupling LLMs with geospatial computing to form a historical event graph(OELG-history); (2) generalizing fragmented historical instances into universal logic patterns and spatial transfer rules to create an abstracted event logic graph(OELG-abstract); (3) reasoning potential disaster chain paths under geographical constraints and automatically generate instantiated observation requirements based on actual disaster events, forming an instantiated event graph (OELG-instance). The 2021 “7.20” Zhengzhou flood disaster chain was used as a case study to validate the framework. By constructing the OELG-instance based on the OELG-abstract, this research predict the spatial extents of various secondary disaster events and calculate the key observation areas for associated observation behavior events. The results demonstratethat the calculated key observation areas align closely with the actual disaster conditions, providing crucial knowledge support for transitioning the observation planning paradigm from “post-disaster passive response” to “pre-disaster proactive foresight”.
