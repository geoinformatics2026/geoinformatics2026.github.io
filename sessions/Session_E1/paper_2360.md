# Resilience and Sustainability in 3D: Modeling Human-Environment Interactions with Geospatial Digital Twins

**Author Information**  
Lei Zou<sup>1</sup>, Debayan Mandal<sup>2</sup>, Zongrong Li<sup>1</sup>, Yifan Yang<sup>1</sup>, Mingzheng Yang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Texas A&M University  

<sup>2</sup> Arizona State University  



## Abstract

Resilience and sustainability increasingly depend on our society’s ability to understand and manage short-/long-term human-environment interactions that unfold not only across space and time, but also along the vertical dimension of cities. Traditional 2D representations of urban environments oversimplify their interactions with human communities, limiting their ability to inform resilient and sustainable design and decision-making. Recent advances in geospatial digital twins, 3D city models, and high-resolution geospatial data offer new opportunities to move beyond surface-based analysis and more realistically capture how people interact with complex urban environments in three dimensions. This research introduces a 3D geospatial perspective for studying disaster resilience and sustainability. Two case studies demonstrate how 3D analysis enabled by geospatial digital twins generates insights that are difficult to obtain using conventional approaches.  The first study develops a Geospatial Digital Twin for Flood Watching and Simulation (FlowsDT) for Galveston, Texas, to improve disaster resilience. FlowsDT simulates hyperlocal 3D flood dynamics, including flood extent, depth, velocity, and duration across space and time. By integrating LiDAR, land cover, urban infrastructure, and coupled hydrodynamic models, FlowsDT enables scenario-based analyses of flooding under various rainfall scenarios, revealing fine-scale building and roadway inundations for resilient infrastructure planning and risk communication. The second study examines window-level exposure to urban greenery using a detailed 3D digital twin of Houston, Texas, to inform sustainable urban environments. By integrating photorealistic 3D tiles, building footprints, and deep-learning-based semantic segmentation, this study quantifies how building height, floor level, and surrounding urban form shape indoor views of nature. Results show that vertical position plays a key role in determining access to green views, and that commonly used 2D greenness metrics only partially capture lived visual experiences.  The findings highlight the importance of integrating 3D geospatial digital twins into urban analysis to support the development of more resilient and sustainable societies.
