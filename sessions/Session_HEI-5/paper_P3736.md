# Optimization of AED Based on Deep Reinforement Learning—Case Study of Haidian District, Beijing

**Author Information**  
Chaofan Zhong<sup>1</sup>, Shaohua Wang<sup>1</sup>, Jingyi Zhou<sup>1</sup>, Haojian Liang<sup>1</sup>, Hao Wang<sup>2</sup>, Chang Liu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Aerospace Information Research Institute  

<sup>2</sup> Henan University  



## Abstract

With the development of the social economy and changes in the lifestyle of the Chinese population-particularly the acceleration of population aging and urbanization-unhealthy lifestyle patterns among residents have become increasingly prominent. Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) has become one of the leading causes of death worldwide. This study is based on the distribution data of Automated External Defibrillator (AED) locations in Haidian District, Beijing, as well as road network data and building footprint data of Haidian District. Combined with other relevant facility POI data and population distribution raster data, spatial analysis, spatial statistics, and GeoDetector methods were employed to conduct an in-depth analysis of the distribution pattern, population coverage, network accessibility, and the influence of related facilities for the existing 1, 053 AED facilities in Haidian District. The results indicate that: (1) The existing AEDs in Haidian District are primarily concentrated in the central and southeastern areas, while the northwestern region has a relatively sparse distribution. This spatial pattern shows a high degree of consistency with the distribution of population, buildings, and related facility POIs. (2) The current number of AEDs in Haidian District is relatively insufficient. The per capita number of AEDS has not yet reached China's existing standard of 100 units per 100, 000 people and therefore requires further optimization and improvement. (3) The population coverage rate of the existing AED facilities in Haidian District is relatively low, and their network accessibility is comparatively poor. In response to the above issues, this study employed a deep reinforcement learning method ReCovNet to optimize the location of newly added AED facilities. By solving the Maximum Covering Location Problem (MCLP), 280 additional AEDs were optimally allocated in Haidian District, resulting in a 9.7% increase in population coverage and a 63.7% improvement in five-minute network accessibility. The findings of this study provide both theoretical and practical recommendations for urban emergency medical facility planning and site selection.
