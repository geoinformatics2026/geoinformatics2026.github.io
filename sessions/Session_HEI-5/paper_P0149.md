# Geospatial Optimization of Local Specialty Catering Facilities Under Hybrid Tourist–Resident Demand: A Case Study of Guilin

## Abstract

Local specialty catering services constitute an important component of urban service systems in tourism-oriented cities, and their demand distribution and service accessibility exhibit pronounced spatial heterogeneity. From a city-scale perspective, this study takes Guilin, a representative tourist city, as the study area to investigate the spatial accessibility characteristics and location optimization of local specialty food establishments. On the demand side, a tourism-resident hybrid demand index is constructed by overlaying tourist clustering intensity and permanent population distribution. In the accessibility analysis stage, service radii are adjusted according to shop ratings, and a gravity-based model is improved to account for differences in service attractiveness. In the location optimization stage, a maximal covering location problem (MCLP)-based model is formulated with the objectives of maximizing relative service population coverage and minimizing rental cost, subject to a constraint defined by the average service radius of Guilin rice noodle shops. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), the Gurobi optimizer, and a Genetic Algorithm (GA) are employed to solve the model and generate the optimal layout for additional facilities. The results indicate that the DRL-based approach achieves objective values close to the optimal solutions obtained by Gurobi while reducing computation time by a factor of five, demonstrating clear advantages in both computational efficiency and coverage performance, and better satisfying service accessibility requirements. This study provides a transferable spatial decision-making framework for optimizing the layout of urban catering facilities in tourism cities, contributing to the economic development of tourism-oriented urban areas.
