# From Circulation Shifts to AI Forecasts: Understanding and Predicting Rainfall in Southern China

**Author Information**  
Zhen Liu<sup>1</sup>, Yaning Wang<sup>1</sup>, Jing Pan<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)  



## Abstract

Southern China has experienced a pronounced interdecadal increase in summer (June–August) precipitation since the early 1990s, while remaining highly vulnerable to heavy rainfall–induced disasters. Improving both physical understanding and forecast skill is therefore essential for disaster mitigation and water resource management.  Our analysis shows that from 1979–1992 to 1993–2006, both extreme precipitation (EP) and non-extreme precipitation increased. Although EP contributes only 26% of climatological rainfall, it explains 43% of the total increase, indicating a growing fractional contribution of extremes. Notably, the increase is primarily driven by higher event frequency rather than stronger event intensity. More frequent monsoon rainfall and tropical cyclone precipitation are associated with strengthened monsoon circulation and increased tropical cyclone passages, both linked to more frequent westward extensions of the western Pacific subtropical high. These results suggest that circulation-controlled increases in rainfall occurrence dominate the interdecadal precipitation rise.  Building on this physical insight, we develop a numerical weather prediction (NWP)-constrained multi-task learning (MTL) framework to improve daily accumulated rainfall forecasts during the rainy season. The model integrates cascaded deep learning classification with dynamical guidance from the WRF model, embedding physical constraints into the learning process. Comparative experiments show that the coupled framework outperforms standalone deep learning and WRF simulations in both heavy-precipitation detection and quantitative prediction, achieving improved stability and physical consistency.  This study bridges dynamical understanding and physics-guided machine learning, advancing both interpretation of regional rainfall changes and operational precipitation forecasting in southern China.
