# Spatiotemporal Divergence and Seasonal Inversion of Daytime and Nighttime Precipitation across China: A Five-Decade Observational Study (1972–2024) from the Geospatial Perspective

**Author Information**  
Jingyi Meng<sup>1</sup>, Haoming Xia<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> 河南大学  



## Abstract

The spatiotemporal evolution of diurnal precipitation patterns represents a key research topic at the intersection of Geographic Information Science and climate–environment studies. Long-term variations in diurnal precipitation are essential for understanding hydrological responses to global warming and supporting GIS-based spatial allocation of water resources. However, long-term, high-resolution investigations into asymmetric trends of daytime and nighttime precipitation over China remain limited, restricting advances in refined climate simulation and geospatial decision-making. From a geospatial perspective, this study systematically examines spatiotemporal divergence and long-term changes of daytime and nighttime precipitation across China, using 1972–2024 observations from 2,399 high-density meteorological stations combined with spatiotemporal analytical methods. The Trend-Free Pre-Whitened Mann-Kendall test is adopted to quantitatively identify spatial trends in daytime precipitation, nighttime precipitation, and their diurnal difference. Results demonstrate that daytime and nighttime precipitation trends over the past five decades show significant spatial heterogeneity and a pronounced seasonal inversion: nighttime precipitation is dominant in spring, autumn, and winter, whereas daytime precipitation prevails in summer. Further spatial analysis indicates a strengthening nocturnal precipitation trend in the Sichuan Basin and the Tibetan Plateau margins, alongside enhanced daytime convective precipitation in other regions. These findings uncover the coupled influences of large-scale monsoon circulations and local topography on spatiotemporal divergence of precipitation, supporting parameter optimization for GIS-integrated regional climate models. This study also provides a scientific basis and technical reference for GIS-enabled water resource management and precise disaster prevention of extreme precipitation under climate change.
