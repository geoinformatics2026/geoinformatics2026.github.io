# Offsetting reservoir emissions with floating solar photovoltaics

**Author Information**  
Xingxing Zhang<sup>1</sup>, Hong Zhu<sup>1</sup>, Zhoupeng Ren<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> IGSNRR  

<sup>2</sup> Institute of Geographic Sciences and Natural Resources Research, Chinese Academy of Sciences  



## Abstract

Reservoirs are a significant yet often overlooked source of greenhouse gas emissions, which are rarely considered in national decarbonization strategies. This oversight underestimates actual emissions and may compromise net-zero emission targets, particularly in countries heavily reliant on hydropower. Here, we present a novel approach to offsetting the overlooked reservoir emissions with floating solar photovoltaics (FPV). By simulating the total emissions from hydropower reservoirs and their onset times, alongside the dynamic performance of FPV, we explore the net-zero benefits of integrating FPV with hydroelectric dams. We analyze how those benefits vary with FPV coverage levels under different climatic scenarios. Our findings show that covering just 5.44%–8.79% of the reservoir surface with FPV could fully offset emissions from reservoirs, enabling Myanmar’s reservoirs to achieve net-zero emissions by 2046. Under this hydro-solar strategy, the electricity demands of Myanmar from 2030 to 2050 can be met, with the possibility of alleviating energy shortages beyond 2050 by increasing FPV coverage. This study holds significant implications for achieving carbon neutrality in hydropower-dependent nations and mitigating the overlooked impact of reservoir-related emissions globally.
