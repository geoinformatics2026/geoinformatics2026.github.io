# An Event Logic Graph for Dynamic Risk Chain Evolution in Historic Urban Areas

**Author Information**  
Shen Chen<sup>1</sup>, Chuli Hu<sup>1</sup>, Ke Wang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> China University of Geosciences  



## Abstract

Effective dynamic evolution analysis of risks in historic urban areas is key to enhancing urban resilience and risk early warning capabilities. Previous studies have predominantly adopted static risk assessment frameworks, which are limited to isolated analyses of single risk events. Consequently, they struggle to reveal the chain transmission mechanisms and dynamic evolution patterns of multiple concurrent risk events. To identify and respond to secondary risks, this study proposes the Risk Event Logic Graph (RELG). By modeling risk events and their logical relationships as nodes and edges, we innovatively constructed a RELG ontology specific to the field of historic urban areas. The RELG-abstract layer was developed by extracting and fusing abstract event logic knowledge from open data sources that conform to the RELG ontology. Subsequently, by integrating geographic knowledge, the RELG-abstract was instantiated within specific geographic environments to form RELG-instances, which serve as the knowledge base for risk early warning chains. When an initial risk event occurs, querying the RELG allows for the real-time generation of dynamic evolution deduction schemes for risk chains, providing decision support for intelligent early warning characterized by dynamic perception and collaborative linkage.



Keywords: Historic Urban Areas,  Event Logic Graph,  Dynamic Risk Evolution
