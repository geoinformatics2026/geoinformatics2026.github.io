# Linking human activities to the U.S. public electric vehicle charging accessibility: a temporal perspective

**Author Information**  
Ruichen Ma<sup>1</sup>, Chengxiang Zhuge<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Department of Land Surveying and Geo-informatics, The Hong Kong Polytechnic University  



## Abstract

Promoting equitable access to public electric vehicle charger system (EVCS) is essential for a more inclusive electric mobility transition and sustainable city. Compared with conventional refueling, extended charging durations highlight the importance of points of interest (POI) as key amenities that support public charging beyond home and workplace settings. However, a critical knowledge gap remains regarding how charging accessibility and equity evolve across both time and space, particularly when charging activities are linked to broader patterns of human activity. This study investigates the evolution of accessibility and spatial equity of public EVCS in the United States (U.S.) from 2014 to 2024, with a focus on population- and facility-based charging accessibility and equity, assessing how easily residents can access EV charging stations either from their places of residence or from various POIs associated with daily activities. We find that public EVCS has rapidly expanded into a clustered and polycentric national network, alongside improving equity. Nonetheless, we identified potential mismatches between charging accessibility and facility types, and with trip purposes. Service disruptions were observed in areas undergoing charger upgrades without transitional safeguards. Furthermore, spatial disparities persisted and continued to widen: urban areas and counties that are Democrat-leaning, with higher GDP and EV adoption, showed higher accessibility and equity as well as faster rates of increase. These findings provide empirical evidence to inform targeted and equity-oriented planning strategies and contribute to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).
