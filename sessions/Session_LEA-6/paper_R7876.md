# Divergent ecosystem responses to climate boundary fluctuations in dryland–humid transition zones

**Author Information**  
Jie Jiang<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Henan University  



## Abstract

Climate boundary fluctuations have emerged as a critical control on ecosystem stability in dryland–humid transition zones under global change, yet their cross-continental response pathways and regulatory mechanisms remain poorly understood. The 400 mm annual isohyet represents a key hydrothermal boundary governing the transition of terrestrial ecosystems from water limitation to energy limitation. Here, we investigate the fluctuation zones of the 400 mm isohyet in China and Australia using multi-source remote sensing and climate datasets. By integrating remote sensing indicators into the Vulnerability Scoping Diagram (VSD) framework, we quantify spatiotemporal dynamics of ecological patterns and identify threshold and nonlinear responses of ecosystem vulnerability to environmental drivers. Results show that the migration rate of the 400 mm isohyet is lower in China than in Australia, indicating greater climate boundary stability. Ecologically, China’s transition zone is dominated by grassland–cropland systems, whereas Australia is characterized by sparse shrubland–grassland systems, with both regions exhibiting pronounced interannual variability and land cover transitions. In Australia, ecological vulnerability is primarily governed by climatic thresholds, decreasing when mean annual temperature is below 21.7 °C and precipitation exceeds 464.2 mm. In contrast, China exhibits both threshold effects and pronounced nonlinear responses, with increased vulnerability observed within a precipitation range of 304.33–449.03 mm. These findings reveal distinct ecosystem response mechanisms to climate boundary fluctuations across continents and provide a scientific basis for ecological risk assessment in dryland–humid transition zones under future climate change.



Keywords: 400 mm isohyet,  dryland–humid transition zones,  ecosystem stability,  shifting climate boundaries,  threshold responses
