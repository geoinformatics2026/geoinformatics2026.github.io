# Risk Assessment of Metro Protection Zones Based on GIS Spatial Overlay and Multi-scale Discretization

**Author Information**  
Ru Zhao<sup>1</sup>, Sen He<sup>2</sup>, Sihuang Chen<sup>1</sup>, Yuchen Jia<sup>3</sup>, Xiang Yang<sup>2</sup>, Fei Wang<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Guangzhou Metro Design Research Institute Co., Ltd.  

<sup>2</sup> Institute of Safety Science and Technology, Tsinghua Shenzhen International Graduate School  

<sup>3</sup> School of Safety Science, Tsinghua University  



## Abstract

As the density of urban rail transit networks increases, the external operation risks within metro protection zones have become increasingly complex. Traditional risk assessment methods primarily rely on a single spatial scale, which struggles to balance the macroscopic demands of operational management and the microscopic requirements of engineering risk control. To address this limitation, this paper proposes a novel GIS-driven multi-scale risk assessment framework for metro protection zones. First, utilizing the spatial analysis capabilities of GIS, three spatial discretization strategies are introduced: inter-station segmentation (macro-level), fixed-length segmentation (meso-level), and original construction method segmentation (micro-level). Second, an evaluation index system comprising seven indicators—such as stratum conditions, structure types, and external operations—is established. Subsequently, quantitative mathematical models are integrated to process heuristic weights and fuzzy membership degrees, ultimately yielding a comprehensive risk grade via a two-dimensional risk matrix. Finally, two typical lines of the Guangzhou Metro are selected as case studies for empirical verification. Through GIS spatial overlay analysis, "consistent high-risk sections" across multiple perspectives are successfully identified. The results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively overcomes the one-sidedness of single-scale evaluations, providing a scientific basis for the refined and hierarchical risk management of metro protection zones.



Keywords: Metro protection zone, Multi-scale discretization, Risk assessment, GIS spatial analysis
