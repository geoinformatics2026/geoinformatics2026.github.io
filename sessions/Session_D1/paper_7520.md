# Optimizing Global Solar and Wind Integration through Trans-continental Grid Interconnection: A Spatiotemporal Modeling Approach

**Author Information**  
Hou Jiang<sup>1</sup>, Ling Yao<sup>1</sup>, Tang Liu<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Institute of Geographic Sciences and Natural Resources Research, CAS  



## Abstract

Achieving carbon neutrality requires unprecedented penetration of variable renewable energy, yet geographic mismatches between resource availability and electricity demand pose fundamental challenges to grid stability and economic viability. This study presents a comprehensive geospatial optimization framework for designing globally interconnected solar-wind power systems. By integrating 500-meter resolution land suitability analysis with hourly meteorological data, we quantify the technical potential of interconnectable renewable resources and identify optimal deployment strategies across multiple temporal phases. Our multi-objective optimization model balances three competing objectives—maximizing renewable penetration, minimizing curtailment, and reducing infrastructure costs—to derive optimal configurations of generation, storage, and transmission assets. Results demonstrate that global interconnection can reduce initial investments by 15.6% compared to regionally isolated systems while achieving over 68% solar-wind penetration. The optimized pathway transitions from adjacent regional connections in the 2030s toward a fully interconnected global grid by 2050, with significant reductions in storage requirements and generation volatility. Stress testing reveals robust resilience against climate extremes and supply disruptions, though geopolitical fragmentation poses substantial risks. These findings provide actionable insights for international energy planning and underscore the critical role of spatial optimization in enabling high-renewable futures.
