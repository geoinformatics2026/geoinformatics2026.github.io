# Global scalability of rooftop photovoltaic charging for electric vehicles

**Author Information**  
Linlin You<sup>1</sup>, Rui Zhu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Sun Yat-Sen University  

<sup>2</sup> Nanjing Normal University  



## Abstract

Harnessing rooftop photovoltaic (PV) generation to power electric vehicles (EVs) can significantly advance the renewable energy transition and carbon mitigation. Yet, the mismatch between electricity production and charging demand, exacerbated by rapid EV adoption, challenges its scalability. Here, we evaluate the spatial and temporal scalability of PV-powered EV charging across 40 global cities, analysing 3.38 billion charging records from 22,000 charging piles. Under three charging strategies, daily generated electricity exceeded daily charging demand by 1-104 times across all urban microgrids throughout 2023, and influential factors affecting daily charging capacity across microgrids of varying sizes strictly followed an exponential scaling law. By 2050, projections show annual PV generation doubling in each city (1.6-434.7 TWh), supported by rooftop area expansions aligned with the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways and improved PV efficiency. Meanwhile, annual charging demand is expected to rise 4–1,759 times (1.5–10,451.7 GWh), driven by increased EV adoption under International Energy Agency scenarios. Despite these increases and global imbalances, each city’s generation-to-demand ratio will decline but remain sufficient (4-25,000) to meet 2050 charging demands. Our study provides insight into scaling rooftop PV for urban EV charging, guiding sustainable city-scale EV penetration.
