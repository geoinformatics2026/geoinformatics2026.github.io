# Stage-dependent scaling of travel distance shaped by 3D urban form

**Author Information**  
Yangzi Che<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Sun Yat-sen University  



## Abstract

Urban shapes mobility demand and transport energy consumption, yet most existing assessments rely on two-dimensional metrics that overlook the vertical organization of cities. Here we applied a floor-area-weighted 3D mobility-distance metric to 6,582 cities (>650 million buildings) across 173 countries and quantified global patterns of mobility space and examine how vertical urban morphology reshapes mobility scaling relationships. Results show that incorporating vertical structure substantially revises mobility characterization. On average, 3D mobility distances differ from conventional 2D estimates by 15.4%, and 79% of cities exhibit distance compression, indicating that vertical agglomeration tends to reduce effective mobility space. However, rapidly urbanizing megacities in developing regions display an opposite pattern—termed “volumetric sprawl”—in which dispersed vertical development expands mobility distances despite high density. We further identify a stage-dependent scaling transition in population–distance relationships, defined by thresholds of approximately 2.7 million residents and $12,476 GDP per capita. Across this transition, the scaling exponent of mobility distance declines from 0.52 to 0.36, indicating increasing spatial efficiency as cities evolve from small low-income systems to large high-income urban regimes. Decomposition using the 3D-BASE model shows that mobility distance in low-income cities is driven primarily by floor-area expansion and urban sprawl, with building height partially constraining this growth, whereas in mature urban systems building density and spatial clustering dominate and the marginal effect of vertical intensification weakens. By incorporating stage-dependent structural transitions into SSP-based projections, we simulate future transport energy pathways and show that these transitions could reduce cumulative global transport energy consumption by 1.50-3.14 years of 2020-equivalent energy use between 2020 and 2100, independent of technological or behavioral change. This study clarifies how vertical urban morphology shapes transport mobility and shows that 3D urban form functions as a stage-dependent climate mitigation lever whose window is rapidly narrowing.
