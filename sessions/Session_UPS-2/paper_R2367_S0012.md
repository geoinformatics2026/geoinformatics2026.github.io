# Decoding China’s Migration Flows: Spatial Inequality and Determinants through Entropy Measure and Eigenvector Filtering

**Author Information**  
Jiawen Lv<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Nanjing University  



## Abstract

The 11th goal of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) is Sustainable Cities and Communities. Building sustainable cities and human settlements requires a balance between the supply and demand of regional resources such as housing, transportation and public services, which is closely related to the size and structure of the local population. With the rapid development of urbanization, migration has become a significant factor influencing the size and structure of regional population. Based on interprovincial migration data from the national population surveys from 1985 to 2020, this paper constructs a Beta regression model for the probability of migration flow and applies eigenvector spatial filtering, to measure the entropy and evaluate the spatial inequality of China's interprovincial migration, analyze the changing pattern, the driving factors and the spatial autocorrelation effects of interprovincial migration in China during 1985–2020. The results show that, China's interprovincial migration exhibits a phased evolution characterized by a transition from dispersion to concentration and then to a multi-center network pattern. There is also a dynamic evolution of the driving factors of interprovincial migration in China. The disparity of regional economic development was the primary driving force of migration in the early stage, while in the later stage, the influence of economic factors was relatively weakened, and migration was affected by multiple factors. Population size consistently play a role in the whole process. The impact of distance shows a diminishing trend. A high level of education of destinations can create a "hidden barrier" that filters in-migrants. The influence of spatial autocorrelation exhibits similar spatial patterns across different periods, though its intensity varies. Spatial autocorrelation effects tend to substantially increase the spatial inequality of migration flows. Exploring the patterns and trend of migration can provide a scientific basis for policy-making related to regional coordinated development in China.
