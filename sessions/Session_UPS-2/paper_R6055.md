# Analysis of the Driving Mechanism of Interprovincial Population Migration in China  Based on the Spatiotemporal Durbin Panel Model

**Author Information**  
Yuqi Chen<sup>1</sup>, Yinxia Pu<sup>1</sup>, Wenjin Wu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Nanjing University, School of Geography and Ocean Science  

<sup>2</sup> NanJing University, School of Geography and Ocean Science  



## Abstract

Since the launch of reform and opening-up, interprovincial population migration in China has generally expanded in scale. Population migration is driven by multiple socioeconomic factors and exhibits spatiotemporal path dependence. Existing studies are often confined to analyses of either temporal or spatial dimensions, overlooking the influence of spatiotemporal interaction mechanisms. Furthermore, modeling population migration frequently neglects uncertainty issues, limiting deeper understanding of the complexity and evolutionary mechanisms of regional migration processes. Based on China’s interprovincial migration data from 1985 to 2020, this study integrates multidimensional variables to construct a spatiotemporal Durbin panel model capable of identifying and quantifying spatiotemporal path dependencies. It employs Bayesian Model Averaging (BMA) and Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods for probabilistic sampling and weighted integration, while utilizing a spatiotemporal effects estimation framework to quantify the driving mechanisms of origin, destination, and network effects across synchronous, short-term, and long-term periods. Findings reveal: ① BMA effectively reduces the arbitrariness of single models while identifying more robust drivers of migration. ② The primary drivers of interprovincial migration in China are proportion of working-age population and real average wages of urban employees. Specifically: origin region population size and proportion of working-age population form a dominant “push accumulation effect” ; while the GDP and wages of the destination region constitute a dual “income-opportunity” pull. In the network effect, labor supply exhibits negative spatial spillover, whereas wage variable shows a positive correlation.③ The migration process exhibits pronounced spatiotemporal path dependence. Spatiotemporal spillover effects peak contemporaneously, gradually decaying in the short term before stabilizing long-term; the cumulative spatiotemporal impact of network effects is pronounced, reflecting the persistent interactive influence of neighboring regions. By quantifying multidimensional spatiotemporal effects, this study comprehensively analyzes the role of regional factors within interprovincial migration networks and their spillover effects, providing scientific foundations for building a population-balanced society.
