# The Synergistic Effects of Warning Message Design and Dynamic Flood Risk on Evacuation Decisions: A Multi-Scenario Virtual Reality Experiment

**Author Information**  
Shiyan Zhai<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Henan University  



## Abstract

Flood warning message is regarded as the most effective measure for protecting human life from potentially lethal threats. Despite growing research on evaluating and modeling the impact of early warnings, there remains a critical gap in understanding how to design messages that effectively prompt public response during rapidly evolving floods. To address this gap, this study employs a Virtual Reality (VR) experiment to assess the synergistic effects of warning messages design and dynamic flood risk on evacuation decision across multiple scenarios. A total of 108 participants experienced three baseline and twelve warning-enabled dynamic pluvial flood scenarios. We systematically designed warning message release time, flood depth, message granularity (abbreviated vs. detailed), and evacuation instruction message type (none, suggested, compulsory). Generalized Linear Mixed Model (GLMM) analyses revealed that warning message significantly elevated evacuation rates, with rates escalating alongside flood risk (averaging 26.04%, 48.46%, and 89.09% at 0 m, 0.2 m, and 0.5 m flood depths, respectively). Crucially, while both detailed content and compulsory instructions were key determinants, their effectiveness was moderated by the level of flood risk. Demographically, prior flood experience positively influenced evacuation, while females exhibited a lower evacuation probability than males; age and education showed no significant effects. This research based on virtual reality experiments elucidates the synergistic mechanism between warning message and dynamic flood risk, providing actionable insights for optimizing context-aware urban pluvial flood warning systems.
