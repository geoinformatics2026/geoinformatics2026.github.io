# Dynamic Environments Impacts on Mental Health in England

**Author Information**  
Yijing Li<sup>1</sup>, Hector Menendez Benito<sup>1</sup>, Wei Fang<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> King's College London  

<sup>2</sup> Nanjing University of Information Science & Technology  



## Abstract

This research discusses the decade-long trajectory changes of mental health conditions in England, both acute and chronic conditions, from 2011 to 2022 against local risks of climate disasters, flooding and heatwave. It features by efforts on big data collection and integration, cutting-edged methods Multiscale Geographically and Temporally Weighted Regression (MGTWR) and Deep Temporal Clustering (DTC) to capture the complicated dynamic influences from varied environments and geographies. It is found that acute mental health conditions were more influenced by and sensitive to climate risks such as heatwaves and floodings and will be affected by social grades and deprivation; chronic mental health conditions were more related to local green/blue infrastructures and air qualities. There were regional disparities among the mental health statuses and driving forces, with identified clusters on basis of the categorical influential factors' trends. The selected methods can better differentiate scale effects for diverse factors, and proved the strong informative ability to inform localized climate adaption strategies.
