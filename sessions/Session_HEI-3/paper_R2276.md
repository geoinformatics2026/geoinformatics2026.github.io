# Compound temperature-humidity extremes drive up acute mental health outcomes: A 12-year case-crossover metropolitan study

**Author Information**  
Xin Liu<sup>1</sup>, Yongsheng Wang<sup>2</sup>, Bin Zhu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Southern university of science and technology  

<sup>2</sup> Southern University of Science and Technology  



## Abstract

Objective: To investigate the comprehensive associations between compound temperature and humidity extremes and acute mental health conditions. Methods: Mental disorders (MDs)-related emergency medical services (EMS) calls data (2011-2023) were from Shenzhen. A time-stratified case-crossover design with non-linear conditional logistic regression was used to examined the intermediate and lagged associations between two compound temperature-humidity indices (the humidex and heat index) and MDs-related EMS calls. Results: In total, 33,568 MDs cases were screened out from 1,538,611 EMS calls. A significant increase in the odds of MDs-related EMS calls was observed with rising humidex and heat index values. The odds ratio (OR) for MDs-related EMS calls was 1.10 (95% confidence interval (CI): 1.05-1.15) for humidex extremes and 1.10 (95% CI: 1.06-1.14) for the heat index extremes. The effect was strongest at a lag of one day and gradually declined by lag day three. Higher risks were observed among younger individuals and those at workplace. Notably, we identified a high-risk threshold (OR>1.15) of MDs-related EMS calls when exceeding the specific levels (humidex>43.88; heat index>89.91). Conclusion: Extreme combinations of temperature and humidity significantly increase the risk of MDs-related EMS calls, emphasizing the importance of integrating composite heat indices into public health strategies.
