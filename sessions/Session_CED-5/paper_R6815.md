# From equality to equity: Dual socioeconomic inequalities in mobility-based exposure and exposure avoidance opportunities to urban flooding and their interaction effects

**Author Information**  
Entong Ke<sup>1</sup>, Yan Zhang<sup>2</sup>, Tian Lan<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Wuhan University  

<sup>2</sup> The Chinese University of Hong Kong  



## Abstract

Understanding inequalities in urban pluvial flood exposure is critical for effective resilience planning and disaster management. Existing environmental justice studies largely adopt an equality-based perspective, focusing on whether static, residence-based flood exposure is evenly distributed across social groups. However, this approach overlooks key dimensions of inequality, as residents differ not only in their mobility-based exposure during daily activities, but also in their opportunities and capacities to avoid exposure. Consequently, structural disadvantages faced by vulnerable populations and their cumulative effects remain insufficiently understood. Using the area within Beijing’s Fifth Ring Road as a case study, we propose an integrated framework that jointly assesses mobility-based flood exposure (MFE) and exposure avoidance constraint (EAC). By combining AlphaEarth Foundation Embeddings with mobile phone signaling data, we quantify flood exposure and avoidance opportunities for 614,510 residents. EAC is measured by constructing Pareto frontiers through Monte Carlo Path Simulation, while multinomial logit models with interaction terms are used to examine the relationships between socioeconomic status (SES) and the dual inequalities. The results show that: (1) residence-based assessment suffers from a pronounced Neighborhood Effect Averaging Problem (NEAP), leading to systematic bias relative to mobility-based measures; (2) low-SES groups experience dual disadvantages, characterized by higher MFE and stronger constraint in identifying low-susceptibility travel paths; and (3) the interaction between high exposure and high avoidance constraint produces a significant Matthew effect among low-SES populations, further amplifying their structural disadvantages. We argue for a shift from an equality-oriented to an equity-oriented perspective that integrates exposure and exposure avoidance opportunities, providing actionable insights for disaster risk reduction and the achievement of SDGs 3, 10, and 11.
