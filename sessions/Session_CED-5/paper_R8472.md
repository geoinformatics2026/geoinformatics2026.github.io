# Modeling and Predicting the Effects of Pluvial Flooding on the Metro Network in the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area

**Author Information**  
Yuyue Yang<sup>1</sup>, Jie Yin<sup>1</sup>, Jianfeng Mai<sup>1</sup>, Dan Gao<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Key Laboratory of Geographic Information Science (Ministry of Education), East China  Normal University  



## Abstract

Urban pluvial flooding, intensified by climate change and rapid urbanization, poses an increasing threat to critical infrastructure, particularly underground metro systems in densely populated megacity clusters like the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area (GBA). This study provides a systematic evaluation of pluvial flood hazards and their cascading impacts on the GBA metro network under both current climate conditions and future RCP8.5 scenarios (2058–2099). A 2D hydraulic model (FloodMap-HydroInundation2D) is employed to simulate flood inundation dynamics across major GBA cities for rainfall events with 10-, 50-, and 100-year return periods. A station-specific flood risk framework is integrated with graph-theoretical analysis to evaluate the vulnerability of individual stations and the topological robustness of the overall network. Results reveal that Shenzhen and Hong Kong were the most exposed cities, with average inundation depths reaching 0.13m and 0.21m, respectively, under a 100-year rainfall event. Critical vulnerabilities were identified in the Guangzhou-Foshan Line 6 and Hong Kong’s Tsuen Wan Line, where high-risk stations are predominantly clustered in central urban districts. Furthermore, network performance analysis indicates that under future climate forcing, the global efficiency of the Shenzhen and Hong Kong metro systems could deteriorate to levels comparable to those of current centennial floods as early as a projected 10-year return period. This study also extends to 116 under-construction stations, identifying emerging risk hotspots in the expanding network. These findings highlight the critical interdependence between urban hydrology and transit reliability in megacity clusters. Beyond contributing to climate adaptation strategies for the GBA, the proposed assessment framework offers a transferable approach to support climate-resilient infrastructure planning in other coastal urban regions worldwide.
