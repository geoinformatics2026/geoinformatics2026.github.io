# Based on a Cloud‑Guided Dual‑Stream MambaVision Framework for Flood Detection: A Case Study of the Miyun Reservoir Area

**Author Information**  
Yao Qixin<sup>1</sup>, Meng Qingxiang<sup>1</sup>, Gong Yuanfu<sup>2</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Wuhan University  

<sup>2</sup> Hubei Institute of Land Surveying and Mapping  



## Abstract

Regional economic development and the public demand for disaster prevention and mitigation impose higher requirements for real-time and accurate flood monitoring. However, one of the most prominent challenges in current flood remote sensing remains the low usability of optical imagery under persistent cloud cover and the limited identification capability of single Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data in complex terrestrial environments. This study takes the Miyun Reservoir area as a case study and innovatively employs multi- source remote sensing data, including SAR imagery and multispectral images. Methods such as dual-stream Mamba Vision encoding, cloud-guided adaptive fusion, and channel-spatial collaborative attention decoding are applied to extract and analyze the multi-modal remote sensing characteristics of the area during flood events. Based on the constructed cloud-guided dual-stream fusion framework, we analyze the contribution of each modality in cloud-covered and cloud-free areas, and conduct a comparative study on the detection performance of the CSCA module. Through a combination of qualitative and quantitative analysis, this study systematically elucidates the multi-modal remote sensing response characteristics of the Miyun Reservoir area during flooding. It provides methodological reference and case support for reliable water body monitoring and flood analysis under complex meteorological conditions.
