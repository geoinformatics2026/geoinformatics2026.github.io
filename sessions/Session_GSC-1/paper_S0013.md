# A factorized spatio-temporal approach for short-term travel origin-destination forecasting

**Author Information**  
Sicong Lai<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (Guangzhou)  



## Abstract

Short-term travel origin-destination (OD) forecasting is important for understanding how travel demand evolves across cities and for supporting urban planning and transportation development. However, this task remains challenging because many OD pairs are weakly connected and sparsely observed, while the full OD interaction matrix grows rapidly with the size of the study area. To address these issues, we develop a factorized spatio-temporal framework, Origin-Destination Neural Factorization Machines (ODNFM), for short-term urban OD forecasting. The proposed approach integrates recent and periodic temporal sequences with decomposed origin and destination embeddings, enabling pairwise mobility relations to be learned in a compact latent space while preserving spatial heterogeneity. We evaluate the framework on two public taxi mobility datasets and compare it with conventional time-series methods as well as graph- and attention-based forecasting models. Results show that ODNFM consistently improved forecasting accuracy over baselines, with up to roughly 14% lower RMSE, while also offering a computationally practical solution for larger OD systems. Case-based analysis further suggests that the model better recovers localized movement structures in both high-activity and weakly connected urban areas.



Keywords: Origin-destination forecasting, urban mobility, spatial interaction, travel behavior
