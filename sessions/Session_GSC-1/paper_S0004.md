# Analysis of the Driving Mechanism of Interprovincial Population Migration in China Based on the Spatiotemporal Durbin Panel Model

**Author Information**  
Yuqi Chen<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Nanjing University  



## Abstract

Since the launch of reform and opening-up, interprovincial population migration in China has generally expanded in scale. Population migration is driven by multiple socioeconomic factors and exhibits spatiotemporal path dependence, yet existing studies often focus on either temporal or spatial dimensions and overlook their interaction, while also neglecting model uncertainty. Based on China’s interprovincial migration data from 1985 to 2020, this study develops a spatiotemporal Durbin panel model to capture path dependence, integrates BMA with Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods to address model uncertainty, and employs a spatiotemporal effects framework to quantify origin, destination, and network effects across synchronous, short-term, and long-term periods. Findings reveal: (1) Economic agglomeration and labor supply–demand mismatch remain the core drivers of interprovincial migration in China. The joint use of BMA and a spatiotemporal Durbin model mitigates the overestimation bias of spillover effects in single-model approaches. (2) Migration behavior is not only directly driven by socio-economic factors, but also forms significant structural amplification and inhibition effects through spatial networks. Network spillover effects generally exceeds origin and destination effects. Different factors show distinct spatial spillover patterns, with labor reflecting competitive effects and wages generating positive diffusion across regions. (3) Migration responses display clear spatiotemporal path dependence and nonlinear temporal dynamics, characterized by strongest contemporaneous effects, followed by short-term decay and long-term stabilization. In the long-term, network effects remain dominant, with labor and GDP reinforcing spatial competition and wage promoting spatial diffusion. Migration patterns increasingly reflect structurally embedded spatial interactions rather than purely short-term differentials. By quantifying multidimensional spatiotemporal effects, this study comprehensively analyzes the role of regional factors within interprovincial migration networks and their spillover effects, providing scientific foundations for building a population-balanced society.
