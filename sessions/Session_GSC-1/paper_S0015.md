# Beyond Static Boundaries: Tracking the Structural Reorganization of U.S. Labor Flow Networks

**Author Information**  
Guangjun Zeng<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> National University of Singapore  



## Abstract

Labor markets function as relational structures driven by worker mobility, organized simultaneously along dimensions of industrial specialization and geographic proximity. Existing research has paid limited attention to the temporal dynamics of geo-industrial boundaries, leaving their long-term persistence, fragmentation, and transformation insufficiently understood. To address this, we introduce the Temporal Geo-Industrial Reorganization framework to systematically track emergent market boundaries over time. Applying this framework to networks built from 300 million U.S. employment histories spanning 2000 to 2024, we translate micro level career transitions into macro level structural transformations. Our analysis reveals that the macroscopic U.S. labor network has evolved into a larger, sparser, and more locally dense system. Internally, these communities reorganize through four distinct evolutionary states: diffuse circulation, strong continuity, controlled expansion, and fragmentation. Geographic continuity tends to be stronger than industrial continuity, acting as a more persistent anchor for labor markets despite ongoing internal topological turnover. This stability exhibits distinct sector-specific heterogeneity, emerging knowledge economies maintain fluid geographic boundaries, while traditional and local service sectors rely on strict spatial proximity. This study bridges network science with evolutionary economic geography by demonstrating that functional labor markets are emergent systems balancing macro-level stability with micro-level fluidity, offering new perspectives for tailored regional economic policies.
