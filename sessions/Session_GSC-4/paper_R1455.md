# Toward a Responsibility-Centred Epistemology of GeoAI: Twin Expressions of AI Ethics and Geographic Information Science

**Author Information**  
Chuan Chen<sup>1</sup>


<sup>1</sup> Chair of Cartography and Visual Analytics, Technical University of Munich  



## Abstract

The rapid convergence of artificial intelligence and geographic information science marks a structural transformation in how spatial knowledge is produced, interpreted, and enacted. As GeoAI systems increasingly shape urban governance, resource allocation, and social management, ethical questions can no longer be treated as external regulatory add-ons. Instead, they must be understood as epistemic conditions embedded within spatial modelling itself. This study advances a responsibility-centred theoretical framework that conceptualizes AI ethics and geographic information science as “twin expressions” of a shared knowledge paradigm. First, we argue for the spatialization of AI ethics. By structuring AI incidents through ontological modeling and relational knowledge representation, ethical events are reframed not as isolated controversies but as interconnected nodes within a semantic and socio-technical network. Complementary narrative mapping of large-scale public discourse reveals that ethical concerns evolve across space and time, exposing asymmetries in global attention, power, and vulnerability. Ethics thus becomes cartographically traceable and structurally analyzable. Second, we propose the ethicalization of spatial analysis. Rather than focusing solely on external harms such as privacy breaches or discrimination, we introduce the notion of endogenous bias arising from spatial heterogeneity, model assumptions, parameterization, and interpretative grouping. This perspective shifts attention from outcome evaluation to the epistemic architecture of spatial reasoning itself. Finally, we reposition visualization as an ethical technology. Visual analytics is not merely communicative representation but an active mechanism for detecting, interrogating, and restructuring responsibility within GeoAI systems. By integrating ontology, explainability, and visual scrutiny, this work outlines a conceptual pathway toward an autonomous ethical framework for geographic information science in the age of spatial intelligence.



Keywords: GeoAI Ethics,  Visual Analytics,  Explainable AI,  Knowledge Graph
